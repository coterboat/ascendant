# R2, Claude (revised): Halfmade

Sketches read hp/attack/defense/morale/cost. **Bold** terms need codex entries.

## What changed and why

- **One Birthright per faction, in one sentence.** R1 broke §7's one-rule limit three times.
- **The Revel get three fixed masks and no carving.** Codex's Forecast Vote showed the approachable size.
- **The Candlewake's wick is per army, and never-rout is now a mandate you buy.** No per-unit bookkeeping and no free turtle.
- **The Stormherd's storm moved onto the Weatherwife (Washout).** Grazing is the whole Birthright.
- **The Crown and Clans take the repo's M4 forks**, and every Becoming **forswears** one mandate. Grok caught the mismatch and brought the pattern.
- **Free Holds added** under the repo's names (Codex and Grok both pitched them).
- **Noonmarks pay +2 research** instead of dripping ruler power (Grok's sag-yards), so M2 holds.
- **Province abilities spread out.** Mine leaned on gold and healing, like Codex's, so three rulers get new ones.
- **Fixes:** Harrow now works on the rout line (Grok's habit), Toll becomes Gleaning, Weaving becomes Painting, and the Revel drop black velvet so they stop reading like the Candlewake.

## 1. The premise: the Wright stopped at noon

The world is **Halfmade** (the world as its maker left it, unfinished). Its maker, **the Wright** (the absent craftswoman who built the world by hand), set her tools down mid-stroke at noon on the last day and walked out for good.

The sun has sunk ever since without reaching the horizon: **the Long Afternoon** (the endless golden hour since the Wright left). Some peoples were made whole, some were never given faces, and death itself was only half built.

**What ascension literally is:** the Wright's unspent making still lies in the world. Gather enough of it where her hands stopped, pick up her tools, and you finish the world in your own image: endless noon, a hunting night, a rain that washes everything clean. Six factions want six endings, and only one gets written. On the 3D map the sky tints toward the leader's ending (noon gold, dusk, rain grey), never toward a player colour.

## 2. The source of power: Quick

**Quick** (the Wright's unspent making, the one power behind every core system) is warm, gold and alive, as in "the quick and the dead":

- **Quick held makes a ruler.** A ruler is whoever has gathered Quick into one body, and Quick held can't also be poured. One body holds only so much, hence the pool of 10.
- **Quick poured is a mandate.** Poured into a people, Quick sets as a law of their nature. Iron Discipline isn't training: a Crown soldier can't run while his captain stands. Mandates outlive the ruler because poured Quick lives in the people. A hurried pour spills, and an established self resists division (Codex's line), so a mid-game sacrifice costs +1.
- **Death spills Quick according to the ruler's kind.**
  - A mortal body cracks. Half spills and the heir catches the rest (half power, 2 turns).
  - The undying bound their Quick into an object, like the sunstone. Nothing spills, but re-forming around it is slow (full power, 4 turns).
  - A god *is* Quick. Each death tears a piece away for good (−3), but the god is back in a turn.
- **Pour enough and the making resumes.** The people get finished, and a thing can only be finished one way. That is a **Becoming** (a transformation fork, locked once taken). Each Becoming **forswears** (closes for good) one mandate, because finished people can't take that law.
- **A Birthright** (one free, innate rule per faction, in one sentence) **is how the Wright left you.** Most asymmetry lives there.

**Free Holds** (the independent provinces, under the repo's names) are peoples who took no ending: they like the afternoon. Sworn Vassals is a hold agreeing to your ending.

## 3. The six factions

### 3.1 The Aurel Crown: Keepers of Noon
*They want the sun nailed back at noon: a lit, lawful world forever.*

**Fantasy:** ordinary people made extraordinary because their king pours everything into them.

**Birthright:** **Charter** (written Quick pours cleanly) means the Crown's mid-game sacrifices cost no +1.

**Your turn:** waiting is free, so the Crown holds power back and pours exactly what the war needs once it sees the enemy's build. Does Corvane give up 3 for Royal Armories now, or stay strong enough to survive at Stonegate?

**Rulers and roster:** unchanged (packet §4–5). The sunstone is a chip of the noon sun.

**Becomings** (repo M4, per Grok):
- **Celestial Host:** they take the noon into themselves. White-gold armour with a ring of light behind the head, Spearmen become Sunspears, and your own provinces heal your wounded. Forswears Heavy Tithes.
- **Living Iron:** the oath is poured into metal. Iron Knights and an Iron Colossus join, hills pay more, and forts heal. Forswears King's Roads.

**Look (3D):** tidy rectangles and forests of spears: a grid of vertical lines under square blue banners. Polished steel, white linen, blue enamel, gilt. Banner unchanged.

**Fortress and muster:** white limestone walls, blue slate cone roofs, a gilt sun-disc over each gate. Crownkeep's glass lantern-tower holds the sunstone: the first beacon on the map. Blue squares drill in a sand yard, then march out in step when the great gate drops.

**Naming:** short golden court names (Corvane, Edrin). Places end in -keep, -march or -watch. Epithets are virtues (the Steady).

### 3.2 The Thornmane Clans: the Night Hunt
*They want the sun to set at last, so the long hunting night can begin.*

**Fantasy:** proud beast tribes who love the world unfinished, unfenced and alive.

**Birthright:** **Wild Ground** (the woods and hills are always home): in forest and hills, the Clans get the terrain bonus whether they attack or defend.

**Your turn:** wounds are fuel and fear is a weapon, and Wild Ground makes attacking into the woods their best move, the reverse of everyone else's. Strike while bloodied and frenzied, or heal with Feast of Victory and lose the edge?

**Rulers and roster:** unchanged (packet §4–5).

**Becomings** (repo M4):
- **Thorn Wild:** they grow into their god's body: thorned, rooted, slow in the open. Forest spreads into one owned province a turn and pays more. Forswears Blood Frenzy.
- **Blood Pact:** they spill their own Quick to call the night early. Fur goes black and eyes red, recruits are cheap but arrive wounded, and invaders on their land lose morale. Forswears Feast of Victory.

**Look (3D):** hunched, horned clumps that never hold a line, with bears looming. Crimson paint pops on green. Tawny fur, bone, moss, briar, hide. Banner unchanged.

**Fortress and muster:** a **thorn-ring** (a wall of giant living briars) around a hill crowned by a colossal horned skull. Warbands gather among totem poles and chained bears, and pour out running when the briars part.

**Naming:** English kennings and deed-names (Splits-the-Oak, the Ninth Fang). Places come from body and land (Fangridge, Bloodfen). Hard g, k and th.

### 3.3 The Candlewake: the dead who are still waiting
*They want death finished, so there is finally somewhere to rest.*

**Fantasy:** death was only half built, so the dead stay, kept lit in bodies of candle wax. They march, polite and a little sad, to finish the one room the world is missing. "We are not angry. We are late."

**Birthright:** **Wick** (the turns a Candlewake army can stay lit, shown on its banner): every army starts at 6 and burns 1 each turn it neither fights nor rests in your fort, any battle relights it to 6, and at 0 it gutters out.

**Faction mandate:** **Unbreaking** (4 power; the dead forget how to run): your side never routs.

**Your turn:** every field army is a clock. "The Second Procession has 2 turns left. Fight, or go home." Enemies kite the dead until they gutter, so simultaneous turns become a chase. Before the game: buy Unbreaking, or keep Half-Death huge and let the dead break?

**Rulers:**
- **Half-Death** (god, front rank): the half-made god of death, half its face carved alabaster, half only a glowing outline. **Gleaning:** after a battle it wins, 1/2/3/4 fallen enemies rise as Wickwardens in your army.
- **The Chandler** (mortal line, second rank): the living candlemaker who keeps the dead. **Vigil:** your armies in her province relight to 7/8/9/10 wick.

**Roster:**

| unit | sketch | trait |
|---|---|---|
| Wickwardens (infantry) | 7/9/8/8/6 | **Pall**: enemies attacking it have −10% hit chance. |
| Keeners (infantry) | 6/11/4/7/7 | **Harrow**: the damage it deals counts double toward the enemy's rout. |
| Great Taper (monster) | 20/13/5/9/18 | **Flare**: when it falls, every standing enemy fighter takes 2 damage. |
| Lamplighter (commander) | 22/11/9/12/30 | **Rekindle**: after a battle its side wins, half its fallen units (rounded down) stand again at half hp. |

**Becomings:**
- **Golden Wake:** beeswax and hives. Wick runs to 10, owned provinces with no army pay +1 gold, and the wax turns honey-gold with the lilac kept. Forswears Unbreaking.
- **Black Wick:** the flames go out and they become smoke. No wick, and enemies have −10% hit chance against all your fighters, but you recruit only where you won a battle this turn. Forswears Muster Grounds.

**Look (3D):** a field of tiny flames: every figure wears one on its crown, so a block reads as glowing dots. Dripping ivory tallow, soot-black wicks and armour, brass snuffers, lilac ribbons. Banner: lilac cloth, soot border, drip-cut hem, an ivory candle ringed by small flames.

**Fortress and muster:** a **wakehouse** (a Candlewake fort) is a stepped tower of candles melted over itself for centuries. The capital, **the Long Vigil**, is a hill of old wax around one candle as tall as a keep. Processions file out each carrying its flame: glowing dotted lines across the map.

**Naming:** soft English mourning words and epitaphs (Mother Tallow, Kept-Till-Morning, Small Ember). Places end in -wake or -vigil. Units are named for household jobs.

### 3.4 The Stormherd: shepherds of weather
*They want **the Washing**: one great rain to scrub the world clean and start the morning over.*

**Fantasy:** gruff, funny nomads in yellow oilskins who herd living weather across the high fells: storm-cloud sheep, thunder rams, a grandfather thunderhead.

**Birthright:** **Grazing** (the herd lives off the land under it): each army earns the income of the province it stands in, owned or not, and owned land with no army pays half.

**Your turn:** where do the herds graze? Every army is wallet and sword at once, and its forecast shows income beside battle odds. Spreading out earns more but risks getting caught alone, and Noonmarks must be held while the herd wants to move. Where the Weatherwife walks, forts stop mattering, so turtles must guess her road.

**Rulers:**
- **Grandsire Thunder** (god, front rank): a living thunderhead shaped like an old ram. **Cloudburst:** every enemy-owned province next to it earns 1/2/3/4 less gold.
- **The Bellmother** (mortal line, front rank): the chief shepherd with the great brass bell, her heirs numbered in the old sheep-count (Yan, Tan, Tethera). **Penning:** your units defending her province have +1/2/3/4 defense.

**Roster:**

| unit | sketch | trait |
|---|---|---|
| Crooks (infantry) | 9/9/5/8/7 | **Gathering**: +1 attack each round after the first, up to +3. |
| Thundersheep (beast) | 12/11/4/7/10 | **Shock**: treats its target's defense as 4 lower. |
| Thunder Ram (monster) | 22/13/8/9/21 | **Thunderclap**: the first enemy it hits in a battle loses its next attack. |
| Weatherwife (commander) | 24/11/9/11/32 | **Washout**: while it stands, no side in the battle gets terrain or fort bonuses. |

**Becomings:**
- **Thunderborn:** the herds go up. Thundersheep become **Stormlings** with **Strike Down** (can attack the enemy's second rank and its commander), and armies move two provinces a turn anywhere, but regular units have −2 hp. Forswears Veteran Levies.
- **Floodborn:** the herds go down into the rivers. Armies that stay put heal 3 hp a turn, and an enemy fort where a Floodborn army ends 2 turns running is washed away: the siege path. Forswears Sworn Vassals.

**Look (3D):** rolling cloud-blobs dotted with yellow oilskins, rams like small thunderheads, cyan lightning that reads at any distance. Slate-grey cumulus wool, yellow oilskin, brass bells, drystone. Banner: oilskin yellow, slate border, a storm-cloud ram's head with a cyan lightning fork.

**Fortress and muster:** a **fold** (a Stormherd fort) is concentric drystone sheepfold rings on a high fell, roofed by one huge storm cloud on chains, flickering like a beacon. The flock waits penned in the rings, then floods downhill like weather breaking.

**Naming:** hill-farm words (tup, hogg, gimmer, fell, gill, scar) plus weather: Gimmer Rattlecloud, Old Tup Hailstone.

### 3.5 The Kilnborn: the people of fired clay
*They want **the Last Firing**: bake the world hard so it never cracks or changes again.*

**Fantasy:** the Wright set them on the sill to dry and left before the firing, so they fire themselves, and each firing is a coming of age. Patient, ceremonial, house-proud, glazed in gorgeous colours.

**Birthright:** **Kilnwork** (building your own forts): you can order a **kiln** (a fort you can recruit at) in any owned province, for gold, ready in 2 turns.

**Your turn:** where does the next kiln go? Each kiln is a forward base, so they advance like a siege line, and a half-built kiln is a target for two turns. Their units are hard and brittle (high defense, low hp): they love forts and fear Shock and Washout.

**Rulers:**
- **The Kiln-Mothers** (undying, second rank): three elder potters travelling as one, who re-fire at any kiln you choose. **Hot Kiln:** a kiln ordered in their province is ready this turn and costs 0/2/4/6 gold less.
- **The Unfired** (god, front rank): the Wright's last clay figure, a soft giant with her thumbprint on its brow. **Thumbprint:** units recruited in its province arrive with +1/2/3/4 maximum hp.

**Roster:**

| unit | sketch | trait |
|---|---|---|

| Firepots (infantry) | 6/11/8/7/9 | **Splash**: each hit also deals 1 damage to another enemy in the same rank. |
| Kiln Walker (monster) | 16/13/13/10/24 | **Firing**: at the end of each round, friendly units get +1 defense, up to +3. |
| Glazier (commander) | 20/11/13/12/32 | **Goldmend**: after each battle, every surviving unit on its side heals 4 hp. |

**Becomings:**
- **High Fire:** porcelain. +2 defense and −2 hp, and Sworn Vassals costs 2 less. Forswears Muster Grounds.
- **Slip:** they refuse the kiln and stay wet clay. Kilns go; they recruit in any owned province, and units heal 2 hp a turn but lose 3 defense. Forswears Iron Discipline.

**Look (3D):** round jar-bodies with loop handles and lids for helmets, in tight blocks under towering walking kilns. Terracotta, turquoise glaze, kiln-black, cream slip. Banner: terracotta, turquoise border, a cream bottle-kiln with a glowing orange mouth.

**Fortress and muster:** tall bottle kilns behind glazed-brick walls, smoke plumes visible across the map. The capital, **the Great Kiln**, is a stepped kiln-mountain whose mouth always glows. New soldiers dry in silent rows, then march out through the kiln mouth, still glowing.

**Naming:** potters' words (slip, grog, bisque, sagger, coil). A name is clay body plus glaze: Grog Turquoise, Bisque-the-Elder. Places: Kilnhollow, Saggerstead.

### 3.6 The Revel: the people with borrowed faces
*They want to finish the Wright's face, and wear it.*

**Fantasy:** the Wright never carved their faces, so they make masks and become whatever the mask is. A festival-nation that never ends: generous hosts, terrifying guests. Visitors who stay too long lose their faces.

**Birthright:** **Masks** (hidden traits worn by army): each turn you secretly give each army **Brass** (Shield Wall), **Grin** (Berserk) or **Hare** (Skirmish), and its **Masked** units have that trait until the turn ends.

**Your turn:** a pure read: the opponent sees your army, not its mask. If the Crown expects Shield Wall and brings Knights, wear the Grin. Both players' forecasts show three odds lines, one per mask: only the choice is hidden, never the rules.

**Rulers:**
- **The Host** (mortal line, front rank): the mask *is* the ruler, and whoever picks it up is the next Host. **Overstay:** enemy armies in the Host's province fight at −1/1/2/3 morale.
- **The Wright's Face** (god, second rank): the face the Wright was carving for herself, unfinished and floating. **Borrowed Glory:** your Masked units have +1/1/2/2 attack in its province's battles.

**Roster:**

| unit | sketch | trait |
|---|---|---|
| Revellers (infantry) | 8/9/5/7/7 | **Masked** |
| Stiltwalkers (heavy infantry) | 12/11/8/8/12 | **Masked** |
| Great Puppet (monster) | 26/9/9/12/20 | **Spectacle**: every enemy attack that can reach it targets it. |
| Prompter (commander) | 22/11/9/12/30 | **Second Act**: from round 5, its side's Masked units wear the Grin. |

**Becomings:**
- **True Face:** they choose one mask and grow it as a real face. Masked units keep that trait for good, with +3 hp. Forswears Drilled Ranks.
- **The Endless Revel:** the masks become the people. They recruit Free Hold units in any owned province, and those arrive Masked. Forswears Sworn Vassals.

**Look (3D):** tall and spindly: stilts, ribbons, the Puppet's head bobbing above the crowd. Rose-pink and lime harlequin diamonds carry the mid-tones, white lacquer masks the light, black gloves and eye-holes the dark. Banner: rose-and-lime diamonds, black border, a white half-smiling mask.

**Fortress and muster:** a **pavilion** (a Revel fort) is striped silk over scaffolding behind painted castle fronts: a fortress from the front, a stage set from behind. The capital, **the Grand Stage**, is an amphitheatre under a giant hanging mask. The army costumes in public at racks of masks, then parades out behind the Puppet.

**Naming:** roles, not names (the Prompter, Second Lady, the Weeping Uncle). A Reveller is called by today's mask. Sing-song doubled sounds (Tumtum, Lalla). Places: Ribbon Fair.

## 4. Rivalries, and why simultaneous turns make drama

Three old wars, each a clash of endings:
- **Noon against Night** (Crown and Clans): order wants the sun nailed up, and the wild wants it down.
- **Fire against Rain** (Kilnborn and Stormherd): kilns are forts, Washout ignores forts, and Floodborn washes them away. The builder hunts a nomad who won't stand still.
- **The stolen faces** (Revel and Candlewake): the Revel's finest masks are pressed from the wax faces of the dead, who want them back.

Every faction bets blind: the mask, the graze, the half-built kiln, the wick's clock, the bloodied charge, the held-back power. The report is the reveal: "You both marched on the Halfway Mountain. The Revel wore Brass." It was a read, not a dice roll.

## 5. Noonmarks: the ascension sites

A **Noonmark** (an ascension site, where the Wright's hand rested when she stopped) is literally unfinished: the land runs out into glowing outline and flat raw colour, as if the paint stopped. Each pays +2 research a turn while held (Grok's sag-yards), so they matter before victory. Seeds:
- **The Halfway Mountain:** a peak that ends in mid-air as glowing lines. Its holder sees far (fog, M5).
- **The Pouring:** a waterfall out of a hole in the sky. Your units there heal 3 hp a turn.
- **The Sun-Nail:** a golden nail the size of a tower, meant to pin the sun. +1 morale in its battles.
- **The Blank:** a white field where nothing is decided. Battles here never have a defender.
- **The Cold Kiln:** the Wright's own kiln, never lit. A fort its holder can recruit at.

## 6. Magic seeds: the six crafts

Magic is doing a little of the Wright's work yourself. Her six **crafts** (the magic schools) come in opposed pairs: **Kindling** (fire, light, sight through fog) against **Painting** (illusion, false armies), **Shaping** (clay, stone, iron) against **Weathering** (wind, rain, eroding forts), and **Sowing** (growth, beasts, healing) against **Paring** (the knife: blood, curses, the dead). The factions lead in §3's order: Kindling, Sowing, Paring, Weathering, Shaping, Painting. Per Grok and repo M5, each Becoming opens its craft's **greater working** (that craft's strongest rituals): Sun is Kindling's, Law is Shaping's, the Wild is Sowing's and Blood is Paring's.

## 7. Tone, and red-team flags

Dark but not grim: the dead are kind, the face-thieves throw a party, and the beast-god loves its cubs. Flags:
- **The absent maker** echoes Dominions' vanished Pantokrator. Nobody here wants worship.
- **The Thornmane** (built) sit near Warhammer's Beastmen. Keep boar, wolf and lion heads, never goat, and no herdstones.
- **Masks** sit near Majora's Mask. The room should give masks to one faction, so Codex's Interval Court should drop them.
- **Half-Death** echoes Hel, so it must avoid Marvel's Hela: no antlers, no green and black.
- **Storm sheep** stay slate-grey and black-faced, far from Pokémon's Mareep.
- **Overlaps across the room** (glaze, storm nations, the gentle dead) need one owner each. Mine each own a turn decision nobody else has: the kiln, the graze, the wick.
