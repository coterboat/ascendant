# R1, Codex: The Oathscar

## 1. The world premise, and what ascension literally is

**The Oathscar** — the visible wound left when the world’s first rulers divided the power that once joined land, people, and sovereign.

The world was not created by gods. It grew around promises. A shelter became a village when its inhabitants agreed to defend it; a road became safe when generations honored the same boundary stones; a crown became more than metal when thousands accepted its judgments. Repeated commitments impressed patterns upon reality until promises acquired physical force.

That force is called **Accord** — power produced when living beings knowingly bind themselves to a shared rule.

A ruler gathers Accord because the ruler is the point through which a people’s promises meet. Ruler power is therefore one budget with two uses. Power kept close strengthens the ruler’s body and magic. Power released through a mandate settles into the ruler’s people, institutions, roads, weapons, or customs.

A **mandate** is a supernatural public promise made enforceable by a ruler’s surrendered power.

Mandates outlive rulers because their power no longer belongs to the person who issued them. It has entered habits, bloodlines, tools, songs, and the land itself. Royal Armories persists because every forge has learned the same impossible standard. Iron Discipline persists because soldiers inherit an oath that their predecessors made real.

A ruler may surrender more power during a crisis, but a mid-game sacrifice costs extra because an established self resists division. At the beginning of a reign, identity is still negotiable. Later, tearing away power also tears away memories, health, or possible descendants. The added cost represents that wound.

Death follows the ruler’s nature. Mortal lines pass surviving Accord through recognition, inheritance, and ceremony, losing much in the transfer. Undying rulers rebuild around an external anchor. Gods return quickly because worship still gives them a shape, but each destroyed incarnation exhausts part of the idea their followers can imagine. Other factions may use stranger successions, but every method visibly accounts for where the ruler’s remaining Accord goes.

Ascension is literal control of the world’s deepest promises. At several places, reality still remembers its earliest form. A ruler who holds enough of these sites can write one final mandate into the world: becoming a god, making an empire permanent, liberating all promises from rulers, or transforming life into something new.

Transformations lock in because bodies and societies cannot serve two final definitions at once. When a people declares “we are iron that remembers” or “we live only through shared dreams,” Accord edits every future birth, craft, and custom toward that truth. Reversing it would require the same world-writing power the factions are fighting to obtain.

The neutral garrisons are **Hearth Leagues** — independent communities whose local defense oaths recognize no distant ruler. Plains muster Spear Companies, hills raise Stoneguards, and forests shelter Wildkin bands. Richer leagues field larger forces under Hearth Captains. Conquest means breaking or superseding their local promise; Sworn Vassals makes a ruler’s wider promise convincing enough that one neighboring league joins without battle.

## 2. The sources of power

Accord has three expressions, all derived from commitment rather than interchangeable magical fuel.

**Crownfire** — Accord concentrated in a recognized ruler, commander, office, or sacred object. It powers mandates, succession, and direct feats.

**Deep Memory** — old Accord embedded in terrain, ruins, roads, graves, and ascension sites. Rituals wake it by reenacting the promise that formed it.

**Living Change** — Accord carried by bodies adapting to a shared identity. It powers faction transformations and explains why transformed units retain recognizable roles while their materials and anatomy change.

Gold still pays recruits, builders, and provisions. Research represents learning how to address these powers safely. Sacrifice, song, law, craft, and worship are methods of shaping Accord, not separate cosmic energies.

## 3. Six factions

### The Aurel Crown

**Aurel Crown** — a human kingdom trying to prove that fair institutions can become stronger than any monarch.

Its fantasy is disciplined public service under a dangerous crown. It favors reliable formations, income, healing, and internal movement. A Crown turn asks whether to concentrate for a forecasted set battle or use King’s Roads and several commanders to threaten two provinces. Wounded veterans matter because hp persists, making the Ever-Regent’s healing and defensive terrain valuable.

Its rulers remain King Corvane, a mortal-line front-rank ruler whose Royal Court raises province income, and the Ever-Regent, an undying second-rank caster whose Mending Light heals wounded units in his province.

Roster seed:

- Spearmen: **Shield Wall** — gains +2 defense while another allied fighter stands.
- Crossbowmen: **Volley** — attacks from a protected second rank while the front rank is large enough.
- Knights: **Charge** — deals +4 damage with hits in round one, reduced to +3 in forests.
- Knight Captain: **Inspire** — grants its side +2 additional morale while standing.

**Common Radiance** — an ascension in which Crownfire is divided among citizens. Knights become bright oath-bearers, towns earn more gold, and ruler abilities weaken; white-gold rings and luminous cloth replace heavy heraldry.

**Iron Testament** — an ascension in which law enters flesh as living metal. Units gain hp and defense but cost more, healing becomes slower, and province resistance rises; dark riveted bodies retain blue trim and familiar weapons.

At distance, the army is a grid of upright spears, rectangular shields, and broad cavalry wedges. Its materials remain steel, white linen, royal blue, and gold. The blue banner bears the gold crown above a rising white sun. Crownkeep is a concentric pale-stone fortress with blue roofs and a central sunstone tower. Its muster ground is a marked square outside the inner gate, where companies form in visible ranks.

Aurel names favor clear two- or three-syllable forms, hard openings, and civic compounds: Corvane, Aurel, Merrow, Crownkeep, Westwatch, Sunward, White Lance.

### The Thornmane Clans

**Thornmane Clans** — beastfolk kin-bands who believe pain honestly shared is stronger than safety imposed from above.

Their fantasy is momentum, wounds, intimidation, and rapid mustering. Berserk and Blood Frenzy turn damaged fighters into threats, while Feast of Victory rewards committing to battles that can be won outright. A Clans turn asks whether to attack before wounds become fatal, split recruitment through the Ninth Fang, or send the Great Thornmane into a battle where Dread can force an early rout.

Their rulers are the Great Thornmane, a front-rank god whose Dread lowers enemy morale where it fights, and the Ninth Fang, a mortal-line front-rank ruler whose Warband Muster recruits units in its current province.

Roster seed:

- Tuskers: **Berserk** — gains +3 attack while below full hp.
- Fangthrowers: **Skirmish** — can attack protected second-rank units.
- War Bear: **Fury** — lowers every enemy side’s morale by 2 while standing.
- Warchief: **Pack Leader** — gives every allied fighter +1 attack while standing.

**Briar Communion** — the clans become interlinked bodies of fur, thorn, and shared sensation. Wounds spread more evenly, forest defense improves, and forest provinces yield extra recruits rather than extra gold; crimson paint remains visible through green growth.

**Red Feast** — the clans become lean predators sustained by victory. Units gain attack and morale after winning but cost more in peaceful turns, while conquered provinces give immediate gold and reduced later income; bone plates darken and crimson markings become luminous.

From above they resemble an uneven tide of broad shoulders, lowered horns, javelin fans, and one huge beast at each center. Tawny fur, bone, moss green, and crimson remain their materials. Their ochre hide banner bears a bone-white horned skull encircled by green thorns. Fangridge is a terraced earth-and-stone ring around a living thorn mound. Warbands muster in clan circles around separate fires, then pour together through six low gates.

Names use claws of sound—short roots, doubled consonants, and titles earned through deeds: Rakka, Varn, Mott, Fangridge, Thornvale, Red-Muzzle, Root-Roarer.

### The Glasswake Compact

**Glasswake Compact** — amphibious artisans who grow translucent armor from memories deliberately surrendered.

The Compact feels precise, fragile, and replaceable until its plans interlock. Its units trade hp for high defense and ranged pressure. Its faction rule is **Recut** — once per turn, one wounded regular unit in a fortified province may be disbanded to reduce the gold cost of its replacement. A Compact turn asks whether to preserve an experienced formation, recycle casualties, or seize hills where defense and accurate fire compound.

**Speaker Pellucid** is a mortal-line second-rank ruler whose Clear Account increases the gold earned by one modest province. **The Returning Shoal** is an undying front-rank collective whose Stillwater restores a small amount of hp to Compact units that did not move that turn.

Roster seed:

- Shardwardens: **Deflection** — gains +2 defense against attacks from enemies with lower attack.
- Prism Casters: **Refraction** — gains +2 attack while protected in the second rank.
- Tide Striders: **Flowstep** — gains +2 defense in border battles and meetings with no defender.
- Ledger Bearer: **Exact Order** — allied fighters use 64% instead of 60% base hit chance while it stands.

**Perfect Vessel** — memories are removed until each body becomes flawless glass. Units gain defense and cost less, but morale falls and rulers generate less province income; pale cyan bodies contain sharp black cores.

**Many-Tide Form** — individuals dissolve into mobile colonies held in flexible shells. Units gain hp and healing, recruitment becomes slower, and wetlands or coastal provinces become more valuable; silhouettes broaden into low, rippling forms while weapons remain recognizable.

Compact blocks glitter as narrow black figures inside cyan fan-shaped shields, with ranged units forming bright diagonal lines. Materials are sea glass, black reed, pearl ceramic, and coral orange accents. Their charcoal banner carries an orange broken circle crossed by a cyan wave. Their fortresses are stepped glass basins nested inside black levees; the capital, **Pel-Lume**, surrounds a mirror-bright reservoir. Troops muster knee-deep in radial pools and rise in ordered files.

Names use liquid consonants, paired vowels, and craft suffixes: Pellucid, Loai, Meru, Pel-Lume, Vey Basin, Shardwarden, Ninth Recut.

### The Ember Orchard

**Ember Orchard** — walking fruiting bodies raised from funeral gardens, each carrying several dead citizens as warm seeds.

The Orchard is a slow, durable society that treats casualties as planting. Its units have high hp, modest defense, and effects triggered by falling. Its faction rule is **Aftercrop** — after winning a battle, each destroyed Orchard regular unit gives a small discount to one recruit in that province. A turn becomes a choice between reinforcing safe armies and accepting calculated losses to establish cheaper frontier musters.

**Grandmother Cinderpod** is an undying second-rank ruler whose Warm Soil improves healing in her province. **Rill of the First Garden** is a god in the front rank whose Ripening increases recruitment capacity where it stands but permanently loses power when destroyed.

Roster seed:

- Husk Bearers: **Packed Pith** — has +4 maximum hp while another Husk Bearer stands.
- Sparkspitters: **Cinder Seed** — its attack gains +2 against a target below full hp.
- Orchard Hulks: **Last Fruit** — when it falls, one allied regular unit heals 4 hp.
- Ash Gardener: **Tending** — every allied regular unit still standing heals 1 hp after a victorious battle.

**Ash Bloom** — the dead become light, fast clouds of ember-pollen. Units lose hp but gain attack, recruits become cheaper, and rich provinces lose income as their stored dead are consumed; orange crowns hover above black stems.

**Stone Fruit** — bodies ripen into heavy ceramic shells containing ancestral choirs. Units gain hp, defense, and morale but cost more and move poorly through rapid campaigns; cream shells split to reveal hot magenta interiors.

Their army reads as an orchard in motion: round crowns, forked limbs, and massive seed-shaped hulks. Charcoal bark, cream ceramic, hot magenta flesh, and ember orange distinguish them. Their cream banner bears a black seed split by an orange flame. Fortresses are circular kiln walls planted with towering ancestor trees. The capital, **Cindergrove**, has a glowing burial furnace at its heart. Muster grounds are furrowed ash fields where new bodies push upward in rows.

Names combine warm vowels with garden or fire images: Rill, Omoa, Cinderpod, Cindergrove, Warm Furrow, Husk Bearer, Ash Gardener.

### The Interval Court

**Interval Court** — masked beings who live in the pauses between events and regard punctuality as sacred mercy.

The Court’s fantasy is predicting simultaneous movement and punishing collisions. It fields few costly units with strong morale and positional traits. Its faction rule is **Appointment** — one declared army move each turn gains +1 attack in a resulting border battle or meeting, but no bonus if it arrives unopposed. The player must read rivals, choose the decisive collision, and risk wasting the benefit.

**The Minute Regent** is an undying back-rank ruler whose Held Hour delays recruitment payment in its province by one turn. **Vessa Between Bells** is a mortal-line front-rank ruler whose Timely Arrival gives an army beginning in her province improved battle forecasts against visible enemies.

Roster seed:

- Pauseguard: **Interpose** — gains +2 defense in round one.
- Bell Archers: **Due Stroke** — gains +3 attack in round two.
- Longstep Riders: **Convergence** — gains +2 attack in battles where every side arrived that turn.
- Measure Keeper: **Counted Breath** — allied fighters gain +1 morale during rounds one through four.

**Still Dominion** — the Court becomes nearly motionless porcelain figures that force battles to occur on their rhythm. Defense and morale rise, movement options shrink, and forts become more valuable; violet joints remain visible beneath white shells.

**Open Interval** — bodies become gaps outlined by luminous clothing. Units gain attack and internal movement, but lose hp and earn less from settled provinces; empty silhouettes carry the same weapons and saffron sashes.

Court armies appear as evenly spaced white masks, vertical polearms, and thin cavalry lines with deliberate empty gaps. Materials are matte porcelain, plum felt, brass, and saffron silk. Their plum banner bears a white open square with a brass dot outside one corner. Their fortresses are asymmetrical courts linked by narrow bridges; the capital, **Seven-Pause**, surrounds an empty central plot no one enters. Armies muster on brass-marked lanes and depart when suspended bells fall silent.

Names favor measured syllables, number-place compounds, and time titles: Vessa, Oren, Talu, Seven-Pause, Last Chime, Pauseguard, Keeper of the Third Measure.

### The Underweather Assembly

**Underweather Assembly** — hollow-bodied people who collect storms inside themselves and govern through loud public argument.

The Assembly is volatile ranged pressure backed by cheap, weak front lines. Its faction rule is **Forecast Vote** — each turn it chooses Rain, Gale, or Heat, granting all Assembly units a small visible bonus to defense, attack, or morale until resolution. The choice is public in reports but made simultaneously, so the player tailors the army to likely battles without knowing opponents’ final orders.

**Chair Sola Bront** is a mortal-line second-rank ruler whose Open Session adds gold in populous provinces. **The Low Thunder** is a god in the front rank whose Pressure Front gives allied ranged units +1 attack in its province and returns weaker after death.

Roster seed:

- Clay Delegates: **Quorum** — gains +1 defense while at least three Clay Delegates stand.
- Rainneedlers: **Downpour** — gains +2 attack when its side has fewer front-rank units than second-rank units.
- Thunder Vats: **Discharge** — deals +4 damage with hits in round one, then has −2 attack.
- Storm Chair: **Call to Order** — allied fighters gain +2 morale after the side first loses a unit.

**Clear-Sky Covenant** — storms are expelled and shaped into permanent aerial servants. Units become cheaper and more accurate but lose hp, while hills yield more gold; pale kites hover above terracotta bodies.

**Worldstorm Body** — every citizen becomes a walking pressure system. Units gain hp and first-round attack, recruitment costs rise, and conquered provinces suffer reduced income for several turns; dark clouds fill transparent chest cavities while turquoise cords retain their hue.

From above, terracotta front ranks form small clustered circles behind which tall copper vats flash. Signature materials are red clay, oxidized copper, turquoise cord, and storm-gray glass. Their turquoise banner bears a white spiral contained in a red hexagonal frame. Fortresses are open amphitheaters ringed by copper lightning towers. The capital, **Roaring Forum**, climbs around a permanent cloud well. Companies muster in debating circles, then align behind the proposal that wins.

Names use open vowels, weather words, and civic titles: Sola Bront, Avo, Neme, Roaring Forum, Cloudstep, Rainneedler, Chair of Vats.

## 4. Rivalries and simultaneous-turn drama

The Crown and Clans dispute whether unity should be built through durable law or continually renewed loyalty. The Compact covets the Orchard’s preserved dead, while the Orchard considers memory stripped from bodies an unforgivable waste. The Court wants conflict made orderly; the Assembly believes uncertainty is the only defense against tyranny.

These rivalries become dramatic because nobody waits politely for another faction’s plan. A Crown army may race down its roads to reinforce a province just as a Clan warband bypasses it. The Court can appoint a predicted meeting, only for the Assembly’s Gale vote to alter the likely result. The Compact may Recut wounded defenders while the Orchard advances expecting easy casualties. Reports can explain these moments as intersecting intentions: who expected a border battle, who arrived first, which public promise shaped the outcome, and why the forecast changed.

## 5. Ascension sites

**First Clauses** — places where one of reality’s earliest promises remains unfinished and can still be amended.

One may be a bridge whose two ends stand in different seasons, a crater where every spoken title becomes briefly visible, or a stairway descending upward into daylight. They are not generic temples. Each is a bright map landmark with a unique silhouette, quiet terrain around it, and a vertical ribbon of pale light visible from a strategy-camera height.

Holding a First Clause lets a faction anchor part of its desired final mandate. Holding enough allows the ruler to join those clauses into an ascension. Rivals want them because only these sites can make a promise binding on the whole world. Their tooltips state ownership, progress, and the exact victory condition; their appearance changes by adding the holder’s material accents without using faction colors as player colors.

## 6. Magic school seeds

**Witness** — magic of observation, truth, forecasts, and revealing hidden armies. Its battle spells expose defense or improve accuracy.

**Imprint** — magic that wakes Deep Memory in roads, forts, graves, and terrain. Its rituals improve provinces or replay an old local protection.

**Revision** — dangerous magic that temporarily alters one clause of a body or rule. It can change attack, defense, or rank behavior for a battle without undoing permanent transformations.

**Chorus** — magic of shared morale, command, healing, and collective motion. Its strongest effects require a standing commander.

**Severance** — magic that breaks hostile mandates at their point of application without permanently deleting them. It weakens traits, province abilities, or magical effects for a clear duration.

**Unsaid** — magic drawn from promises refused or left incomplete. It creates concealment, false movement forecasts, and costly attacks against rulers, but never hides odds or rules from the player.

## 7. Tone and lookalike risks

The world’s darkness comes from choices: a good law can consume its author; ancestors can become military resources; a people can perfect itself until nobody remembers why. It is not hopeless. Hearth Leagues defend ordinary lives, factions contain sincere ideals, and every transformation offers beauty as well as loss. Bright materials, bold silhouettes, argumentative rulers, funeral gardens, storm parliaments, and moving glass armies keep the world colorful and characterful.

The Aurel Crown risks resembling a familiar generic human kingdom, so its defining image must be public promises becoming physical infrastructure, not chosen-one monarchy. The Thornmane Clans risk generic beast barbarians; their kinship ethics, mixed species, and deliberate use of shared wounds must outweigh savagery. The Glasswake Compact could drift toward familiar aquatic mystics, so it should emphasize memory craft, black internal frames, and civic accounting rather than fish features. The Ember Orchard could resemble generic plant undead; ceramic fruit bodies and consensual ancestral households should remain central. The Interval Court risks abstract clockwork people, so it uses no gears or mechanical timepieces. The Underweather Assembly risks elemental storm folk, so its loud civic culture, hollow vessels, and turn-by-turn votes must carry the design.
