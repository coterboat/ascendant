# R1 pitch, Claude's seat: Halfmade

This answers `PACKET.md` §8. Stats are sketches (hp/attack/defense/morale/cost). A **bold** term is new and needs a codex entry.

## 1. The premise: the Wright stopped at noon

The world is **Halfmade**. Its maker, **the Wright**, was building it by hand: raising hills, pouring seas, shaping peoples. At noon on the last day she set her tools down mid-stroke and walked out for good.

The sun has been sinking ever since and has never reached the horizon. It is always the golden hour when the work should be done and isn't: **the Long Afternoon**. Some peoples were made whole, some were never given faces, and death itself was only half built.

**What ascension literally is:** the Wright's unspent making still lies in the world. Gather enough of it where her hands stopped, pick up her tools, and you finish the world in your image: endless noon, a hunting night, a rain that washes everything clean. Six factions want six endings, and only one gets written. On the 3D map the sky shifts toward the leader's ending, so everyone sees who is close.

## 2. The source of power: Quick

**Quick** is the Wright's unspent making: warm, gold and alive, as in "the quick and the dead". Every core system is something done with it:

- **Quick held makes a ruler.** A ruler is whoever has gathered Quick into one body. Kept power makes a king a paladin and a god a giant. One vessel holds only so much, hence the pool of 10.
- **Quick poured is a mandate.** Poured into a people, Quick sets as a law of their nature. Iron Discipline isn't training: a Crown soldier physically can't run while his captain stands. Mandates outlive the ruler because poured Quick lives in the people. **A hurried pour spills**, so a mid-game sacrifice costs +1.
- **Death spills Quick by kind.**
  - A mortal vessel cracks: half spills and the heir catches the rest (half power, 2 turns).
  - The undying bound theirs into an object, like the sunstone: nothing spills, but re-forming is slow (full power, 4 turns).
  - A god *is* Quick: each death tears a piece away for good (−3), but it returns in a turn.
- **Pour enough and the making resumes:** the people get *finished*, and a thing can only be finished one way. That is a **Becoming** (a transformation), and why forks lock in.
- **A Birthright is how the Wright left you:** one free, innate rule per faction, beside the mandates. Most asymmetry lives here. Magic (the six crafts, §6) is the second source.

## 3. The six factions

### 3.1 The Aurel Crown: Keepers of Noon
*They want the sun nailed back at noon: a lit, lawful world forever.*

**Fantasy:** ordinary people made extraordinary because their king pours everything into them. This is the spend faction.

**Birthright, Charter:** written Quick pours cleanly, so **the Crown's mid-game sacrifices cost no +1.**

**Your turn:** Charter makes waiting free, so the Crown holds power back and pours exactly what the war needs once it sees the enemy's build. Does Corvane give up 3 for Royal Armories now, or stay strong enough to survive Stonegate?

**Rulers and roster:** unchanged (packet §4–5). The sunstone is a chip of the sun the Wright meant to fix at noon.

**Becomings:**
- **Noonward:** the bible's celestial look. Knights become sunriders, and lit provinces pay more.
- **Ironsworn:** the oath poured into metal. Living iron, slow and unbreaking, with upkeep in ore.

**Look (3D):** tidy rectangles and spear forests: a grid of vertical lines under square blue banners. Materials: polished steel, white linen, blue enamel, gilt.

**Fortress:** white limestone walls, blue slate cone roofs, a gilt sun-disc over each gate. Crownkeep's glass lantern-tower holds the sunstone, glowing like a second sun: the first beacon on the map.

**Muster:** blue squares drill in a sand yard beside a tiltyard, then march out in step when the great gate drops.

**Naming:** short golden court names (Corvane, Edrin). Places end in -keep, -march or -watch. Epithets are virtues ("the Steady").

### 3.2 The Thornmane Clans: the Night Hunt
*They want the sun to set at last, so the long hunting night can begin.*

**Fantasy:** proud beast tribes who love the world unfinished: unfenced and alive. Their god is a beast of briar and raw Quick.

**Birthright, Wild Ground:** **in forest and hills the Clans get the terrain bonus whether they attack or defend.**

**Your turn:** the momentum faction. Wounds are fuel, fear is a weapon, and Wild Ground makes *attacking into the woods* their best move, the reverse of everyone else's. Strike while bloodied and frenzied, or heal with Feast and lose the edge?

**Rulers and roster:** unchanged (packet §4–5).

**Becomings:**
- **Briarbound:** they grow into their god's body: thorned, regenerating in forest, slow in the open.
- **Duskrunners:** they call the night early: dark-furred hunters who strike first and see far in fog.

**Look (3D):** hunched, horned clumps that never hold a line, with bears looming. Crimson paint pops on green. Materials: tawny fur, bone, moss, briar, hide.

**Fortress:** a **thorn-ring** of giant living briars instead of walls, around a hill crowned by a colossal horned skull. Fangridge's lodge sits in its jaw.

**Muster:** a stamping ground of totem poles and chained bears. When the briars part, the clans pour out running.

**Naming:** English kennings and deed-names (Splits-the-Oak, the Ninth Fang). Places from body and land (Fangridge, Bloodfen). Hard g, k and th.

### 3.3 The Candlewake: the dead who are still waiting
*They want death finished, so there is finally somewhere to rest.*

**Fantasy:** death was only half built, so the dead stay, kept lit in bodies of candle wax. They march, polite and a little sad, to finish the one room the world is missing. "We are not angry. We are late."

**Birthright, Unbreaking and Wick:**
- **The Candlewake never rout.** Their battles go to the last fighter or to round 8.
- **Every regular unit has 6 wick.** It burns 1 each turn, and at 0 the unit gutters out.
- **Any battle relights the survivors to full.**

**Your turn:** a clock on every army: "the Second Procession has 2 turns left. Fight, or go home." The dead *want* contact, so their enemies win by kiting them until they gutter, and simultaneous turns become a chase. Because they never break, a Candlewake defender nearly always reaches round 8 and holds.

**Rulers:**
- **Half-Death** (god, front rank): the half-made god of death: one side of its face carved alabaster, the other only glowing outline. *Toll:* after a battle it wins, 1/2/3/4 fallen enemies rise as Wickwardens in your army.
- **The Chandler** (mortal line, second rank): the living candlemaker who keeps the dead. *Vigil:* your units in her province burn no wick and gain 1/2/3/4 wick each turn.

**Roster:** morale is "–" because they never rout.

| unit | sketch | trait |
|---|---|---|
| Wickwardens (infantry) | 7/9/8/–/6 | **Pall**: enemies attacking it have −10% hit chance. |
| Keeners (infantry) | 6/11/4/–/7 | **Harrow**: hp it takes counts double toward the enemy breaking. |
| Great Taper (monster) | 20/13/5/–/18 | **Flare**: when it falls, every standing enemy fighter takes 2 damage. |
| Lamplighter (commander) | 22/11/9/–/30 | **Rekindle**: after a battle its side wins, half its fallen units (rounded down) stand again at half hp. |


**Becomings:**
- **The Golden Wake:** they settle into beeswax and keep hives. Wick lasts twice as long, recruits cost hives, and provinces warm to them.
- **The Black Wick:** their flames die and they become smoke. No wick, harder to hit, recruiting only from the enemy's fallen.

**Look (3D):** a field of tiny flames. Every figure wears one on its crown, so blocks read as glowing dots. Materials: dripping ivory tallow, soot-black wicks and armour, brass snuffers, lilac ribbons, a candle-amber accent. Banner: lilac cloth, soot border, drip-cut hem, an ivory candle ringed by small flames.

**Fortress:** a **wakehouse**, a stepped tower of candles melted over itself for centuries, its ledges crowded with flames. The capital, **the Long Vigil**, is a hill of old wax around one candle as tall as a keep.

**Muster:** processions filing out of the wakehouse, each carrying its flame: glowing dotted lines across the map.

**Naming:** soft English mourning words and epitaphs: Mother Tallow, Kept-Till-Morning, Small Ember. Places end in -wake or -vigil. Units are named for household jobs.

### 3.4 The Stormherd: shepherds of weather
*They want the Washing: one great rain to scrub the world clean and start the morning over.*

**Fantasy:** gruff, funny nomads in yellow oilskins who herd living weather across the high fells: storm-cloud sheep, thunder rams, a grandfather thunderhead.

**Birthright, Grazing and Storm-borne:**
- **Each army earns the income of the province it stands in, owned or not.** Land they own but have left pays half.
- **Battles with a Stormherd army are fought in the storm:** no terrain or fort bonus for anyone.

**Your turn:** where do the herds graze? Every army is wallet and sword at once. Spreading out earns more but risks getting caught alone, and grazing enemy land starves them. Forts don't stop the herd, so turtles must fight in the rain. But Noonmarks have to be *held*, and the herd wants to move. That tension is the faction.

**Rulers:**
- **Grandsire Thunder** (god, front rank): a living thunderhead shaped like an old ram. *Cloudburst:* every enemy-owned province next to it earns 1/2/3/4 less gold.
- **The Bellmother** (mortal line, front rank): the chief shepherd with the great brass bell, her heirs counted in the old sheep-count (Bellmother Yan, Tan, Tethera). *Shearing:* the province she stands in pays you +2/3/4/5 gold, owned or not.

**Roster:**

| unit | sketch | trait |
|---|---|---|
| Crooks (infantry) | 9/9/5/8/7 | **Gathering**: +1 attack each round after the first, up to +3. |
| Thundersheep (beast) | 12/11/4/7/10 | **Shock**: treats its target's defense as 4 lower. |
| Thunder Ram (monster) | 22/13/8/9/21 | **Thunderclap**: the first enemy it hits in a battle loses its next attack. |

Gathering is the anti-Charge (strongest in round 4, not 1), and Shock answers Shield Wall.

**Becomings:**
- **Thunderborn:** the herds go up. Fragile flying flocks that raid deep and reach commanders.
- **Floodborn:** the herds go down into rivers. Slow, healing in water, wearing forts away: the siege path.

**Look (3D):** rolling cloud-blobs dotted with yellow oilskins, rams like small thunderheads, and cyan lightning that reads at any distance. Materials: slate-grey cumulus wool, yellow oilskin, brass bells, drystone. Banner: oilskin yellow, slate border, a fringe of little bells, a storm-cloud ram's head with a cyan lightning fork.

**Fortress:** a **fold**, concentric drystone sheepfold rings with turf-roofed huts on a high fell. One huge storm cloud is **tethered above it on chains**, flickering: roof, beacon and armoury at once.

**Muster:** herds penned in the rings, until the gates open and the flock floods downhill like weather breaking.

**Naming:** hill-farm words (tup, hogg, gimmer, fell, gill, scar) and weather words: Gimmer Rattlecloud, Old Tup Hailstone. Ordinals come from the sheep-count.

### 3.5 The Kilnborn: the people of fired clay
*They want the Last Firing: bake the world hard so it never cracks or changes again.*

**Fantasy:** the Wright set them on the sill to dry and left before the firing. They fire themselves, and each firing is a coming of age. Patient, ceremonial, house-proud, glazed in gorgeous colours.

**Birthright, Kilnwork:** they can raise a **kiln** in any province they own (gold, 2 turns). A kiln is a fort they can recruit at. Their units are hard and brittle: high defense, low hp.

**Your turn:** where does the next kiln go? Each kiln is a forward base and a fort, so they advance like a siege line, and a half-built kiln is a tempting target for two turns. Every hit that lands hurts brittle units, so they love forts and fear Shock and the storm.

**Rulers:**
- **The Three Kiln-Mothers** (undying council, second rank): three elder potters, nearly stone from refiring. **Their power is split across three bodies in three places**, and each re-fires at a kiln 4 turns after breaking. *Quick Firing:* a kiln ordered in a Mother's province is finished this turn, and costs 0/2/4/6 gold less.
- **The Unfired** (god, front rank): the Wright's last clay figure, a soft giant with her thumbprint still on its brow. *Wet Clay:* your units in its province heal 2/4/6/8 hp a turn.

**Roster:**

| Firepots (infantry) | 6/11/8/7/9 | **Splash**: each hit also deals 1 damage to another enemy in the same rank. |

**Becomings:**
- **High Fire:** porcelain. Harder, more brittle, and so beautiful that neutral provinces join for prestige.
- **Slip:** they refuse the kiln for wet clay. Soft and regenerating, they merge wounded units and recruit anywhere, without kilns.

**Look (3D):** round jar-bodies with loop handles and lids for helmets, in tight blocks under towering walking kilns and smoking chimneys. Materials: terracotta, cream slip, turquoise glaze, kiln-black. Banner: terracotta with a turquoise border and a cream bottle-kiln with a glowing orange mouth.

**Fortress:** a **kilnhold**, tall bottle kilns behind glazed-brick walls under turquoise tiles, smoke plumes visible across the map. The capital, **the Great Kiln**, is a stepped kiln-mountain whose mouth always glows.

**Muster:** drying yards where new soldiers stand in silent rows, then march out through the kiln mouth still glowing.

**Naming:** potters' words (slip, grog, bisque, sagger, coil). A name is clay body plus glaze: Grog Turquoise, Bisque-the-Elder. Councils are Firings. Places: Kilnhollow, Saggerstead.

### 3.6 The Revel: the people with borrowed faces
*They want to finish the Wright's face, and wear it.*

**Fantasy:** the Wright never carved their faces, so they make masks and become whatever the mask is. A festival-nation that never ends: generous hosts, terrifying guests. Visitors who stay too long lose their faces.

**Birthright, Masks:**
- **Each turn you secretly give each army a mask, which is a trait.** Every regular unit's trait is **Masked**: it has its army's mask that turn.
- **They start with three masks:** Brass (Shield Wall), Grin (Berserk) and Hare (Skirmish).
- **They can carve any trait they have fought against into a new mask.**

**Your turn:** the purest read on the opponent, who sees your army but not its mask until the battle. If the Crown expects Shield Wall and brings Knights, wear the Grin. Forecasts show a range across masks, so both players see the bet. The game's meta is the Revel's tech tree.

**Rulers:**
- **The Host** (mortal line, front rank): the mask *is* the ruler, and whoever picks it up becomes the next Host. *Masquerade:* the Host may travel inside any of your armies, and opponents see only which armies might hold it.
- **The Wright's Face** (god, second rank): the face the Wright was carving for herself, unfinished and floating, wearing whoever it likes. *Borrowed Glory:* your masked units have +1/1/2/2 attack in its province's battles.

**Roster:**

| unit | sketch | trait |
|---|---|---|
| Revellers (infantry) | 8/9/5/7/7 | **Masked** |
| Stiltwalkers (heavy infantry) | 12/11/8/8/12 | **Masked** |
| Great Puppet (monster) | 26/9/9/12/20 | **Spectacle**: every enemy attack that can reach it targets it. |

The Puppet soaks up a charge. Second Act opens in Brass and finishes in Grin.

**Becomings:**
- **True Face:** they choose one mask forever and grow it as a real face: no more guessing, but big stats and a new roster.
- **The Endless Revel:** the masks become the people and wear the conquered, recruiting beaten factions' unit types.

**Look (3D):** tall and spindly stilts and ribbons, the Great Puppet's head bobbing above the crowd. Matte-black bodies with white masks make high-contrast dots. Materials: black velvet, white lacquer, rose-pink and lime motley in diamonds, gilt. Banner: black-and-white diamonds, rose border, a white half-smiling mask.

**Fortress:** a **pavilion** of striped silk over scaffolding behind painted castle fronts: a fortress from the enemy side, a stage set from behind. The capital, **the Grand Stage**, is an amphitheatre under a giant hanging mask.

**Muster:** a dressing-yard where masks hang on racks like weapons. The army costumes in public, then parades out behind the Puppet.

**Naming:** roles, not names: the Prompter, Second Lady, the Weeping Uncle. A Reveller is called by today's mask. Sing-song doubled sounds (Tumtum, Lalla). Places: Ribbon Fair.

## 4. Rivalries, and why simultaneous turns make drama

There are three old wars, each a clash of endings:
- **Noon against Night** (Crown against Clans): order wants the sun nailed up, and the wild wants it down. The M1 matchup becomes the world's oldest quarrel.
- **Fire against Rain** (Kilnborn against Stormherd): kilns are forts, the storm ignores forts, and rain softens unfired clay. The builder hunts a nomad who won't stand still.
- **The stolen faces** (Revel against Candlewake): the Revel's finest masks are pressed from the wax faces of the dead. The Candlewake want their faces back, and the Revel want the play to go on.

Across them: the Crown's law against the Revel's lies, and the Clans' fear, which does nothing to the Unbreaking dead.

Simultaneous turns make this drama because every faction **bets blind**: the mask, the graze, the half-built kiln, the dead's clock, the bloodied charge, the held-back power. The report is the reveal: "You both marched on the Halfway Mountain. The Revel wore Brass." A fair scene: it was a read, not a dice roll.

## 5. Noonmarks: the ascension sites

A **Noonmark** is where the Wright's hand rested when she stopped: the world there is literally unfinished, and Quick pools in it. On the 3D map the land runs out into glowing outline and flat raw colour, as if the paint stopped.

Six seeds:
- **The Halfway Mountain:** a peak that ends in mid-air as glowing lines. Its holder sees far (fog, M5).
- **The Pouring:** a waterfall falling out of a hole in the sky into a lake still filling. Income and healing.
- **The Wright's Bench:** her stone workbench, tools still on it. Cheaper paths and Becomings.
- **The Sun-Nail:** a golden nail the size of a tower, meant to pin the sun at noon. Morale for its holder, and the Crown's holy ground.
- **The Blank:** a white field where nothing has been decided. Battles here never have a defender. The Revel are born here.
- **The Cold Kiln:** the Wright's own kiln, never lit. A fort anyone holding it can recruit at.

**Proposal: Noonmarks drip Quick,** +1 kept power per 5 turns held, so they matter before victory and ruler power gets a source on the map. M2 says kept power never rises, so it's Alex's call.

## 6. Magic seeds: the six crafts

Magic is doing a little of the Wright's work yourself. Her six crafts are the schools, in opposed pairs:
- **Kindling** (fire, light, sight through fog) against **Weaving** (illusion, false armies, bindings).
- **Shaping** (clay, stone, iron: walls, golems) against **Weathering** (wind, rain, time: storms, eroding forts).
- **Sowing** (growth, blood, beasts, healing) against **Paring** (the knife: curses, endings, the dead).

Leads: Crown Kindling, Clans Sowing, Candlewake Paring, Stormherd Weathering, Kilnborn Shaping, Revel Weaving. Each mixes in a second craft, and fog of war becomes Kindling against Weaving.

## 7. Tone, and red-team flags

Dark but not grim: the dead are kind, the face-thieves throw a party, and the beast-god loves its cubs. Darkness comes from what things are, not from mud-brown palettes. Each new faction grows from a homely craft (candle-making, shepherding, pottery, theatre): original and easy to picture.

Lookalike risks for the red team:
- **The absent maker** echoes Dominions' vanished Pantokrator. Here nobody wants worship: the prize is finishing the world.
- **Masks** sit near Majora's Mask and Game of Thrones' "Faceless". Revel masks are roles, not new bodies, and that word stays unused.
- **Candle people:** wax soldiers, never cartoon candlesticks with faces.
- **Storm sheep:** slate-grey storm clouds with black faces, far from a famous fluffy yellow electric-sheep monster.
- **Kilnborn glazes** stay invented: no terracotta army.

**Bake-off picks: the Candlewake and the Stormherd.** They read at a glance (a flame on every head, yellow oilskins among storm clouds), and their asymmetries are the simplest to explain and the hardest to master.
