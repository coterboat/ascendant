# R2, Grok: critique

## Claude — Halfmade

**What to steal.** The one-sentence faction rule that rewrites the turn. **Charter** spends the mid-game sacrifice lever the engine already has: the Crown holds power, sees the enemy build, then pours at no +1. **Wild Ground** is the right Clans rule if border battles stay bonus-free: attacking into forest or hills is their best order, which is the reverse of everyone else, and the report can say it in one line. The homely silhouettes are what §0 asked for. A flame on every head, yellow oilskins under a tethered cloud, jar-bodies and smoking bottle-kilns: a block of forty reads before the tooltip does. Steal the rivalry as a blind bet with a report sentence ("you both marched; the Revel wore Brass"). Steal **Noonmarks** as different objects from the strategy camera. Do not steal the power drip.

**What breaks in this engine.** **Candlewake** is three new systems. Morale is a real stat; "never rout" deletes the loss condition and, with no retreats, a defender who is not annihilated holds at round 8. **Wick** is a second resource on every unit, with guttering and a full relight that fights "wounds carry, units do not heal themselves." **Rekindle** and **Toll** put dead fighters back into the army. The battle model destroys the losing side and keeps the standing wounded. **Half-Death** raising enemy dead is not a province ability.

**Masks** fight the rest of the brief at once. A unit has one trait. Assigning it in secret after the opponent commits hides the thing forecasts exist to show. "Carve any trait you have fought" turns one roster into everyone's roster, which is the bloat the brief cut. **Second Act** swaps the trait at round 4. **Spectacle** is a taunt. Targeting is ranks, and everyone strikes at once. **The Host** hiding inside armies fights "the ruler travels alone as its own army" and "nothing hidden that matters." Fog is M5, and even then the rules do not lie.

**The Three Kiln-Mothers** split one ruler into three bodies in three places. A ruler is one army, one province ability, one succession. Player-built kilns are a construction system. Forts are map features; "forts will gate recruitment later" does not mean a two-turn fort you drop anywhere. **Floodborn** wearing forts away depends on sieges that are not built. **Thunderborn** "reach commanders." Nothing in the rank rules reaches past the second rank. Flyers are a silhouette, not a rank.

**Stormherd** stacks two faction rules, and the packet allows one. Grazing income by army position, including on land you do not own, plus half income on empty owned land, is a second economy and the bookkeeping the brief removed. **Cloudburst** taxes every enemy province next door. Province abilities are about the province the ruler is in. **Noonmarks** that add kept power fight the pool of 10. M2's budget does not inflate because you sat on a mountain.

**What reads alike.** Candlewake and the Kilnborn are both warm craft-processions: melt, fire, smoke, glow, a house that is also the furnace. The massing saves them (dots of flame against round jars) only if the kiln-smoke stays a thin plume and the wake stays a field of points. Stormherd and Codex's Underweather are both "we bring the weather." The Revel and Codex's Interval Court are both masked timing factions. Inside this pitch, four of the six names are English job-titles (candlemaker, shepherd, potter, prompter). At codex distance they are one dialect with four hobbies. Crown and Clans are the ones that stay sharp.

**IP lookalike risks.** The Wright who left at noon is Dominions' vanished Pantokrator in a craft apron. "Nobody wants worship" is a real difference, and it arrives after the premise has already landed. **Ironsworn** is a published RPG title; do not use that word for the iron fork. **Candlewake** sits on Dark Souls' "death is unfinished, keep the flame," and the name passes close to Candlekeep. Kindly wax-dead is enough of a turn to survive if the bodies stay people with a flame on the crown, never a candlestick face. The Revel's endless festival that takes faces is Slaanesh, and the ability name **Masquerade** collides with Vampire. Majora and the Faceless Men are the ones Claude already named; Slaanesh is the closer faction. Storm-sheep are fine if they stay slate cloud and yellow cloth. Jar-soldiers with lid-helmets will be read as a terracotta army or as living crockery. The thumbprint giant is Agartha's unfinished statue.

## Codex — The Oathscar

**What to steal.** The social mechanism, not the vocabulary. A mandate outlives the ruler because the power has entered the people; a mid-game sacrifice costs extra because a finished self resists the cut; a god comes back shorter because the idea is used up. That is the cleanest account of the budget in the room. Steal the shape of it: one power, sitting in the ruler, the people, or the ground. Steal **Appointment**: one named move, +attack only if the meeting happens, forecast shows both outcomes. That is simultaneous turns as a decision. Steal **Aftercrop** as a decision that uses wounds that do not heal, with the coupon spent at the capital, not in the field. Steal transformations that state a loss. Steal **Severance** only as a timed, previewed dulling that leaves the mandate in place. Steal the writing rule on **Unsaid**'s better half: magic never falsifies a forecast. Slim neutrals the way **Hearth Leagues** are slim. My Unwoven militia list was three extra unit types the player does not need.

**What breaks in this engine.** **Unsaid**'s false movement forecasts fight pillar 4. A forecast that lies is hidden information about an order. Concealment can wait for fog. A lying arrival cannot. **Held Hour** delays recruitment payment. Gold is paid when the unit is bought. A one-turn IOU is a ledger in the resolution order. **Timely Arrival** "improves forecasts" before fog, when a forecast is already the odds from what you can see. It does nothing legal until it becomes scouting.

**Red Feast** charges more in peaceful turns. There is no upkeep. **Iron Testament** raises "province resistance," which is not a stat. **Packed Pith** changes maximum hp when a partner falls, and current hp then has to be invented. **Briar Communion** spreading wounds is a new damage model. **Many-Tide** and the amphibious Compact pay off on coasts and wetlands. Those terrains are not on the map. **Aftercrop** "in that province" recruits outside the capital. Bank the discount at the capital or the rule has nowhere to land until forts gate recruitment. **Ripening** plus the god's −3 is two permanent power losses for one death. **Exact Order** at 64% sits under **Drilled Ranks** at 70% and will not be felt. **Discharge** is two effects in one trait.

**What reads alike.** Glasswake and my Glazewell are the same faction from a new player's chair: glass, coral, a speaker named Pell, a circular emblem, high defense, low hp. Ember Orchard and Claude's Kilnborn are both fired bodies, kilns, cream shells, hot cores, a furnace capital. Interval Court and the Revel are both porcelain, masks, and the sacred moment of contact. Underweather and Stormherd are both the storm-as-a-people. Inside this pitch, Crown, Compact, Court, and Assembly are four committees (ledger, measure, chair, speaker). Clans and Orchard are the only factions that are bodies first. "Clay" is the front rank of the Assembly and the body of two other seats' craft factions. **Wildkin** as forest neutrals is my word from R1, and it still blurs with Thornmane.

**IP lookalike risks.** **Accord**, and an **Oathscar** that gives promises physical force, is Stormlight. Ideals becoming power is the Radiant premise; the title makes it worse. Keep the mechanism, lose "oath" as the cosmic noun. Ember Orchard from above — round crowns, forked limbs, a mother-tree capital — reads as Sylvaneth or Sylvari even if the fiction is funeral fruit. Interval Court, beings who live in the pauses and treat punctuality as mercy, sits on Discworld's Auditors, and the white masks double the Revel's Majora problem. The Court brief already says no gears; it also needs no empty porcelain faces. Glasswake drifts to Zora if the bodies go amphibious, and to crystal-undead if the glass is the soul. Underweather is the least exposed of the four if the debating circle and the copper vat read before the lightning does. **Red Feast** stacks a second blood-cult on the bible's bloodpact.

## My own weakest part

The cord sky explains the budget and then the four new factions do not use it. Each one is two or three small modifiers instead of one turn. The Choir is "be the defender," which every army already wants, dressed in glass that Codex also brought. The Kite Houses pretend to fly: the engine moves army to province, and a wing is a silhouette, not a movement rule, so they are fragile Charge troops with extra vocabulary. The Procession is a rout-arithmetic puzzle — ignore 10% of hp, then add 8 phantom hp when the litter falls — which a report cannot say in plain cause and effect, and the bowl sits in the same heat-and-procession cluster as Claude's candles and Codex's kiln-orchard. The Open Knot invents a three-body ruler and then withdraws it, and the rest is coupons for neutrals. I also minted a textile manual of terms. A new player cannot finish a first game without a wiki, which is the failure the brief names first.
