# R2, Claude: critique

## Codex: The Oathscar

**What to steal**
- **Hearth Leagues.** Independents with a terrain flavour (spears on plains, stoneguards on hills, wildkin in forest) make the neutral map a place instead of grey filler, and they give Sworn Vassals a story. Grok reached it too, and the repo already calls them Free Holds.
- **Appointment's target.** A faction rule built around the meeting battle (nobody is the defender because everyone arrived this turn) is the most honest use of simultaneous turns in any pitch. The +1 is too small (below), but it aims at the right thing.
- **Forecast Vote's size.** Three fixed stances, chosen blind and revealed at resolution, is the approachable version of my Revel masks. I'm capping the Revel at three masks because of it.
- **"An established self resists division."** This is the cleanest reason any seat gave for the +1 on a mid-game sacrifice.
- **Seven-Pause's empty plot that no one enters.** The best single fortress image in the room.

**What breaks in this engine**
- **Rank traits on units that can't reach the rank.** Only Volley puts a unit in the second rank, and a unit gets one trait. Prism Casters' Refraction (+2 attack "while protected in the second rank") and Rainneedlers' Downpour (a bonus when your side has more second-rank than front-rank units) can never switch on, because neither faction fields a Volley unit.
- **Bonuses too small to feel.** Exact Order (64% base hit chance) is worth one point of attack. Appointment's +1 attack is 4% hit chance in one battle a turn. A faction rule has to be felt, and no player will feel these.
- **Vessa's "improved battle forecasts."** Pillar 4 promises honest forecasts to everyone. A ruler ability that improves forecasts implies that everyone else's are worse.
- **The province abilities all converge.** Codex's five new rulers bring two gold abilities (Clear Account, Open Session), two heals (Warm Soil and Stillwater, both Mending Light in a new coat) and a payment delay. That is the Crown's two abilities dealt out again.
- **Packed Pith** changes maximum hp mid-battle while wounds persist. When the other Husk Bearer falls, does a unit on 3 hp die?
- **Recut** is a once-a-turn disband-and-discount order. That is the busywork the brief cuts.
- **The transformations mostly move stats** ("gain hp and defense but cost more"). §7.5 wants the roster, the economy's rules and how provinces respond to change.

**What reads alike**
- **Three ceramic factions:** Glasswake (pearl ceramic), the Ember Orchard (cream ceramic, kiln walls, Stone Fruit shells) and the Assembly (Clay Delegates in red clay). From the strategy camera that's three pale, glazed blocks.
- **Across the room:**
  - Glasswake, Grok's Glazewell and my Kilnborn glazes: every seat grabbed glass or glaze.
  - The Assembly and my Stormherd are both nations with storms in their bodies, down to a thunder god each (the Low Thunder, Grandsire Thunder).
  - The Ember Orchard and my Candlewake are both the gentle dead carried by a small flame.
  - The Interval Court's "evenly spaced white masks" sit right on top of the Revel's white masks.
  - Codex's Recut is Grok's word for a transformation.
- **Six cosmology terms** (Oathscar, Accord, Crownfire, Deep Memory, Living Change, First Clauses) arrive before a single faction does. Each one needs a codex entry.

**IP lookalike risks**
- **Oaths that give literal power, plus "Radiance", in a world called the Oathscar.** This sits close to Sanderson's Stormlight Archive, where sworn Ideals grant power and the knights are Radiant. An ascension named Common Radiance makes it worse.
- **The Ember Orchard.** Seed-pods that carry the dead and grow them new bodies are Age of Sigmar's Sylvaneth and their soulpods.
- **The Interval Court.** Keepers of time who live between moments are near Pratchett's History Monks (*Thief of Time*) and Ende's *Momo*.
- **An amphibious nation** in a Dominions-spirit game invites the comparison with Dominions' undersea nations, which the brief bans.

## Grok: The Strung

**What to steal**
- **The repo mismatch.** Grok caught that the repo's proposed M4 already names the forks: Celestial Host and Living Iron for the Crown, Thorn Wild and Blood Pact for the Clans, with the schools Sun, Law, the Wild and Blood. My packet said they weren't set, and my R1 invented others. I'm adopting the repo's four.
- **Forswearing.** Each transformation closes one mandate. It uses §7.5's "which mandates become available" without any new system, and it makes the fork hurt on the build screen.
- **Free Holds with numbers.** One trait per terrain garrison, a Hold Captain from 8 income, one fighter per 4 income. Ready for the engine.
- **Traits built on the rout line.** Thick Count, Dead Weight and Ringing Ground read the morale formula and play with it. My Keeners' Harrow was an attempt at this, and I wrote it wrong. I'm fixing it Grok's way.
- **The Choir against the Kite Houses.** One wants to arrive first, and the other wants the meeting where nobody is the defender. The rivalry is made of the resolution order itself.
- **Sag-yards pay +2 research.** That's a reason to hold a victory site before you win, and it doesn't break M2's "kept power never rises".

**What breaks**
- **Two rules per faction:** Ringing Ground + Still Rim, Open Sky + First Wind, Thick Count + Reliquary, Loose Welcome + Second Seat. §7 allows one. (So did I.)
- **"Flyer" is a subsystem.** Open Sky, Cast Shadow, Haul and the Stake spell all depend on a flyer tag the engine doesn't have. Reed Lancers carry "flyer" and Stoop, which is two properties on a one-trait unit. Who can hit a flyer, and which rank does it stand in? Nobody has written it.
- **The Choir can't take ground.** Lock Rim, Still Rim, Hold the Note, a fort and a forest stack to +9 defense. Attack 11 against defense 18 hits 32% of the time for 2 damage. In a game you win by capturing ground, with no sieges built, that's a stalemate faction. And with attack 7 to 9, it can't attack out either.
- **Dead Weight and Count the Living cancel out.** One unit's trait is a drawback and the commander's trait removes it. That's two codex entries for zero effect.
- **The Open Knot runs out of map.** Its rule and its commander feed on Free Holds, which don't grow back. Grok admits it's "average against a rival". By mid-game it's a faction with no rule.
- **Vocabulary.** Lengths, shuttle, standing stitch, splice, recut, swatch, canopy, next band, live cord, sag-yard, Unwoven: the UI's own words (power, mandate, sacrifice, fork, preview) each get a second name. Rule 7 says invent few terms.

**What reads alike**
- **Three pale factions:** the Choir (chalk, coral), the Procession (cream, soot) and the Open Knot (white rope on marigold). Pale discs, cream files and white loops blur together, and pale figures fight the bible's rule that every figure needs a dark area.
- **Too many guards:** Rimguard, Bowlguard, Stake Guard, Hearthguard, Doorward.
- **Across the room:**
  - Glazewell and Codex's Glasswake.
  - Black reed in both the Kite Houses and the Glasswake.
  - Warden Pell beside Codex's Speaker Pellucid of Pel-Lume.
  - Grok's Toll and my Half-Death's Toll.
  - Bells in the Choir, Codex's Court and my Stormherd.

**IP lookalike risks**
- **A woven world with a loom, threads and a shuttle** sits near Wheel of Time's Pattern and LucasArts' *Loom*, with its weavers' guild. Grok's fate-weaving flag undersells this.
- **The Amber Procession,** carrying a sealed relic in single file, reads as the Ark of the Covenant before it reads as a tool.
- **The built Thornmane** (a horned-skull banner, boar-headed beastmen) are close to Warhammer's Beastmen. Grok is right to flag it, and the flag applies to every seat.

## The weakest part of my own R1

The Revel. I dressed a subsystem up as a faction: a hidden trait per army per turn, a library that grows with every trait it fights, a commander that switches to a second hidden choice, and forecasts that become ranges across an open-ended list. It's my most fun idea on paper and the hardest to explain in two sentences, which §7.7 requires. The same habit runs through the rest of the pitch. I gave the Candlewake three rules (never rout, wick, relight) and the Stormherd and the Revel two each, which is exactly what I charge Grok with. The Candlewake's never-rout was also a turtle that I advertised as a feature: a defender that can't break, holds after round 8, and sits in the Chandler's no-burn province is a wall. And I left out independents, ignored the repo's M4 forks, and broke M2 with the Quick drip.
