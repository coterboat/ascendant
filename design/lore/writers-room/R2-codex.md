# R2, Codex (revised): The Oathscar

## What changed and why

- I kept Accord as the single cosmological cause but separated its three practical forms more sharply, so every faction does not sound like another kind of promise.
- I stole Claude’s public victory-pressure image: the sky visibly changes toward the leading ascension, making the race readable without a panel.
- I stole Claude’s fortress principle: every muster ground has a distinct “army spills out” animation readable from the strategy camera.
- I stole Grok’s defender-versus-meeting contrast. The Glasswake Compact now wants to arrive first and hold; the Underweather Assembly wants uncertain same-turn collisions.
- I cut Glasswake’s Recut replacement rule, Interval Court’s Appointment rule, and Underweather’s three-choice vote. All required extra order-state or unit bookkeeping.
- I replaced the Interval Court with the Hearthbound Concord, a faction built around neutral provinces, borrowing Grok’s strongest Open Knot idea while keeping the packet’s existing Hearth Leagues.
- I made every transformation name a plain declaration and tied each branch to roster, economy, mandates, and appearance.
- I reduced magic to six plain schools with jobs that forecasts and reports can explain.
- I made the six armies differ first by battlefield desire: formation, wounds, prepared defense, casualties, neutral expansion, or same-turn collision.

## 1. The world premise, and what ascension literally is

The world bears the **Oathscar**: a bright fracture across land and sky left when the first universal promise was broken.

Reality in this world grows from commitments that many living minds accept and repeat. A trail becomes a road because generations agree where it leads. A judge becomes a sovereign because strangers agree to obey the same decisions. A grave becomes sacred because descendants keep returning. Repeated commitments press grooves into reality until conduct becomes natural law.

**Accord** is the force created when living beings knowingly bind themselves to a shared rule.

The ancient rulers discovered that all local agreements could be joined beneath one promise. They attempted the First Accord, which would have bound land, people, death, and sovereignty into a stable whole. At the final declaration, the rulers disagreed over who would stand as its voice. The unfinished force tore upward. The Oathscar still arcs across the sky like a white-gold crack and descends into several provinces where reality remains writable.

A ruler gathers Accord because the ruler is the recognized meeting point of a people’s commitments. Ruler power is not private mana. It is the portion of collective possibility still held in one person.

Power kept close makes the ruler more fully the nation’s living emblem: tougher, deadlier, more magically capable, and better able to change the province where the ruler stands. Power surrendered becomes a mandate.

A **mandate** is a public rule made physically true by ruler power.

Royal Armories does not merely allocate better budgets. It teaches every loyal forge the same impossible precision. Iron Discipline does not erase fear; it makes the commander’s continued presence proof that the army’s common promise still holds.

Mandates survive a ruler because surrendered power no longer resides in that ruler. It persists in drill, inheritance, roads, tools, bodies, and expectations. An heir may be weak while the kingdom remains well armed.

Mid-game sacrifice costs one additional power because identity has hardened. Before the game, the ruler and nation establish what their reign means. Later, removing more power tears away a settled piece of the ruler: remembered victories, future descendants, a divine name, or the integrity of an undying body. The preview shows the exact mandate gained and the ruler statistics and ability rank lost.

Succession follows where kept Accord is anchored. A mortal ruler’s authority must be recognized again in an heir, losing half during the contested transfer. An undying ruler has placed identity in an object or location and rebuilds slowly around it without losing power. A god returns quickly from continued worship, but each destruction makes the worshippers’ idea of that god smaller, permanently reducing power.

A transformation locks because Accord can sustain one settled answer to “what are we becoming?” Once births, crafts, laws, and bodies follow that answer, the rejected futures cease to be possible. Each preview therefore shows three concrete views: the transformed army formation, a typical province card, and the capital fortress. The player sees lost units, mandates, income rules, and appearance before confirming.

Ascension is the act of completing the First Accord. A ruler holding enough of the Oathscar’s grounded fractures can speak a final rule through all of them at once. The winner might make law stronger than blood, free every community from distant rulers, turn memory into a shared afterlife, or make change itself the world’s only permanent law.

The race is public. As a faction approaches victory, one sixth of the Oathscar takes on that ascension’s material and motion: ordered rays, grasping briars, glass facets, drifting embers, linked hearth-lights, or rotating storm bands. This steals Claude’s strongest presentation idea from *Halfmade*: the world itself is the victory forecast.

## 2. The sources of power

Accord is one force with three states.

**Crownfire** is Accord concentrated in a recognized person, office, or anchor. It is ruler power, commander authority, and the force behind mandates.

**Deep Memory** is Accord embedded in places and objects. Roads remember processions; forts remember refuge; graves remember names. Province rituals awaken those memories for visible, limited effects.

**Living Change** is Accord settling into bodies and inherited practice. It drives transformations. It also explains why transformed units retain their class silhouette and weapon: the people redefine what they are without forgetting what each soldier does.

Gold remains ordinary wealth: wages, food, tools, transport, and political gifts. Research is the disciplined knowledge needed to direct Accord. None of the three states is collected as a second currency. Their fiction explains existing mechanics rather than adding resource shuffling.

Independent provinces are **Hearth Leagues**: local communities bound by defense promises to neighbors rather than distant rulers. Their garrisons use plain regional units. Sworn Vassals works because a ruler offers a larger agreement they can accept without repudiating their local one.

## 3. Six factions

### The Aurel Crown

The Aurel Crown is a human kingdom trying to prove that durable institutions can be stronger than heroic rulers. Playing it feels like building a reliable machine whose weakest component may be the monarch.

Its existing levers remain formations, income, healing, and internal movement. Spearmen protect Crossbowmen; Knights punish an exposed first round; commanders stabilize morale. King’s Roads creates interior threats. A Crown turn asks whether to concentrate behind a fort, move two provinces to reinforce a forecasted battle, or divide commanders and contest multiple approaches. Persistent wounds make the Ever-Regent’s location matter.

King Corvane is a mortal-line front-rank ruler. Royal Court adds gold in his province. Keeping power makes him a paladin; spending it makes an ordinary man the architect of an exceptional state.

The Ever-Regent is an undying second-rank ruler anchored to Crownkeep’s sunstone. Mending Light heals wounded units in his province.

Roster:

- Spearmen — **Shield Wall:** Gets +2 defense while any other fighter on its side still stands.
- Crossbowmen — **Volley:** Shoots from the protected second rank while enough front-rank units stand.
- Knights — **Charge:** Deals +4 damage with every hit in the first round, or +3 in a forest.
- Knight Captain — **Inspire:** While it stands, its side gets +2 morale in addition to the normal commander bonus.

**The Common Crown** distributes Crownfire through civic offices. Knights become mounted Justiciars with lower attack and higher morale; provinces with forts earn more gold; ruler province abilities lose one rank. New mandates favor income and healing. Steel remains, but gold moves from royal ornaments to thousands of small white-gold collar rings.

**The Iron Testament** puts law into flesh. Regular units gain hp and defense and cost more; healing is less effective; forts gain stronger recruitment value. Bodies and armor become dark riveted living iron with orange seams, while royal-blue trim and original weapons preserve recognition.

At distance the army is a rectangular spear forest with a straight blue second rank and wide white cavalry wedges. Materials are steel, white linen, royal blue, and gold. The banner remains blue with a gold crown above a rising white sun.

Crownkeep is a concentric pale-stone fortress with blue roofs and a central sunstone tower. Companies muster on a square marked by gold lines. At departure, four tidy blocks merge into one column and the main gate drops.

Names favor clear two- or three-syllable personal names and civic compounds: Corvane, Merrow, Edrin; Crownkeep, Westwatch, Rivermarch; Sunward, White Lance.

### The Thornmane Clans

The Thornmane Clans are mixed beastfolk kin-bands who believe loyalty must be renewed through shared danger, never frozen into distant law. Playing them feels like controlling a wounded charge that becomes more dangerous before it collapses.

Their levers are missing hp, attack, enemy morale, mobile recruitment, and victory healing. A Clan turn asks whether wounded Tuskers can win one more battle before their low defense catches up with them, whether the Ninth Fang should establish recruitment away from the capital, and whether the Great Thornmane’s Dread can push a close enemy over its rout threshold. Feast of Victory creates a real tension: winning restores safety but turns off wound bonuses.

The Great Thornmane is a god in the front rank. Dread lowers enemy morale in its battles. It returns quickly but permanently diminished.

The Ninth Fang is a mortal-line front-rank ruler. Warband Muster recruits regular units in the province where the Fang stands.

Roster:

- Tuskers — **Berserk:** Gets +3 attack while below full hp.
- Fangthrowers — **Skirmish:** Can attack protected second-rank units.
- War Bear — **Fury:** While it stands, every enemy side gets −2 morale, within the existing total cap.
- Warchief — **Pack Leader:** While it stands, every fighter on its side gets +1 attack.

**The Shared Briar** joins the clans through living thorn networks. Units gain defense in forests; forest provinces improve recruitment rather than gold; the roster adds a broad Briar Bear that anchors fragile Tuskers. Blood Frenzy becomes unavailable. Fur and bone remain visible beneath moss-green briars, with crimson paint unchanged.

**The Red Hunger** turns victory into sustenance. Recruits cost less but arrive wounded; winning battles heals survivors; peaceful rich provinces earn less gold. The roster loses the War Bear for a lean Red Maw with high attack and low defense. Bone darkens, bodies narrow, and crimson markings shine against tawny fur.

From above the army is an uneven wedge of hunched backs, tusks, javelin fans, and one broad beast mass. Materials remain tawny fur, bone, moss green, and crimson. Its ochre banner bears the bone-white horned skull in green thorns.

Fangridge is a terraced earth-and-stone ring around a living thorn mound. Each kin-band musters around its own fire. At departure, six low thorn gates open and separate circles pour together into one downhill wave.

Names use hard roots, repeated consonants, and earned deed-titles: Rakka, Varn, Mott; Red-Muzzle, Root-Roarer; Fangridge, Thornvale, Bloodfen.

### The Glasswake Compact

The Glasswake Compact consists of amphibious artisans who grow glass armor by surrendering selected memories to communal reservoirs. They are precise defenders who become dangerous only after choosing ground.

This revision steals Grok’s best mechanical contrast: the Compact wants to arrive first, while another faction below wants the same-turn meeting. The Compact uses defense, forts, second-rank attack, and healing. Its single faction rule is **Settled Formation:** Compact regular units get +1 morale when their side is the defender.

A Compact turn asks whether to stop on valuable ground and dare an attack or move into a meeting without its bonus. High defense is not enough if the army arrives late, and a passive army surrenders the map. The opponent can see the prepared position but not whether reinforcements will join it.

Speaker Pellucid is a mortal-line second-rank ruler. Clear Account adds gold in a low- or middle-income province, encouraging useful placement rather than capital parking.

The Returning Shoal is an undying front-rank collective anchored to Pel-Lume’s reservoir. Stillwater heals Compact units in its province if their army did not move that turn.

Roster:

- Shardwardens — **Set Shields:** Gets +2 defense while its side is the defender.
- Prism Casters — **Refraction:** Gets +2 attack while protected in the second rank.
- Tide Striders — **Countercurrent:** Gets +2 attack in battles where the enemy arrived that turn.
- Ledger Bearer — **Exact Order:** While it stands, allied fighters use 64% instead of 60% base hit chance.

**The Clear Vessel** removes private memory until citizens become flawless glass. Units gain defense and cost less, but morale falls; provinces with forts earn more gold. Tide Striders become tall Clear Lancers. Pale cyan bodies contain sharp black frames, with coral-orange knots preserved.

**The Many-Tide Body** puts several minds in each flexible shell. Units gain hp and morale but cost more; wounded armies heal more effectively when stationary; low-income provinces produce an additional small income. Models broaden into low rippling forms while shields, staves, and class silhouettes remain intact.

Compact armies read as cyan fan-shields around black vertical cores, with diagonal caster rods behind them. Materials are sea glass, black reed, pearl ceramic, and coral orange. The charcoal banner carries an orange broken circle crossed by a cyan wave.

Pel-Lume is a stepped glass basin inside black levees around a mirror reservoir. Troops muster knee-deep in radial pools. At departure, the water drains through six channels and reveals ordered files already facing the gates.

Names favor liquid consonants, paired vowels, and craft endings: Loai, Meru, Pellucid; Pel-Lume, Vey Basin, Low Mirror; Shardwarden, Glass Auditor.

### The Ember Orchard

The Ember Orchard is a society of walking funeral growths. Each body carries several consenting dead as warm seeds, so citizenship continues through remembered voices rather than individual resurrection. Playing it feels slow, durable, and willing to exchange bodies for continuity.

Its levers are hp, casualty-triggered traits, post-battle healing, and recruit cost. The faction rule is **Returned Seed:** after a victorious battle, the first destroyed Orchard regular unit reduces the next recruit’s cost in that province by 3 gold. Only one discount can wait in a province, so there is no corpse inventory.

An Orchard turn asks whether a costly hulk can survive, whether a calculated casualty will make a frontier replacement affordable, and whether to attack now for a victory heal or remain behind terrain. Losses help only after a win, so feeding units into hopeless battles never pays.

Grandmother Cinderpod is an undying second-rank ruler anchored to the ancestor furnace. Warm Soil heals wounded units in her province.

Rill of the First Garden is a front-rank god. Ripening reduces regular recruit costs in its province by 1/2/3/4 gold. It returns quickly at reduced power.

Roster:

- Husk Bearers — **Packed Pith:** Gets +4 maximum hp while another Husk Bearer stands.
- Sparkspitters — **Cinder Seed:** Gets +2 attack against targets below full hp.
- Orchard Hulks — **Last Fruit:** When it falls, one wounded allied regular unit heals 4 hp.
- Ash Gardener — **Tending:** After its side wins, every surviving allied regular unit heals 1 hp.

**The Flying Ash** opens every ancestral seed at once. Units lose hp and gain attack; recruits become cheaper; rich provinces lose 1 gold as stored dead are consumed. Sparkspitters become wide ember-pollen fans. Orange crowns hover above charcoal stems, with hot magenta cores visible in hard shadow.

**The Stone Fruit** seals ancestors into ceramic bodies. Units gain hp, defense, and morale but cost more; forts heal surviving units; Orchard Hulks become immense choir jars. Cream shells split to show magenta interiors, while ember-orange leaves preserve the faction read.

An army resembles an orchard walking in rows: round crowns, forked limbs, and huge seed-shaped monsters. Materials are charcoal bark, cream ceramic, hot magenta flesh, and ember orange. The cream banner bears a black seed split by an orange flame.

Cindergrove is a ring of kiln walls around towering ancestor trees and a glowing burial furnace. Bodies muster as buds in furrowed ash. At departure, the furrows crack in sequence and full-grown ranks pull themselves free.

Names join warm vowels with garden and fire images: Omoa, Rill, Cinderpod; Warm Furrow, Cindergrove, Ash Vale; Husk Bearer, Ash Gardener.

### The Hearthbound Concord

The Hearthbound Concord is a traveling federation of cooks, witnesses, bridge keepers, and household militias. It believes no province should be ruled by someone who cannot sit at its table. Playing it feels like turning neutral expansion into a network that must then be defended.

Its levers are Sworn Vassals, neutral morale, modest income, cheap units, and commanders. Its single faction rule is **Open Terms:** neutral sides fighting the Concord have −1 morale.

A Concord turn asks whether to spend power on Sworn Vassals and expand without casualties, attack a rich Hearth League before a rival reaches it, or consolidate cheap garrisons beneath commanders. Open Terms does nothing against rival factions, so early expansion does not become late military superiority by itself.

First Host Nella is a mortal-line second-rank ruler. Guest Right adds gold in a province first acquired within the last two turns, rewarding travel along the frontier.

The Banked Welcome is an undying front-rank figure made from the coals of thousands of accepted hearths. Warm Hall gives allied regular units +1/2/3/4 morale when defending its province. It reforms at the capital’s common oven.

Roster:

- Doorwardens — **Threshold:** Gets +2 defense while its side is the defender.
- Road Slingers — **Waystone:** Gets +2 attack when fighting outside territory owned at the start of the turn.
- Table Beasts — **Fed Well:** Has +4 maximum hp while a commander stands.
- Pact Herald — **Known Terms:** While it stands, enemy morale penalties affecting its side are reduced by 1.

**The Great Household** turns federation into settled common law. Provinces earn more gold; Doorwardens gain heavier shields; Sworn Vassals becomes unavailable because no further autonomous leagues are admitted. Models gain cream plaster plates and copper roof-shaped helmets, while marigold cords remain.

**The Open Road** makes permanent travel the common identity. Regular units cost less and lose defense; recently acquired provinces recruit sooner when fort recruitment arrives; Road Slingers become mounted Cart Slingers. Buildings become lighter timber waystations, and marigold awnings spread over dark teal wagons.

At distance, the Concord is a low, loose cluster of roof-shaped shields, handcarts, and tall cooking-smoke standards. Materials are dark teal wool, warm wood, cream plaster, copper pans, and marigold cord. Its teal banner carries a cream doorway above a copper bowl.

The capital, Commonroof, is a many-courtyard hill town enclosed by linked houses rather than curtain walls. Troops muster at long outdoor tables. At departure, the tables fold into carts and the army exits through several household gates.

Names use short domestic words and two-beat personal names: Nella, Oren, Tavi; Commonroof, Warmdoor, Three Bridges; Doorwarden, Pact Herald.

### The Underweather Assembly

The Underweather Assembly is a people of hollow ceramic bodies who keep weather inside themselves and govern through public argument. Playing it feels volatile: weak fronts protect dangerous ranged vessels, and the army prefers collisions before an enemy settles into terrain.

Its levers are Volley, first-round attack, morale recovery, and battles without a defender. Its single faction rule is **Breaking Weather:** Assembly regular units get +1 attack when no side is the defender.

A turn asks where enemy movement is likely to create a border battle or same-turn meeting. Attacking a settled Compact position is bad; intercepting Compact reinforcements is ideal. Splitting armies creates more collision chances but exposes their cheap front ranks. This is Grok’s arrival-state insight turned into a clean rivalry.

Chair Sola Bront is a mortal-line second-rank ruler. Open Session adds gold in her province if she did not fight that turn, forcing a choice between administration and battle.

The Low Thunder is a front-rank god. Pressure Front gives second-rank units +1/1/2/2 attack in its province’s battles. It returns quickly but loses permanent power.

Roster:

- Clay Delegates — **Quorum:** Gets +1 defense while at least three Clay Delegates stand.
- Rainneedlers — **Volley:** Shoots from the protected second rank while enough front-rank units stand.
- Thunder Vats — **Discharge:** Deals +4 damage with hits in the first round.
- Storm Chair — **Call to Order:** After its side first loses a unit, allied fighters get +2 morale for the rest of the battle.

**The Clear-Sky Accord** expels captive weather into permanent kite servants. Units lose hp and cost less; hills earn more gold; the roster gains a wide flying Kitefront with modest attack. Pale fabric forms hover over terracotta bodies, retaining turquoise cords.

**The Worldstorm Body** makes every citizen a walking pressure system. Units gain hp and first-round attack and cost more; newly conquered provinces earn less gold for two turns. Clay Delegates broaden into storm-filled Glass Delegates. Dark clouds churn inside gray glass chests, crossed by turquoise ties.

From above, terracotta front ranks form small circular clusters before tall copper vats and diagonal rain rods. Materials are red clay, oxidized copper, turquoise cord, and storm-gray glass. The turquoise banner carries a white spiral inside a red hexagonal frame.

Roaring Forum is an open amphitheater surrounded by copper lightning towers and built around a cloud well. Companies muster in debating circles. When a decision is reached, copper shutters snap open and every circle straightens into a column beneath one rolling thunderclap.

Names use open vowels, weather roots, and civic titles: Sola Bront, Avo, Neme; Roaring Forum, Cloudstep, Raincourt; Rainneedler, Chair of Vats.

## 4. Rivalries, and simultaneous-turn drama

The Crown and Clans dispute whether unity should be preserved in institutions or renewed through shared danger. Crown roads create surprise reinforcement; Clan mobile recruitment creates surprise pressure. Their drama is the gate each chooses before resolution.

The Compact and Assembly have an immediate mechanical rivalry. Glasswake wants to occupy the province now and defend it next turn. Underweather wants to collide during movement, before terrain and Settled Formation apply. Both can see the destination; neither knows whether the other will stop, reinforce, or intercept.

The Orchard and Concord disagree about belonging. Orchard citizenship can outlive bodies for centuries. Concord membership must be renewed by living households. Both value continuity, but one stores voices while the other keeps doors open.

Simultaneous resolution turns each rivalry into intersecting intent. The report can say: “The Compact held Vey Basin and gained Settled Formation. The Assembly arrived this turn, but this was not a meeting because the Compact was already present.” Or: “Both armies entered Warmdoor this turn. No side defended; Breaking Weather applied.” These are dramatic reveals made from documented rules, not hidden exceptions.

## 5. Ascension sites

**First Clauses** are provinces where unfinished fragments of the First Accord entered the ground. Each contains a rule reality has begun obeying but never completed.

The Bridge of Two Dawns has one end in morning and one in evening. The Speaking Crater makes titles briefly visible as colored shapes. The Upward Stair descends into the earth while emerging higher in the sky. The Unclosed Gate casts a doorway-shaped shadow even at noon.

Each site has a unique vertical silhouette and a pale ribbon linking it to the Oathscar overhead. The surrounding terrain remains mechanically ordinary and is shown on its province card. Sites do not restore ruler power; that would erase the ruler-budget tradeoff.

Rulers want First Clauses because only they can carry a final mandate beyond one nation. Holding enough begins a clearly displayed ascension count. The site model gains a small material overlay from its holder—glass facets, briars, embers, copper cloud rings—while player ownership remains a code-drawn ring and emblem, never faction color.

## 6. Magic school seeds

**Witness** governs sight, truth, forecasts, and revealing concealed armies. Battle spells expose defense or improve accuracy.

**Memory** wakes old protections in roads, forts, graves, and landmarks. Province rituals improve healing, defense, or income for a stated duration.

**Revision** temporarily alters a fighter’s attack, defense, morale, or trait application. It never reverses a locked transformation.

**Chorus** governs command, morale, healing, and coordinated movement. Its strongest battle spells require a standing commander.

**Severance** weakens a mandate, province ability, or magical effect for a visible duration. It never permanently removes a purchased mandate.

**Storm** governs wind, heat, rain, and pressure. It supports ranged attacks and siege preparation without changing the documented resolution order.

Every ritual shows cost, target, duration, and resolution timing. Every battle spell appears in the pre-battle order and the report names the statistic it changed.

## 7. Tone and lookalike risks

The Oathscar is dark because sincere commitments can consume the people who make them. A fair kingdom can turn citizens into living law. Shared pain can become hunger. Ancestors can become military resources. Hospitality can harden into annexation. None of that makes the world hopeless: ordinary Hearth Leagues matter, every faction contains generosity, and transformations offer real beauty alongside loss.

The Aurel Crown remains at risk of generic human-kingdom imagery; its civic distribution of supernatural authority must stay more prominent than royal destiny. Thornmane remains near familiar beastmen; mixed species, kin ethics, and wound management must carry it beyond barbarian shorthand. Glasswake could resemble aquatic crystal mystics, so accounting, surrendered memories, black frames, and prepared defense must remain central. Ember Orchard could resemble plant undead; consensual ancestral households and ceramic fruit bodies distinguish it. Hearthbound risks cozy halfling associations, so its people should have varied human proportions, severe roof-shields, and political hospitality rather than pastoral comedy. Underweather risks generic storm elementals; hollow civic vessels, argumentative formations, and collision-seeking play are the defense.

The cosmology risks resembling oath-based fantasy settings in the abstract. Its distinguishing feature is not that promises grant individual magic. Collective commitments physically become roads, institutions, bodies, and battlefield rules—and the strategy layer shows exactly which promise changed which number.
