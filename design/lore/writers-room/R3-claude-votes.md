# R3, Claude: red-team and votes

## 1. Red-team

**Short version.** The world image is earned. The faction layer is thinner than it reads: three of six faction rules hang on one binary (who defended), two rules sit dormant for most of a game, and all four new peoples are village trades. The merge credited my seat for the look and names, left the rules to the seats that wrote them, and then counted that as "three of four peoples".

### Engine fit

1. **One axis, not six last clicks.** Wild Ground, Breaking Weather and Already There are all statements about who defended. Threshold, Floodborn's flip and the Kilnborn's defence stack (Glazed, Firing, High Fire) pay the defender again, on top of §7.2 handing the defender the win at round 8. The synthesis promises "six different last clicks"; I count about three. Expect a turtle meta that Washout and Shock cannot crack alone.
2. **Breaking Weather is dormant.** If holds garrison the neutral provinces (§5 implies it) and "Holds always defend," it never fires early. Forts and Washout cancel it late. That leaves border swaps and meetings in the middle, and the Stormherd player cannot cause one alone. The synthesis never says how often it fires. Someone must count no-defender battles in replays before Alex sees this.
3. **The Free Holds carry too much.** Named Hold, Loose Terms, Sworn Vassals, Wick fuel, the Bellmother's early game, Wildkin and the Noonmark garrisons all draw on one finite pool, and Q5 (two attackers, one hold) is still open. Once the pool is empty Named Hold has no target, and the "transformation decides" fallback is +1 gold or cheaper regulars.
4. **Wick is under-specified.** Stopping in a fort only stops the burn, yet the pitch and Vigil say "go home and relight". Splitting off a one-unit army to farm relight fights against a hold is free. Rulers are armies, so does Half-Death gutter out? An enemy who "refuses battle" by leaving just loses the province.
5. **Golden Wake banks a gold coupon per fallen unit.** That is the second currency §3 swears off, plus the bookkeeping the brief calls busywork. Make it flat: recruits cost 3 less after a win.
6. **Named Hold pays gold for conquest** while the pitch says the Concord talk holds out of fighting. The Hearth school's seed spell then repeats Named Hold as a ritual.

### Approachability

Crown, Clans and Candlewake pass in two sentences (Candlewake is the best pitch in the document). **Stormherd fails:** "nobody holds the ground" is a state players do not see and cannot cause. **Kilnborn** passes with a dangling clause ("one firing per coming of age" has no rule behind it) and a rule name that says arrival while the effect says defence. **Concord** passes, but its gold payoff is not in its pitch. The glossary runs to roughly 70 lines against rule 7, and the "cut fort types to save terms" saved four.

### Tone

"Dark but not grim" fails on range: candles, sheep, pots and a dinner party. Four new peoples, four domestic trades, with menace carried by the Candlewake and the Clans alone. Three endings are "freeze it" (noon forever, baked unchanging, golden hour forever), and the Held Sun already is the golden hour, so a Concord win changes nothing on screen. Palette: yellow oilskins sit on P3 Gold, marigold likewise, terracotta on P1 Vermilion, teal near Jade and Sky. The art-lead flag covers one clash of five.

### Lookalikes

- **Halfmade:** Felix Gilman's *The Half-Made World* (2010), same words and the same unfinished-world premise. It is my name and I missed it in R1.
- **Guest Right:** George R. R. Martin's term, near-exact.
- **Weatherwife:** one syllable from Granny Weatherwax, and shepherds-plus-weather is Pratchett's Tiffany Aching ground.
- **Cosmology:** power made by many believers is Dominions' own premise, which the brief says not to copy. Mild, since it is lore only. Quick also echoes Highlander's Quickening.
- **Kilnborn** sits near Pratchett's clay golems and Warcraft's Earthen. The model guard is the right defence. The Candlewake guards hold.

### Fairness of the merge

Half earned, half home bias.

**Earned.** All three seats converged on the same four slots (gentle dead, weather, ceramic, hospitality), and the merge was not gentle with me. The Wright, Grazing, Kilnwork, Unbreaking, Rekindle and the Revel are cut. Stormherd and Kilnborn keep none of the rule I wrote.

**Bias.**
- **I lost rules; the others lost looks and worlds.** Everything a player meets first (pitch, look, names, three capitals) is mine. Codex lost its vocabulary and three peoples, and Grok lost its world and most of its peoples.
- **Every three-way duplicate went my way.** Gentle dead (Candlewake over the Long File and Ember Orchard), storm (Stormherd over the Underweather Assembly) and ceramic (Kilnborn over Glasswake, Coilwrights and Pans). The stated reason each time is silhouette, the part I am best at and the part least tested by play.
- **The brief ranks what a faction does to your turn above how it looks.** On that measure Codex and Grok own the Stormherd, Kilnborn and Concord rules. "Three of four peoples" counts skin.
- **Common-mode taste.** Three models agreeing on cosy tropes is not proof these are the best four, and an editor of my own family will read my prose as "the world image".
- **The ballot is narrow.** Q2 offers only same-role swaps (Pans, Kite Houses). The synthesis calls the Lacquer Reed the best unused people in the room and does not put it on the ballot.

**What deserved a place.** Grok's Lacquer Reed (judged only from the synthesis's one line, hollow marsh musicians: eerie, a new terrain, the opposite of the domestic four), and Grok's industrial and mercantile voice (Brass Toll, Pans), since the final six have no city or trade-road people at all. From Codex, the Underweather Assembly deserved a rules-level tie-break: Breaking Weather is already its work, so the Stormherd is now Codex's engine under my coat. Codex's Concord surviving is fair, and the Oathscar mechanism is the load-bearing cosmology.

## 3. My one change

**Rewrite Breaking Weather as "Stormherd regular units have +2 attack in any battle where they are not the defender", and let Floodborn flip it to "when they defend".**

- **Why.** The current trigger is dormant against holds (early), cancelled by forts (late), and cannot be caused by the Stormherd alone.
- **What it fixes.** The rule now fires the first time the herd marches. Thunderborn (two provinces a turn) and Floodborn (stand and heal) become true mirror forks. The Stormherd and the Kilnborn become a clean pair, the herd that fights when it moves against the kiln that fights when it stands. Washout stays the herd's answer to a fort.
- **What it costs.** The blind "will they come?" bet leaves the Stormherd and stays with the Kilnborn. I think that trade is right, because a rule that fires is worth more than a drama that rarely happens.
