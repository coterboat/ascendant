# R3 synthesis: Halfmade

Status: **PROPOSED**. Nothing here is approved until Alex signs it off.

Credits name the seat, as in (Claude), (Codex, R1) or (Grok, full cut). **(R3 fix)** marks an editor's repair where the critiques agreed something broke. Sketches read hp/attack/defense/morale/cost, and design owns every number.

---

## 1. The starting ground in five sentences

1. The world is **Halfmade**, still being finished, and its sun has hung at golden hour (**the Held Sun**) for as long as anyone remembers, because every people pulls the day toward a different ending.
2. Its one power is **Quick**, the making not yet spent: gathered in one body it is a ruler's power, poured into a people it sets as a mandate, and pooled in the ground it marks the **Noonmarks**, the ascension sites where the day can be settled.
3. The Aurel Crown wants noon forever and the Thornmane Clans want night, and the four new peoples are the **Candlewake** (wax-lit dead who want death finished), the **Stormherd** (weather shepherds who want **the Washing**), the **Kilnborn** (fired-clay people who want **the Last Firing**) and the **Hearthbound Concord** (a hospitality federation that wants **the Long Supper**).
4. Each plays one rule: the Crown spends late at list price, the Clans attack into the woods, Candlewake armies burn down like candles, the Stormherd win when both sides march, the Kilnborn win where they arrived first, and the Concord name one of the **Free Holds** each turn (the local peoples who want no ending at all).
5. The world image and three of the new peoples come from Claude, the mechanism and the Concord from Codex, and most of the rules that keep it inside the engine from Grok, while the absent maker, the oaths and the cord sky are cut for lookalike risk.

---

## 2. The world, and what ascension is

Halfmade's unfinished world (Claude) runs on the Oathscar's mechanism (Codex): things become real when many people pour themselves into them.

**(R3 fix)** The Wright, Claude's maker who walked out, is cut, because Codex and Grok both read her as Dominions' vanished Pantokrator. Nobody left. The peoples simply want different things.

- **The world.** Living will does the making: generations walking one way settle a road, and generations obeying one judge settle a crown (Codex). Death and the end of the day never settled (Claude).
- **Ascension.** Hold enough Noonmarks and your ruler settles the day. The world is finished in your people's image, and the sun moves at last or stays by choice. The sky tints toward the leading ending, never toward a player colour (Claude; adopted by Codex).
- **The budget.** A ruler is where a people's Quick gathers in one body. Quick held cannot also be poured (Claude, Grok), and one body holds ten.
- **A mandate.** Quick poured into a people, set as a law of their nature. Under Iron Discipline, a Crown soldier cannot run while his captain stands (Claude).
- **Why a mid-game sacrifice costs +1.** An established self resists division (Codex, R1). The Crown's Charter is the exception (Claude).
- **Why mandates outlive the ruler.** Poured Quick lives in drill, tools, roads and bodies (all three seats).
- **Why the kinds return differently.**
  - A mortal body cracks: half the kept Quick spills, and the heir catches the rest.
  - The undying bound theirs into an object such as the sunstone: nothing spills, but re-forming is slow.
  - A god is Quick with its own shape. Each death, some of it settles into the world as hills and weather for good (−3), and the rest re-gathers in a turn (Claude; Codex's "the idea grows smaller").
- **Why transformations lock in.** A people can be finished only one way (Claude, Codex). Each transformation closes one mandate for good (Grok, R1). Its preview shows the army block, a province card and the fortress (Codex).
- **A faction rule.** A **faction rule** is the one thing about a people that settled before any ruler, said in one sentence. It is Claude's Birthright under a plain name, kept to one per faction as Grok insisted.

## 3. Sources of power

There is one power in three places, and the places get no extra names (Grok, R2: "one cord, three seats"; Codex, R2: "one force with three states"):
- **In a ruler:** ruler power.
- **In a people:** mandates, the faction rule and transformations.
- **In the ground:** the Noonmarks. They pay research but never ruler power, so the pool stays 10 (Grok, Codex).

Gold is ordinary wealth. Research is learning to shape Quick without spilling it. Magic is a little of the making done by hand (Claude). Nothing is gathered as a second currency (Codex), and no spell falsifies a forecast (Codex, Grok).

## 4. The six factions

### 4.1 The Aurel Crown (built)

**Pitch.** Ordinary humans made extraordinary because their king pours his power into them, and they want the sun nailed at noon. They watch the enemy's build, then buy exactly the laws the war needs at the printed price.

**Rule: Charter** (Claude; adopted by Grok). The Crown's mid-game sacrifices cost no +1, because its laws are read aloud at Crownkeep (Grok, R2).

**Your turn.** Spend now, or keep Corvane strong enough to survive the next battle? A king who waits can die before he spends (Grok, R2). King's Roads makes the last click "stop on my side of the line" (Grok, full cut).

**Rulers and roster.** Unchanged:
- **Rulers:** King Corvane (mortal line, front, Royal Court) and the Ever-Regent (undying, second rank, Mending Light). The sunstone is a chip of noon (Claude).
- **Roster:** Spearmen (Shield Wall), Crossbowmen (Volley), Knights (Charge) and the Knight Captain (Inspire).

**Transformations** (repo M4, found by Grok). The blue plume survives both.
- **Celestial Host:** white-gold armour with a ring of light. Spearmen become **Sunspears**, and wounded units heal a little in your own provinces. Closes Heavy Tithes.
- **Living Iron:** riveted iron with orange seams. **Iron Knights** and an **Iron Colossus** join, owned hills pay more, and forts heal. Closes King's Roads.

**Look** (Claude, Codex).
- **Army:** tidy rectangles and spear forests in steel, white linen, royal blue and gold. The banner is unchanged.
- **Fortress:** Crownkeep is concentric limestone with blue cone roofs. The sunstone burns in a glass lantern-tower, the map's first beacon.
- **Muster:** blocks drill on gold lines, merge into one column, and the gate drops.

**Naming.** Short court names (Corvane, Edrin, Merrow), places ending in -keep, -march and -watch, and virtues as epithets (Claude, Codex). No griffons, steam tanks or warrior priests (Grok, full cut).

### 4.2 The Thornmane Clans (built)

**Pitch.** Proud beastfolk tribes who fight best when hurt and want the sun finally to set. They attack into forest and hills, where the land fights for them from either side.

**Rule: Wild Ground** (Claude; narrowed by Grok, R2). When the Clans attack in forest or hills, they also get that terrain's defender bonus. Border battles and meetings have no defender, so it adds nothing there.

**Your turn.** Attack into the wood and refuse the open plain in front of crossbows. Stay wounded for Berserk and Blood Frenzy, or cash a win into Feast of Victory (Codex, Grok). The Fang's province is a second barracks.

**Rulers and roster.** Unchanged:
- **Rulers:** the Great Thornmane (god, front, Dread) and the Ninth Fang (mortal line, front, Warband Muster).
- **Roster:** Tuskers (Berserk), Fangthrowers (Skirmish), the War Bear (Fury) and the Warchief (Pack Leader).

**Transformations** (repo M4). The crimson survives both.
- **Thorn Wild:** briar over the same bodies. The bear stays a bear, and nobody becomes a tree. Owned forests pay +2 gold, and their defenders get +1 more defense (Grok, R2). Closes Blood Frenzy.
- **Blood Pact:** bone darkens. Recruits cost 2 less and arrive 2 hp down, so Berserk wakes at once (Grok, R2; Claude). Closes Feast of Victory.

**Look.**
- **Army:** hunched, horned clumps that never hold a line, with bears looming. The banner is unchanged.
- **Fortress:** Fangridge is a ring of giant living briars around a hill crowned by a colossal horned skull (Claude).
- **Muster:** kin-bands gather round separate fires, then pour downhill as one wave when the briars part (Codex).

**Naming.** Kennings and deed-names (Splits-the-Oak, Red-Muzzle) and hard roots (Rakka, Ruk, Salla) (all three seats). To stay clear of Warhammer's Beastmen: boar, wolf, lion and bear, never goat; no herdstones and no "gor" words (Claude, Grok).

### 4.3 The Candlewake (Claude)

**Pitch.** Death was never finished, so the dead stay, lit in bodies of candle wax, and march politely to finish it so there is somewhere to rest. Every Candlewake army burns down like a candle: fight, or go home and relight.

**Rule: Wick** (Claude, R2). Each Candlewake army has a wick of 6, shown on its banner. It burns 1 each turn the army neither fights nor ends in a fort you own. Any battle relights it to 6, and at 0 the army gutters out and is lost.

**Your turn.** Every field army is a clock: "The Second Procession has 2 turns left. Fight, or go home." Free Holds are fuel early and rivals late. Enemies win by refusing battle, so the turns become a chase (Claude; praised by Codex).

**(R3 fix).** Both of these follow Codex and Grok.
- **Unbreaking** (never rout) is cut. A defender that cannot break holds every battle to round 8, and Iron Discipline already covers it.
- **Rekindle** is cut, because the losing side is destroyed, not revived.

**Rulers.**
- **Half-Death** (god, front rank): death's half-made god, with half its face alabaster and half a glowing outline. No antlers, and no green and black (Claude's Hela guard). **Gleaning:** after it wins a battle in its province, 1/2/3/4 Wickwardens join your army for free (Claude). **(R3 fix)** It never raises enemy units.
- **The Chandler** (mortal line, second rank): the candlemaker who keeps the dead. **Vigil:** your armies in her province relight to 7/8/9/10 (Claude).

**Transformations.** The lilac survives both.
- **Golden Wake:** beeswax and hives. The wick runs to 10, and after a win each fallen regular unit takes 3 gold off its next recruit at your capital. This is Codex's Aftercrop, banked at the capital as in Grok's Aftercount. Closes Muster Grounds.

Closes Iron Discipline.

**Look** (Claude).
- **Army:** a flame on every crown, so a block reads as glowing dots and a march as a dotted line.
- **Materials:** ivory tallow for the light area, soot-black wicks and armour for the dark, brass snuffers and lilac ribbons.
- **Banner:** lilac, with a soot border, a drip-cut hem, and an ivory candle ringed by flames.
- **Fortress:** forts are stepped towers of melted candles. The capital, **the Long Vigil**, is a hill of old wax around one candle as tall as a keep.
- **Muster:** processions file out, each carrying its flame.
- **Lookalike guards:** people with a flame on the crown, never candlesticks with faces (Codex: Litwick). The theme is death unfinished, not fire fading (Grok: Dark Souls). The Candlekeep echo goes to the vote (Grok).

**Naming.** Epitaphs as names (Mother Tallow, Kept-Till-Morning, Small Ember), and places ending in -wake and -vigil (Claude).

### 4.4 The Stormherd (Claude's people, Codex's and Grok's rule)

**Pitch.** Gruff, funny nomads in yellow oilskins who herd living weather across the high fells and want the Washing, one rain to scrub the world and start the morning over. When both armies march and nobody holds the ground, the storm breaks their way.

**Rule: Breaking Weather** (Codex, R2). Stormherd regular units have +2 attack in battles where no side is the defender. The +2 is the felt size of Grok's Named Wind, and the target is the one Codex's Appointment aimed at.

**Your turn.** You order where you think the enemy will march, and put a herd across their road. The forecast shows both lines: if they come, and if they don't. Sitting still denies the bonus, so the herd threatens whatever the enemy must move to save. The Crown's two-province road march is the move they want to meet (Grok, full cut). Holds always defend, so the Bellmother carries the early game.

**(R3 fix)**
- **Grazing** (every army earns the income where it stands) is cut as a second economy (Codex, Grok). It survives as the Bellmother's ability.
- Codex's Underweather Assembly is cut as this faction's double, as all three seats flagged.

**Rulers.**
- **Grandsire Thunder** (god, front rank): a living thunderhead shaped like an old ram. **Cloudburst:** in battles in its province, enemy fighters have −1/1/2/2 attack. **(R3 fix)** Claude's version taxed the neighbouring provinces, and Grok noted that abilities act on the ruler's own province.
- **The Bellmother** (mortal line, front rank): the chief shepherd with the great brass bell. Her heirs are counted Yan, Tan, Tethera. **Shearing:** the province she stands in pays you +2/3/4/5 gold, owned or not (Claude, R1).

|---|---|---|
| **Crooks** (infantry) | 9/9/5/8/7 | **Gathering:** +1 attack each round after the first, up to +3. |
| **Thundersheep** (beast) | 12/11/4/7/10 | **Shock:** treats its target's defense as 4 lower. |
| **Thunder Ram** (monster) | 22/13/8/9/21 | **Thunderclap:** in the first round, enemy front-rank fighters have −2 attack (R3 fix: the old version tracked one target, per Codex). |
| **Weatherwife** (commander) | 24/11/9/11/32 | **Washout:** while it stands, no side gets terrain or fort bonuses. It is the herd's answer to a fort, and design may narrow it to forts. |

**Transformations.** The yellow survives both.
- **Thunderborn:** the herds go up. Thundersheep become **Stormlings** with Skirmish, the reach Grok's Rake wanted, using a trait that already exists. Armies move two provinces a turn anywhere, and regulars have −2 hp (Claude). Closes Veteran Levies.
- **Floodborn:** the herds go down into the rivers. Armies that did not move heal 3 hp a turn, and Breaking Weather flips to pay when you defend (the flip Grok gave its Staked Houses). Fort-washing waits for sieges (Grok). Closes Sworn Vassals.

**Look** (Claude).
- **Army:** rolling cloud-blobs dotted with yellow oilskins, rams like small thunderheads, and cyan lightning visible at any distance.
- **Materials:** slate cumulus wool, oilskin, brass bells and drystone.
- **Banner:** yellow, with a slate border and a storm-cloud ram's head with a cyan fork.
- **Fortress:** the capital, **the High Fold** (R3 name), is concentric drystone sheepfolds on a fell, under one huge storm cloud on chains that flickers like a beacon.
- **Muster:** the flock waits penned in the rings, then floods downhill like weather breaking.
- **Lookalike guard:** slate-grey, black-faced sheep, never fluffy and yellow (Claude, Codex: Mareep).

**Naming.** Northern hill-farm words plus weather (Gimmer Rattlecloud, Old Tup Hailstone), with sheep-count ordinals (Claude).

### 4.5 The Kilnborn (Claude's people, Grok's rule and model sheet)

**Pitch.** Clay people who fire their own bodies, one firing per coming of age, and want the Last Firing: the world baked so it never cracks or changes again. On ground they reached first, they do not break.

**Rule: Already There** (Grok, R2; it is what Codex's Glasswake aimed at). When the Kilnborn defend, their side's morale counts 2 higher. Meetings and border battles give nothing.

**Your turn.** Race to be standing on the ground before the enemy, then refuse to step out. Every order asks: will they come to me, or will we both arrive? Their brittle, high-defense units win long battles, so they love forts and fear Washout and Shock (Claude). They are natural Noonmark holders.

**(R3 fix)**
- **Kilnwork** (build a fort anywhere in 2 turns) is cut as a construction system (Codex, Grok).
- Every seat brought a ceramic people, and this is the one that stays.

**Rulers.**
- **The Kiln-Mothers** (undying, second rank): three elder potters who fight as one body and are re-fired at the Great Kiln (Claude, R2). **Refire:** one regular unit of your choice in their province heals 4/8/12/16 hp a turn. Mending Light heals a stack a little; this saves one veteran. It comes from Grok's First Firing (full cut) and replaces Hot Kiln, which needed Kilnwork.
- **The Unfired** (god, front rank): the first clay that ever stood up, a soft giant who refused every kiln. **First Shape:** units recruited in its province arrive with +1/2/3/4 maximum hp. This is Claude's Thumbprint renamed; the thumbprint image is dropped on Grok's lookalike flag.

**Transformations** (Claude). The turquoise survives both.

- **Muster:** a neat grid stands on a pale tile floor, then marches out through the kiln mouth still glowing (Claude, Grok).

**Naming.** Short stressed names with a glaze for a surname (Hest Turquoise, Calda Celadon) and kiln places (Kilnhollow, Saggerstead, Redmouth) (Claude, Grok). To stay clear of dwarves: no runes, no grudges, no mountain halls (Grok).

### 4.6 The Hearthbound Concord (Codex's people, Grok's rule)

**Pitch.** A travelling federation of cooks, bridge-keepers and household militias who say nobody should rule a place without first sitting at its table, and want the Long Supper: the golden hour kept for good, with a seat for every hold. Each turn they name one Free Hold and talk it half out of fighting before they arrive.

**Rule: Named Hold** (Grok's Factor Houses, R2). Each turn, name one Free Hold next to your land. Its garrison fights you at −2 morale this turn, and if you take the province this turn, you gain 2 gold per garrison unit. It replaces Codex's Open Terms, whose −1 was too small to feel (Claude).

**Your turn.** Which hold, and now or next turn? The forecast shows the morale and the gold, never whether a rival is marching on the same hold. They expand fastest early. Holds don't grow back, so by mid-game the transformation decides what they become: Codex's branches answer Claude's warning about Grok's Open Knot.

**Rulers.**
- **First Host Nella** (mortal line, second rank). **Guest Right:** a province you first took within the last two turns earns +2/3/4/5 gold while she is there, so she rides the frontier (Codex).
- **The Banked Welcome** (undying, front rank): a figure built from the coals of thousands of accepted hearths and re-formed at the common oven (Codex). **Loose Terms:** every 4/3/2/2 turns, one Free Hold next to its province joins you without a battle (Grok's Walking Seal).

| unit | sketch (Grok's Factor stats) | trait (Codex) |
|---|---|---|
| **Doorwardens** (infantry) | 10/9/8/8/10 | **Threshold:** +2 defense while its side is the defender. |
| **Road Slingers** (infantry) | 7/11/4/7/8 | **Waystone:** +2 attack when fighting outside land you owned at the start of the turn. |
| **Table Beasts** (beast) | 16/12/6/7/16 | **Fed Well:** +2 defense while a commander on its side stands (R3 fix: Codex's +4 maximum hp changed hp mid-battle, per Claude and Grok). |
| **Pact Herald** (commander) | 18/9/8/12/32 | **Known Terms:** enemy morale penalties on its side are 1 smaller. |

**Transformations** (Codex). The marigold survives both.
- **Great Household:** the federation hardens into law. Owned provinces earn +1 gold, and Doorwardens get +1 defense under cream plaster plates and copper roof-helmets. Closes Sworn Vassals: no more holds are admitted.
- **Open Road:** travel becomes the people. Regulars cost 2 less and have −1 defense, Road Slingers become mounted **Cart Slingers**, and buildings become timber waystations under marigold awnings. Closes Heavy Tithes (R3).

**Look** (Codex).
- **Army:** a low, loose cluster of roof-shaped shields, handcarts and tall cooking-smoke standards, never a rectangle.
- **Materials:** dark teal wool for the dark area, cream plaster for the light, warm wood, copper pans and marigold cord.
- **Banner:** teal, with a cream doorway above a copper bowl.
- **Fortress:** the capital, **Commonroof**, is a many-courtyard hill town walled by linked houses.
- **Muster:** long outdoor tables fold into carts as the army leaves by several household gates.
- **Lookalike guards:** varied human proportions and severe roof-shields, not halfling comedy (Codex). The terms are always printed, so it is never a fae bargain (Grok).
- **Art-lead flag (R3):** keep the teal dark against the Kilnborn's bright turquoise.

**Naming.** Two-beat warm names (Nella, Oren, Tavi), household titles, and places named for doors, roofs and bridges (Commonroof, Warmdoor, Three Bridges) (Codex, Grok).

## 5. The Free Holds

The independents are built (#144). In Halfmade they are places that settled their own small things (fields, feasts and feuds) and want no one's ending: they like the afternoon (Claude, R2). They answer to no ruler and pour no mandates (Grok, R2), and they owe defense to neighbours, not to crowns (Codex's Hearth Leagues). That is why they hold high morale and never march, recruit or heal: a garrison is a village standing in its own fields.

Garrisons run 3 to 5 units by income:
- **Free Company** (plains): **Free Spears** (Shield Wall) and **Hill Slingers** (Skirmish).
- **Hillguard** (hills): the same two kinds, slingers first.
- **Wildkin** (forest): beastfolk who refused the Thornmane god (Grok, R1), with slingers. They share Berserk with the Tuskers, so they need their own look: beasts the Clans don't count (badger, elk, hare), fern hoods and bark coats, and no crimson, bone or thorn (R3, after Grok's full cut).
- **Hold Captain:** leads a hold with income 8 or more. It has no trait.

**Look.** A short mixed block in undyed wool under each hold's own home-stitched sign, never a faction silhouette (Grok, full cut). The slate heater stays their code mark.

**How every faction meets them.**
- Sworn Vassals is a hold accepting your ending.
- The Concord name holds, and the Candlewake fight them to relight.
- The Clans meet their Wildkin cousins in every forest.
- Most Noonmarks sit under a rich hold (Grok).
- The Long Supper is the ending closest to what holds already have, and they still fight the Concord: a table you didn't set is someone else's table.
- Once independents are recruitable (packet §7.3), a taken hold should offer its local unit (Grok, full cut).

## 6. Rivalries

There are three old quarrels. Each is a clash of endings and a different bet on how orders collide.

- **Noon against Night: the Crown and the Clans** (Claude). Both can order the same wood. If the Crown already holds it, both get the forest bonus: the Crown as defender, the Clans through Wild Ground. If they cross into it on the same turn, nobody does, and the report says which (Grok, R2). Roads bring the Crown surprise reinforcements; the Fang brings the Clans surprise recruits (Codex).
- **The Kiln against the Storm: the Kilnborn and the Stormherd.** This is Claude's Fire against Rain, built on Grok's arrive-first-against-meeting pairing, which Codex also took up.
  - The Kilnborn want to have arrived last turn, and the Stormherd want both armies to arrive now: "Both armies arrived this turn. No one held the ground: the weather broke, and the kiln was not set."
  - Floodborn makes the herd a holder and Slip makes the kiln a wanderer, so one lock-in can swap the rivalry's sides.
- **The Supper and the Wake: the Concord and the Candlewake.** This is Codex's quarrel over belonging, first written for its Ember Orchard.
  - The Long Supper keeps the golden hour forever, which leaves death unfinished forever: the Concord's best ending is the Candlewake's worst.
  - Both want holds, one to take and one to relight against: "You named Warmdoor. The Second Procession, one turn from guttering out, came to relight there too."

**Across the pairs.** Blood's curse dulls one of a Charter Crown's late mandates. The Pact Herald blunts Fury and Dread. Stormherd herds hunt the Crown's road marches.

**Why it's drama.** Simultaneous turns make every faction bet blind on something nobody sees until resolution (Claude), so one map has six different last clicks (Grok, full cut). The report is the reveal, and it names the rule that decided it: each outcome was a read, not a dice roll.

## 7. Noonmarks

A Noonmark is where the making stopped mid-stroke: the land runs out into glowing outline and flat raw colour, as if the paint stopped (Claude). Each one rises in a single tall silhouette with a pale ribbon of light toward the held sun (Codex).

**One rule, so a new player learns one sentence** (Grok).
- Hold enough Noonmarks to settle the day and win.
- Each one held pays +2 research a turn and never adds ruler power (Grok, Codex).
- The ground under it keeps its terrain, income and defender bonus (Codex, Grok).
- Most sit under a rich hold, so the first grab is a garrison fight. As a sketch, a small map has two and a six-player map four (Grok).

**On the map.**
- **Holder's materials:** a held Noonmark takes on its holder's materials (wax, briar, glaze, oilskin, copper or gilt), while ownership stays the code-drawn ring (Codex).
- **Shared muster:** any army mustering there forms a ring around it before spilling out in its own shape. This one shared animation says ascension is the same act for everyone (Grok, full cut).

**Seeds.**
- **The Halfway Mountain** (Claude): a peak that ends in mid-air as glowing lines.
- **The Pouring** (Claude): a waterfall out of a hole in the sky.
- **The Sun-Nail** (Claude): a golden nail the size of a tower, meant to pin the sun. It is the Crown's holy ground.
- **The Blank** (Claude): a white field where nothing is decided yet.
- **The Bridge of Two Dawns** (Codex): one end stands in morning, the other in evening.
- **The Upward Stair** (Codex): a stair that goes down into the earth and comes out higher in the sky.

## 8. Magic school seeds

Magic is the making done by hand (Claude). There are six schools:
- the repo's proposed **Sun**, **Law**, **Wild** and **Blood**, found by Grok and adopted by Claude
- **Weather** and **Hearth** (R3)

How they connect:
- **Pairs:** the schools come in opposed pairs (Claude's structure), and each pair mirrors a rivalry (R3).

## 10. What was left out and why

**Claude (Halfmade)**
- **The Wright.** Codex and Grok read her as the Pantokrator (Q1).
- **"The Long Afternoon" as a name.** It echoes the US title of Aldiss's *Hothouse* (R3 flag). The image stays.
- **Birthright and Becoming as words.** They are second names for UI words: Claude's own rule-7 charge against Grok, applied evenly.
- **The Revel.** It is a subsystem, and it sits on Majora's Mask, Slaanesh and the Faceless Men (Codex, Grok; Claude's own weakest idea). It is parked (Q4).
- **Engine fixes.** Grazing, Kilnwork, Unbreaking, Rekindle, fort-washing and Thorn Wild's spreading forest are each fixed or cut in §4.
- **Per-site Noonmark powers and the Cold Kiln** (Q3).
- **Painting**, the illusion craft. Magic never lies about a forecast.
- **Named fort types** (wakehouse, fold, kilnhold, pavilion). They are cut to save terms.

**Codex (The Oathscar)**
- **Oathscar, Accord, Crownfire, Deep Memory, Living Change and First Clauses.** Oaths that grant power sit on Stormlight (Claude, Grok), and six terms arrive before any faction does. The mechanism and two site images stay.
- **The Glasswake Compact.** It is part of the glass cluster, and it reads as Zora or Dominions' undersea nations (Claude, Grok). Its role went to the Kilnborn.
- **The Ember Orchard.** It reads as Sylvaneth soulpods (Claude, Grok). Aftercrop and its quarrel over belonging live on.
- **The Underweather Assembly.** It was the second storm people. Breaking Weather and Call to Order live on.
- **Common Crown, Iron Testament, Shared Briar and Red Hunger.** The repo's forks win, though Common Crown's civic collar-rings would suit a later Crown fork.
- **Revision as a school.** Every spell changes a number. Witness, Memory, Chorus, Severance and Storm are folded into §8.
- **Open Terms and Warm Hall.** Open Terms was too small to feel, and Warm Hall doubled Already There.

**Grok (The Strung)**
- **The cord sky and its words.** It reads as fate-weaving, Wheel of Time's Pattern and *Loom* (Claude, Codex), and Grok's own factions didn't use it. Kept: one power in three places, and every sag-yard rule.
- **The Verdigris Pans.** They fill the Kilnborn's role. They are the named fallback (Q2).
- **The Kite Houses.** The collision role went to the Stormherd, which needs no flyer tag. Named Wind's +2 and the Staked Houses' flip live on.
- **The Long File.** It was a third gentle-dead people. Aftercount lives in Golden Wake.
- **The Factor Houses.** The body went to the Concord. Pulled Thread's Unpick and Ink Fee are parked, and the anti-mandate counter lives in Blood.
- **From the full cut.**
  - The Coilwrights are merged into the Kilnborn.
  - **The Lacquer Reed** (hollow marsh musicians) is the best unused people in the room.
  - The Brass Toll's Open Splice is covered by the Concord.
  - The Glass Wake's Wrack gold and Splinter are parked.
  - The Crown's third fork, Red Splice (the bible's bloodpact look), waits for M4.
