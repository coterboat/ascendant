# R1, Grok: The Strung

## 1. The world premise, and what ascension is

The world is **the Strung**: land under a sagging sky of dyed cord. That sky is the **canopy**, cloth you can snag a wheel on. Cords pool in valleys, catch on ridges and tangle in trees. People are loose cord that learned to walk. A ruler holds a **shuttle**, the right to pass cord through a whole people.

Ascension ties that shuttle into the **next band**, the unwoven canopy above this one. Whoever ties enough of it sets the roads and the recruits of the next age. Godhood and empire are different knots in the same band.

A ruler starts with ten **lengths**. A length is one point of ruler power. The same cord cannot sit in two places. Lengths you keep wrap your body: harder hits, more hp, a thicker gift on one province. Lengths you spend run through every person as a **standing stitch**, a mandate. Recruits are woven from the current cloth, so they arrive already stitched. A stitch outlives the weaver, because it lives in the people. That is why mandates survive a death.

Spending more mid-war is a **splice**, a mid-game sacrifice. Opening finished cloth frays one extra length. The game shows the cloth before and after.

Rulers return by their knot. A **mortal line** is blood or office in an ordinary body: the heir takes half the kept lengths and two turns to sit. An **undying** ruler is knotted to an object: a new body climbs that cord in about four turns, and comes back whole. A **god** is canopy wearing a body: kill it and a new body is tied next turn, permanently shorter.

A transformation is a **recut**. Research dyes cloth. A recut changes which pattern the loom can still make, and the other pattern is gone. Until the cut, the cord could go either way. Before the cut you get a **swatch**, the lock-in preview: the finished people, the recruits you lose, and the new look.

One mismatch. The packet says the Crown and Clans forks come later. Proposed M4 already names Celestial Host, Living Iron, Thorn Wild and Blood Pact, with Sun, Law, the Wild and Blood. This pitch follows the repo.

The Free Holds keep those rules names. Here they are the **Unwoven**: cord tied to a province, not a shuttle. No mandates. A **Hold Captain** leads a richer garrison, holds a line, and cannot stitch a people. You take the land by beating them. Plains **Free Company**, spears and slings, **Loose Rank** (+2 defense while another stands). Hills **Hillguard**, **Long Sling** (while defending hills, they may hit the second rank). Forest **Wildkin**, beastfolk outside the Thornmane braid, **Unbraided** (+2 attack while defending a forest). **Local Standard**: +2 garrison morale while the captain stands. Sketch: one fighter per 4 income, a captain from 8. Sworn Vassals invites a province in with no battle.

## 2. The source of power

One source. The canopy sheds **live cord** where it frays: colour that still moves, and a body that wears it can hold more shape than flesh usually can. Ten lengths is as much as one shuttle can carry. Provinces store it as noon-cord in the sunstone, thorn-cord in the deep wood, salt-glass on the pans, wind-cord in the kite masts, amber-cord in the bowl, and slack cord in every Free Hold. Magic pulls a cord you are not touching. Research teaches a new weave. Gold is dye, food and metal.

## 3. The six factions

### The Aurel Crown

The Crown knotted a fallen noon-cord around the sunstone at Crownkeep. Ranks should read as one shape: steel, white linen, royal blue, gold. You play them by keeping a formation chosen early.

The turn is ranks and interior lines. Spearmen in front, crossbows behind, knights for the first-round charge. King's Roads shifts an army two provinces inside your land, so the choice is often which of your own gates spills. Royal Armories (+1 attack on regulars) makes the nation sharp because the ruler stayed plain. King Corvane, mortal line, front rank: keep his lengths and he rides as a paladin; spend them and an ordinary man fields the best kit in the land. Royal Court adds gold where he sits, so parking him is a treasury choice. The Ever-Regent, undying, second rank, sunstone staff: Mending Light heals the wounded in his province, more if he kept lengths, and he re-forms in four turns at full lengths because the knot is the stone. One army wants the hospital. The other wants the road.

Roster, shipped: Spearmen (Shield Wall), Crossbowmen (Volley), Knights (Charge), Knight Captain (Inspire).

M4 recuts. The **Celestial Host**: Spearmen become Sunspears, Seraphs dive at commanders, the capital takes a Tithe of Light, Hallowed Land heals 3 hp a turn on your ground. Blue plume stays; armour goes white-gold with a ring of cord-light. Forswears Heavy Tithes. **Living Iron**: Knights become Iron Knights, an Iron Colossus joins them, hills pay Deep Mines, forts heal you to full. Blue plume stays; plate becomes dark riveted iron with orange seams. Forswears King's Roads.

From above: a blue-and-white spear block, knights a wider slab of white horses, the captain's standard over the helmets. Crownkeep is a high pale keep and a gold sun-court. Muster is the inner yard, blocks on painted lines, one gate. Banner: royal blue, white border, three-point hem, gold crown over a rising sun. Names stay stone-and-sun: Corvane, Harl, Voss; Crownkeep, Stonegate, Rivermarch; Sunspear, Seraph, Iron Knight, Iron Colossus.

### The Thornmane Clans

Beastfolk who let cord grow fur, tusk and thorn. The Great Thornmane is a god-thread in a body of briar and fur. The Ninth Fang holds the braid when that body is spent. Wildkin are cousins who refused it. You play them by arriving hurt and being glad.

The turn is whether to stay wounded. Berserk and Blood Frenzy pay you for missing hp. Feast of Victory fills you after a win and turns that off. Low defense makes an open-plain volley fatal, so you want a forest (defender +2 defense) or a border fight, where nobody is the defender. The Fang's Warband Muster recruits one to four units where the Fang stands: the ruler is a second barracks, and a dead Fang pauses recruiting for the heir. The god stays a huge front-rank body, lays Dread, and returns next turn at −3 lengths forever. Keep the god and the nation is thin.

Roster, shipped: Tuskers (Berserk), Fangthrowers (Skirmish), War Bear (Fury), Warchief (Pack Leader).

M4 recuts. The **Thorn Wild**: Thornthrowers, rooted Briarwardens, more gold from forest, one owned province becomes forest each turn. Forswears Blood Frenzy. The **Blood Pact**: Bloodtuskers (fewer hp, more attack), Bloodsworn who drink hits back, cheaper recruits who arrive wounded, invaders on your land lose morale. Forswears Feast of Victory. Crimson paint stays either way.

From above: an uneven tawny mass, odd-angled clubs and javelins, a bear wider than the line. Fangridge is a thorn palisade on a ridge. Muster is the open slope, a wedge spilling downhill. Banner: ochre hide, crimson border, bone-hung torn edge, horned skull in green thorns. Names are short compounds: Ruk, Salla, the numbered Fangs; Fangridge, Thornvale, Bloodfen. Wildkin stay plainer: Moss, Bristle, Unn.

### The Glazewell Choir

Salt-glaziers of ordinary height: chalk, coral-pink trim, brass collars, glass from cord that crossed the pans. Late rounds are yours only if you were already on the ground.

**Ringing Ground**: while you defend, morale is figured 2 higher. A same-turn meeting has no defender, so the ring is silent and your low attack shows. The turn is whether you can be there first. **Still Rim**: +1 defense while defending.

Warden Pell, mortal line, front, bell-shield. **Glass Tithe**: Pell's province earns +2/3/4/5 gold by rank. Heir in two turns at half lengths. The Kept Note, undying, second rank, a voice in the well's brass rim. **Salt Seal**: heal 2/4/7/10 hp a turn in that province. Re-forms in four turns, full lengths. Keep Pell and the ruler is the wall. Keep the Note and one province is a hospital.

Sketches. Rimguard 11 hp, attack 7, defense 9, morale 10, cost 10. **Lock Rim**: +2 defense while defending. Chord Slinger 8, 11, 5, 8, cost 9. **Crack Pitch**: while one stands, every enemy side has −1 morale. Bellwalker 26, 9, 9, 8, cost 24. **Toll**: while it stands, your side must lose 10% more hp than usual before it breaks. Choir Marshal 24, 9, 13, 14, cost 35. **Hold the Note**: while the marshal stands, your fighters have +2 defense.

**Clear Rim**: clear shields, coral rims kept. New Prism (hp 14, attack 9, defense 9, morale 10, cost 18), **Glare**: while it stands, enemy hit chance against you is 8 percent lower. Plains earn +2 gold. An enemy ending a turn on your land loses 2 hp. **Struck Rim**: cracked bells, dark teal, coral kept. Rimguards take **Shard Wall** (+2 attack while defending). New Shard Choir (hp 8, attack 13, defense 5, morale 7, cost 12), **Shrapnel**: a hit also deals 1 damage to another front-rank enemy. Wins pay 1 gold per enemy lost. Forts add another +2 defense.

From above: pale discs, wider than Crown spears. Glazewell is a low glass ring around a well, on plains. Muster is the inner pan, circles of companies, one cut. Banner: chalk, coral border, a brass circle cut by a bar. Soft, short names: Pell, Sera Vell, Brin Holst; Pinkrim, Chordfen, Lowpan.

### The Kite Houses

Rival families on black reed and indigo silk, pulled by wind-cord. The wing is the mount. You want a meeting, and a fort eats the eight rounds you had.

**Open Sky**: with no defender, flyer hits deal +1 damage. **First Wind**: another +1 for flyers on round one. The turn is a hunt for the army that just left a wall.

Ribbon-Captain Oku, mortal line, front, on a kite. **Signal Mast**: recruit 1/2/3/4 lancers where Oku stands. Heir in two turns at half lengths. Kept lengths make that yard work, and a reed frame in the front dies fast. The High Cord, a god, second rank, will not land. **Vertigo**: enemies there have −1 morale, or −2 at the top two ranks. Returns next turn at −3 lengths. Skirmish reaches a second-rank god. The Clans already can.

Sketches. Reed Lancer 9 hp, attack 13, defense 5, morale 7, cost 16, flyer. **Stoop**: +3 damage on round-one hits. Threadcutter 8, 11, 4, 6, cost 12, flyer. **Rake**: on round one, may strike the second rank. Stake Guard 10, 7, 8, 9, cost 8. **Stake Line**: +2 defense on rounds 4 through 8. House Pilot 18, 13, 9, 11, cost 34. **Wind Mark**: while the pilot stands, flyers have +2 attack.

**High Band**: a smaller second sail, silhouette still wide. New Canopy Ray (hp 20, attack 12, defense 5, morale 8, cost 24, flyer), **Cast Shadow**: enemy flyers have −2 attack while it stands. Border provinces +1 gold. One extra plains step on your own land. Red tails stay. **Staked Houses**: lower, tethered, wings still wider than tall. Stoop becomes **Tethered** (+2 defense). New Tether Crew (hp 10, attack 9, defense 9, morale 10, cost 11), **Haul**: tethered flyers have +1 defense while one stands. Forts +2 gold. An enemy attacking you at home has −1 morale.

### The Amber Procession

Porters of a heating-bowl of amber-cord, the tool that keeps their weave workable. A rout line is a share of starting hp, so one fallen monster can end a small army.

**Thick Count**: ignore the first 10% of starting hp when figuring the rout. **Reliquary**: the Littermass has +8 hp. Opening on Iron Discipline and Reliquary leaves a quieter ruler. The bowl is the nation.

First Porter Ivo, mortal line, front. **Ash Tax**: if Ivo did not fight, the province earns +3/4/5/6 gold by rank. Heir in two turns, half lengths. The Sealed Bowl, undying, second rank, because it is carried. **Heavy Lid**: defenders there have +1 defense, or +2 at the top two ranks. Remade at Sootbowl in four turns at full lengths, or six if the capital has fallen.

Sketches. Bowlguard 12 hp, attack 7, defense 9, morale 9, cost 11. **Shoulder the Bowl**: +2 defense while your commander stands. Censer 8, 11, 5, 7, cost 10, second rank. **Seam Mark**: a fighter it has hit this battle is treated as having −2 defense against the rest of your side. Littermass 32, 11, 8, 6, cost 28. **Dead Weight**: when it falls, count an extra 8 hp lost toward the rout. Bowl Speaker 22, 7, 12, 13, cost 35. **Count the Living**: while the speaker stands, that extra loss is ignored.

**Open Bowl**: honey amber, cream cloth, verdigris nails kept. New Spark Porter (hp 10, attack 9, defense 8, morale 8, cost 12), **Share Heat**: wounded fighters heal 2 hp after a win. Every province +1 gold. Neighbouring holds fight at −1 morale. **Sealed Lid**: soot plate, amber seams, copper nails kept. **Under the Lid** replaces Shoulder the Bowl (+2 defense while defending). New Lidwalker (hp 18, attack 9, defense 13, morale 10, cost 20), **Set Down**: +2 defense in a fort. Recruits cost +2 gold and have +3 hp. Forts heal 4 hp a turn to units standing there.

From above: cream-and-soot files, one taller litter on the spine. Sootbowl is a long roofless amber shed. Muster is the inner lane, single file, out the mouth. ### The Open Knot

Neighbours by trade. They knot local cord and move on. Average against a rival. Bonuses fade once they own the ground, so the turn is usually a hold.

**Loose Welcome**: name one neighbouring Free Hold. It fights at −2 morale, and taking it pays 2 gold per garrison fighter. **Second Seat**: Sworn Vassals, plus 6 gold when the province joins. Spend for that and skip the fight. Keep Ness's lengths and the welcome still pays on a shaken hold.

Host Ness, mortal line, second rank, rope standard. **The Chair**: holds in Ness's province and next door fight at −0/1/1/2 morale by rank, added to Loose Welcome. Heir in two turns, half lengths. The Three Signatures are a **compact**, a proposed kind: three hosts, one office, one front-rank body. **Old Debt**: every 4/3/2/2 turns a neighbour joins you, garrison cashed at 2 gold a fighter. On death the next signature sits in two turns at half lengths if a commander remains, else four. Refused, that kind is just a mortal line at these numbers.

Sketches. Hearthguard 9 hp, attack 9, defense 8, morale 8, cost 9. **Fresh Seat**: +2 defense in a province you did not own at the turn's start. ## 4. Rivalries, and why simultaneous turns make them worse

Crown ranks meet a pack that wants the wood. Seraphs dive at Warchiefs. Living Iron wants hills. Thorn Wild grows forest and blunts a charge. Blood Pact wants an invader who arrives already short of heart.

The Choir is strong on a hill it reached last turn. The Houses want the meeting, where Open Sky pays and Ringing Ground does not. Both orders go in blind. Diamonds on Redmast's roof show from next door. The destination does not.

Ash Tax pays when Ivo does not fight, and a dead Littermass can rout the file, so the Procession likes a quiet turn. The Open Knot wants the next hold named and taken. Two entries into one province leave the garrison as a third party: one army can hit the Unwoven, the other a border with no defender. The wrong name wastes Loose Welcome.

Wildkin make a forest march a battle, for the Clans to braid, the Open Knot to buy, or the Choir to wait out. Fortresses fill in view. The gate they spill from is still hidden at commit.

## 5. Ascension sites

A **sag-yard** is a province where canopy has pooled into a climbable coil. Terrain under it stays plains, forest or hills, with the usual income and defender bonus. On the map the coil is a bright knot at the centre, in the cord's hue, never the player's colour, with no text. From the strategy camera it is sky lying on the ground.

Enough sag-yards tie the next band. That is the win. While you hold one, it also yields +2 research a turn, beside the capital's 5 and 1 from each other province. A hold on a yard is often rich enough for a captain, so the first grab is a garrison fight. A yard in a fort stays findable under fog, since owner, terrain and fort are public. Sketch: two on a small map, four for six players, split across the mirror.

## 6. Magic school seeds

Two schools each. The greater working waits on its matching recut, as M5 already has it for Crown and Clans. Rituals cost gold. Battle spells follow a list set before the fight.

**Sun** (Celestial Host) smites and mends. **Law** (Living Iron) shields and wards. **The Wild** (Thorn Wild) is briar, beast and forest. **Blood** (Blood Pact) spends hp, so Tithe of Blood stays with the Clans.

**Rim** (Clear Rim) adds defense on your land, and a ritual dries a few hp off an army. **Pitch** (Struck Rim) is −1 enemy morale while a bellwalker stands, and strips one fort's defense for a turn. **Lift** (High Band) adds flyer attack for a round, and reports a neighbour army's rough count. **Stake** (Staked Houses) pulls one enemy flyer into the front rank, and holds an army to one province next turn. **Ember** (Open Bowl) heals a few front-rank hp at a round's end, and adds 2 gold to a province for a turn. **Lid** (Sealed Lid) makes hits on one unit deal 1 less damage, and adds 2 defense to a fort for a turn. **Table** (Bound Table) is +1 morale off your own land, and −2 morale on one Free Hold for a turn. **Fray** (Pulled Thread) is −1 attack on fighters standing on a mandate bonus, and takes 4 gold from a neighbour.

Fog is canopy too low to see under. Far sight is a cord pulled taut toward a province you are not in.

## 7. Tone, and the lookalikes to watch

Bright cloth, sharp deals. The Choir cracks a bell at a festival and holds the wall with the pieces. Banners stay chalk, coral, lacquer, amber, marigold, royal blue and moss.

A cord sky can slide into fate-weaving. This canopy snags, armies muster under it, and it does not speak. Seraphs can read as angels: Crown soldiers, stiffened cord-ends, blue plume kept. The Blood Pact can read as a blood cult: keep the levy, cheap recruits who arrive wounded. Briarwardens can read as walking trees: beastfolk that have rooted. Living Iron can read as forge-folk: it is still Crownkeep. Shipped Thornmane already sit near a famous wargame's beastmen: Wildkin are those peoples unbraided. Kite Houses can read as air-monks or horse archers: reed frames, no horses. The Choir can read as miners: ordinary height, open pans. The Procession can read as a grail parade: the bowl is a tool. The Open Knot can read as sacred hospitality: it is a purse. If one image goes, drop the feathered Seraph before the canopy.
