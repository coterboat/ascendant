# R3, Codex: red-team and votes

## Red-team

### Engine fit

The synthesis is mostly disciplined, but several faction rules smuggle in state the engine may not support.

**Wick is the largest risk.** It adds a persistent counter to every Candlewake army, changes that counter after battles and fort visits, permits values above its normal maximum through Vigil, and destroys an army outside combat. Splitting, merging, retreating, ruler attachment, simultaneous fort capture and forced movement all need rules. Which wick survives a merge? Can a one-wick army evade destruction by joining another? If it captures a fort this turn, did it “end in a fort you own”? This is not a one-sentence faction rule until those cases exist in the engine.

The Concord also carries too much special handling. Named Hold requires a pre-resolution target declaration, checks adjacency to “your land,” modifies a neutral garrison, and awards gold per unit if that specific province changes hands that turn. Loose Terms then annexes provinces without battle. Contested annexation, simultaneous capture and the disappearance of eligible holds all need resolution rules. The faction supposedly plays one rule, but actually depends on a second diplomacy-shaped acquisition channel.

Several abilities create bookkeeping or new subsystems despite the stated purge:

- Golden Wake banks a discount at the capital for each fallen regular unit. That is a persistent, typed coupon ledger.
- Shearing earns income from an unowned province, potentially while another player owns it. That needs explicit theft and reporting behavior.
- Thunderborn’s universal two-province movement may bypass the road and adjacency assumptions behind movement forecasts.
- Weatherwife cancels fort bonuses while the text says fort-washing waits for sieges. Those claims conflict.
- “A taken hold should offer its local unit” is future scope, not current lore. Remove it until independent recruitment exists.
- Transformation-gated foreign magic schools require a technology dependency the packet may not have. If the engine only supports ordinary research prerequisites, say that plainly instead of implying a bespoke unlock graph.

Already There is safe but undersells its fiction. “On ground they reached first, they do not break” sounds like arrival-order tracking; the actual rule is simply a defender morale bonus. The pitch promises a more specific system than the rule delivers.

The Noonmarks are the cleanest part: one ownership check, one victory threshold and one research yield. Keep them that simple.

### Approachability

The Clans, Candlewake and Stormherd pass the two-sentence test. Their identities and battlefield incentives are immediate: hurt forest attackers, decaying undead armies seeking fights, and weather shepherds seeking meeting engagements.

The Kilnborn almost pass, but “ground they reached first” is misleading. A player who moved into a province earlier in the same turn may expect the bonus; only formal defender status matters. Say “They harden when defending ground they already hold.”

The Crown fails mechanically. “Spend late at list price” means nothing before the player understands ruler power, mandates, sacrifice timing and the universal +1 surcharge. Its fantasy is clear; its play pattern is written in internal economy language.

The Concord fails hardest. “Name one Free Hold and talk it half out of fighting” describes a diplomatic action, while the rule is a temporary morale debuff plus a bounty for conquest. The fiction says consent; the mechanic says mark a village for easier slaughter and collect payment per defender. That contradiction will confuse new players and sour the supposedly warm faction.

### Tone

The world is colourful and dark without becoming miserable. Candle processions, chained weather, living kilns and linked-house fortresses are strong map images. The held golden hour gives the setting warmth instead of generic apocalypse.

The weak point is that every culture has been reduced to a slogan ending. Six eschatological programmes can make characters feel like mouthpieces for victory conditions. The Free Holds help, but the major factions need ordinary internal dissent: a Candlewake who fears rest, a Kilnborn family that prefers repair to permanence, or Concord households that reject compulsory welcome.

Halfmade is also too neat. Wax, weather, clay and hearth form a designerly elemental set. The Clans and Crown feel inherited from an earlier game while the other four feel commissioned to fill conceptual slots. One stranger culture would break the symmetry.

### Lookalikes

The Crown remains close to Warhammer’s Empire and Bretonnia: blue-and-gold human monarchy, knights, spear blocks, crossbows and radiant transformation. The Clans remain close to Warhammer Beastmen and Warcraft orcs even after removing goats and “gor” names; deed-named beast tribes, berserkers, bone and a war bear are familiar fantasy stock.

Candlewake risks reading as *Dark Souls* or *Elden Ring*: dignified undead, dying flame, vigils and a world arrested because death has malfunctioned. The wax bodies are the differentiator, but “Black Wick” and soot-armoured flame-headed troops push back toward that cluster. The individual silhouettes must carry wax deformation, not merely fire crowns.

Stormherd combine *Pokémon*’s Mareep/Wooloo vocabulary with Miyazaki-style pastoral weather spirits. Yellow oilskins and chained clouds help, but “flying sheep become Stormlings” is the exact cute turn the lookalike guard claims to avoid.

Kilnborn sit between Zelda’s Gorons, *Final Fantasy IX*’s Black Mages in manufactured bodies, and generic terracotta armies. Gold-mended cracks also invoke contemporary kintsugi fantasy design, now common enough to be shorthand. They are attractive, not original.

The Concord risk becoming respectable hobbits: warm names, household militias, outdoor tables, carts, hospitality and a permanent supper. Roof-shields and coercive treaty mechanics distinguish them, but the pitch suppresses that severity.

At world level, ascension sites and pretender-like rulers remain structurally close to *Dominions*. Cutting the absent maker removes the most obvious Pantokrator echo, but “hold sacred sites so your ruler remakes the world” is still the comparison Alex will hear.

### Fairness of the merge

Three of four new peoples going to Claude is partly earned and partly home bias. Claude supplied the strongest immediately drawable silhouettes: wax dead, storm sheep and kiln people. Keeping all three is defensible on art direction alone. It is not defensible as proof that they were the three strongest complete factions, because the merge repeatedly repairs their rules with Codex and Grok material while continuing to label the resulting peoples as Claude’s.

The omissions from my seat were mostly fair. Glasswake, Ember Orchard and Underweather duplicated roles or carried real lookalike problems. But the Common Crown’s civic collar-rings deserved to survive as a concrete Crown transformation detail; they make “law poured into a people” visible in a way white-gold armour does not. Revision also deserved consideration as a school name or organizing principle because it described the setting’s distinctive act better than generic Law.

Grok’s Lacquer Reed deserved an actual slot or blind concept test. It was named the best unused people and then excluded while the safer, more conventional ceramic faction stayed. The Verdigris Pans at least deserve the promised art bake-off before Kilnborn are treated as settled. That is the clearest sign of home bias: the synthesis recognizes a strong alternative, then places it outside the proposal’s main six.

|---|---|---|
| Aurel Crown | **change** — rewrite the pitch around adaptive royal mandates | The faction works, but “spend late at list price” is tutorial-hostile accounting language. |
| Thornmane Clans | **keep** | The terrain attack rule, wounded-unit tension and silhouette form one legible package. |
| Candlewake | **change** — replace Wick with a one-bit “fought last turn / rested in an owned fort” state | The current per-army countdown creates too many split, merge and destruction edge cases. |
| Stormherd | **keep** | The meeting-battle bonus is distinctive, forecastable and tightly matched to the fiction. |
| Kilnborn | **change** — put them through a blind visual and pitch bake-off against the Verdigris Pans and Lacquer Reed | Their rule is solid, but the people are the most conventional of the four newcomers. |
| Hearthbound Concord | **change** — make Named Hold an explicit ultimatum, not friendly persuasion | The conquest bounty contradicts the language of hospitality and consent. |
| Halfmade | **keep** | Short, evocative and directly explains the setting’s central condition. |
| the Held Sun | **keep** | It is a strong world image and immediately frames the factions’ competing endings. |
| Quick | **change** — rename it **Making** | “Quick” suggests speed or mercury; “Making” explains ruler power, mandates and sites without glossary work. |
| Noonmarks | **keep** | The name is vivid, and the shared rule is appropriately simple. |
| Free Holds’ role | **change** — keep them neutral and defensive, but cut local recruitment and automatic annexation until supported | They ground the world well; speculative systems should not hitchhike on them. |
| Sun magic | **keep** | Light, healing and revelation are clear, and Golden Wake gives it a useful foreign bridge. |
| Wild magic | **keep** | Growth, beasts and self-wounding fit the Clans and existing combat language. |

| Weather magic | **keep** | It supports reconnaissance and pressure without lying to the forecast. |
| Hearth magic | **change** — remove “lower one hold’s morale” or frame it as an ultimatum | Rest and steadiness fit; magically softening neutrals repeats the Concord’s tonal contradiction. |
| Blood magic | **keep** | Costs, curses and unfinished death give it a coherent identity distinct from ordinary necromancy. |

## My one change

Replace Candlewake’s Wick countdown before Alex sees the proposal.

Use: **Afterglow — Candlewake regulars gain +2 morale if their army fought last turn or began the turn in an owned fort. Otherwise they suffer −1 morale while outside owned land.**

It preserves the intended rhythm—fight, return home, or weaken—without per-army countdowns, values above the normal cap, spontaneous army deletion, or unresolved split-and-merge exploits.
