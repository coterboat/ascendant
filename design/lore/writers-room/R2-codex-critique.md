# R2, Codex: critique

## Claude seat: *Halfmade*

### What to steal

The strongest idea is the Long Afternoon: a world visibly stuck at golden hour because its maker stopped halfway through creation. It explains the title race, gives every map a shared visual condition, and makes victory progress legible when the sky bends toward the leading faction’s intended ending. I am stealing that last device—not the absent maker, but the changing sky as a public ascension forecast. It turns victory pressure into scenery.

Claude also understands faction silhouettes better than either other pitch. Candlewake armies are fields of crown-flames; Stormherd armies are yellow oilskins among rolling cloud-beasts; Kilnborn armies are jar bodies beneath smoking walkers. Those images survive the strategy camera. The fortresses also behave: the thorn-ring opens, the storm fold releases a downhill flood, and the kiln mouth disgorges glowing soldiers. I am stealing that principle for every revised muster ground: each must show one clear action when an army departs.

The Candlewake’s military clock is excellent strategic drama. “Fight before your wick expires” gives simultaneous movement an immediate purpose. I would not steal the implementation wholesale, but the broader lesson is valuable: a faction’s map state should tell the player what kind of battle it wants this turn.

### What breaks in this engine

Candlewake carries two faction-wide rules before its units and rulers begin: never rout, per-unit wick, automatic wick loss, battle relighting, and ruler-dependent restoration. That is a new attrition subsystem with counters on every unit. Worse, “never rout” makes defense disproportionately powerful because the defender wins after round eight. Iron Discipline already occupies the no-rout space and preserves commander counterplay; Candlewake removes it.

The Chandler’s Vigil gives different amounts of wick by ruler rank, but wick is capped at six, producing unclear rank value. Half-Death raising enemy units directly into an army collides with curated rosters and recruitment rules. The Black Wick recruits only from enemy fallen, which could leave the branch unable to replace losses and needs corpse ownership, storage, and timing rules.

Stormherd’s economy breaks province ownership too casually. Each army earning full income from any occupied province encourages many tiny armies, while abandoned owned land paying half creates army bookkeeping and demands rules for two friendly armies, contested provinces, and income timing. Erasing all terrain and fort bonuses also invalidates a large amount of map preparation with one sentence.

The Revel is farther outside scope. Secret per-army traits, changing every turn, trait copying from enemies, two masks during one battle, uncertain forecasts, and copied foreign unit types form several new systems. They also violate the clean “one unit, one trait” model: three roster entries effectively have an open-ended trait slot rather than a defined job. The Host’s hidden location arrives before fog of war and would make ruler-risk forecasts harder to explain.

The Three Kiln-Mothers are three simultaneous ruler bodies, contradicting the current single traveling ruler model. Kilns that are both forts and recruitment sites may be feasible later, but building them anywhere in two turns overwhelms existing fort scarcity and planned siege play.

### What reads alike

Quick does too many semantic jobs: ruler power, world-making substance, mandate material, transformation fuel, divine body, and site reward. The fiction is simple, but repeated “pour Quick” language makes distinct institutions blur.

Several factions share the same operational sentence: establish a special structure or resource, then fight around it. Candlewake returns to the wakehouse, Kilnborn advances kiln by kiln, and Stormherd moves grazing income. That is good strategic texture individually, but together they crowd the map with bespoke exceptions.

Noonward and the packet’s celestial transformation are effectively identical. Ironsworn is Living Iron under another name. Briarbound and Duskrunners are recognizable but generic endpoints. The Revel’s mask identities also blur internally because masks replace rather than clarify roster roles.

### IP lookalike risks

The vanished Wright is close to *Dominions*’ absent Pantokrator at the exact structural point where the game is already being compared with *Dominions*. “The maker vanished; claim sites and finish creation” will be read as substitution, even without worship.

The Revel combines *Majora’s Mask*, the Faceless Men, and theatrical fae. The Endless Revel’s recruitment of conquered unit types further evokes assimilation factions common to fantasy strategy.

Candle-headed mourners risk reading as a nation extrapolated from the Pokémon Litwick line, while the Stormherd’s electric cloud-sheep invite the Mareep comparison Claude already notices. Kilnborn risk a cute ceramic construct species unless their civic life is more specific than pottery vocabulary.

## Grok seat: *The Strung*

### What to steal

Grok’s best work is mechanical discipline. “The Choir wants to have arrived last turn; the Kite Houses want both sides to arrive now” is exactly the level of asymmetry the engine supports. Defender status and same-turn meetings already exist, appear in forecasts, and generate simultaneous-turn reads without another phase. I am stealing that contrast for the Glasswake Compact: it will become the faction that prepares and holds, rather than a vague high-defense faction with a recycling subsystem.

The Open Knot’s focus on neutral provinces is also strong. It makes Sworn Vassals and independent garrisons part of faction identity rather than generic map furniture. I am stealing the idea that one faction should specialize in persuading Hearth Leagues, but I will place it in an ascension branch rather than give it several overlapping morale and gold clauses.

The Strung also gives transformation previews a physical metaphor—the swatch—that is more visual than my abstract account of future identity. I am stealing the presentation principle: transformation previews should show a rotating formation, province card, and fortress model before commitment.

### What breaks in this engine

The premise renames nearly every established term: world, sky, ruler power, mandate, mid-game sacrifice, transformation, preview, and neutral communities all acquire cord vocabulary. “Lengths,” “standing stitch,” “splice,” “recut,” and “swatch” create avoidable codex burden. Worse, my pitch already uses Recut for a different rule, showing how quickly invented nouns collide.

Many traits are not one effect. Shock changes defense rather than stating a normal attack modifier; Thunderclap removes an attack and needs target tracking; Seam Mark remembers targets across rounds; Shrapnel and Splash select additional targets without an existing rule; Rekindle revives units after battle. These are plausible future mechanics, but collectively they expand battle resolution substantially.

The Kite Houses require a flyer rank and rules for reaching commanders. “Flyer” currently describes a silhouette, not a mechanical rank. Extra plains movement and recruitment away from the capital also add map exceptions. The Open Knot’s named neutral, neighbor debuffs, garrison conversion, and gold per garrison fighter require prediction and ownership rules beyond the current neutral system.

The Noonmark proposal that restores ruler power undermines the defining permanent budget. A ruler who spent heavily could refill and retain mandates, eventually erasing the keep-versus-spend tradeoff. Site rewards should never manufacture ruler power.

### What reads alike

The canopy metaphor flattens the entire world. Every faction becomes another cord condition: noon-cord, thorn-cord, salt-glass cord, wind-cord, amber-cord. The factions have distinct models but not distinct explanations.

Glazewell Choir and Amber Procession both read as slow pale formations built around circular sacred objects, high defense, healing, and commanders who prevent their morale plan from collapsing. Open Knot and Aurel Crown both express institutional order through compact human-looking forces and province income.

Several branch pairs repeat “lighter mobile version versus darker defensive version.” Clear Rim/Struck Rim, High Band/Staked Houses, and Open Bowl/Sealed Lid need stronger economic and roster identities.

### IP lookalike risks

A woven world, living threads, rulers as shuttles, and reality edited by weaving approach a broad but crowded fate-weaving tradition. The danger is not one direct copy; it is that every reader already knows textile cosmologies from the Norns, Pattern-based fantasy, and loom-of-fate stories.

Kite Houses risk resembling *Avatar: The Last Airbender*’s air cultures once flight, silk, family houses, and high settlements combine. Glazewell’s disciplined bell society could drift toward fantasy monastery imagery. The Open Knot’s hospitality conquest resembles fae guest-right stories without yet having a distinct visual body.

## Weakest part of my own round-one pitch

My weakest material is the Interval Court. “People in pauses” sounds elegant at paragraph scale and evasive at game scale. Appointment is a marked move layered over ordinary orders, Held Hour postpones payment without a real benefit, and Timely Arrival improves information rather than creating a satisfying province ability. Its transformations are variations on abstract absence, difficult to model brightly and dangerously close to clockwork or time-mage clichés. More broadly, my pitch explains everything through Accord so thoroughly that cultures sometimes feel like demonstrations of the cosmology. Several transformations promise healing, movement, income changes, or wound sharing without saying which existing levers implement them. The revision must make each faction’s desired battle state obvious from the map and cut rules that require bespoke bookkeeping.
