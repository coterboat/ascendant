# R2, Grok (revised): The Strung

**What changed and why**

- Cut the textile manual. Five terms do the cosmology: **canopy**, **stitch**, **splice**, **recut**, **sag-yard**. Ruler power stays "ruler power."
- Stole Codex's mechanism and left the Stormlight nouns. A stitch outlives the weaver because it is in the people. A splice costs +1 because finished cloth resists. A god returns shorter because the body was the idea.
- One faction rule each, in the Claude shape. Crown gets **Charter** (Claude). Clans get **Wild Ground** (Claude), and it does not fire in border battles or meetings.
- Rebuilt the four new factions so none of them is glass, wax, clay, storm, mask, or orchard. Each rule is one forecastable bet.
- Kites no longer fly across the map. **Named Wind** (Codex's Appointment, credited) pays only when the named move is a meeting or a border battle.
- Deleted rout math, phantom hp, seraphs diving on commanders, map-wide forest conversion, the three-body compact, and any site that adds kept power.
- Transformations now state a loss (Codex). One accent hue and the old silhouette stay, per the bible.
- Neutrals are one sentence (Codex's Hearth Leagues were right). Magic never falsifies a forecast.

## 1. The world premise, and what ascension is

The world is **the Strung**. The sky is a **canopy** of dyed cord, low enough to snag a mast. It does not speak and it does not choose. People are cord that holds a shape. A ruler is whoever can carry ten lengths of it in one body. That is the whole pool.

The same cord cannot sit in two places. Power you keep is wrapped on the ruler: hp, attack, defense, and the rank of one province ability. Power you spend is run through the people as a **stitch**, a mandate. Recruits are cut from the current cloth, so they arrive already stitched. The stitch stays when the ruler dies because it is not in the ruler. Codex's point, in this world's words: Royal Armories persists because every forge was restretched to that pattern.

Spending more mid-war is a **splice**. The cloth is already finished, so the opening frays one extra power. The game shows the cloth before and after. The Crown is the exception, under Charter, below.

Rulers return by how they were tied. A **mortal line** is a knot in an ordinary body: the heir sits in two turns with half the kept power. An **undying** ruler is tied to an object: a new body climbs that cord in four turns and comes back whole. A **god** is canopy wearing a body: kill it and a new body is tied next turn, permanently three power shorter, because some of the idea was used up.

A **recut** is a transformation. Research can still go either way. The recut picks one pattern and burns the loom's ability to make the other. The preview shows the new people, the recruits you lose, the mandates that close, and the new materials. Silhouette, pose, and the held weapon stay. One accent keeps its hue.

**Free Holds** are provinces tied to themselves. No ruler, no stitches. A hold fights with a plain militia and a captain who adds +2 morale while standing. You take the land by beating them. **Sworn Vassals** talks one neighbouring hold into joining, with no battle. That is the whole neutral rule.

## 2. The source of power

One cord, three seats. In the ruler, it is personal might. In the people, it is stitches. In the ground, it is a **sag-yard**, canopy pooled thick enough to climb. Provinces do not mint extra power. Gold is dye, food, and metal. Research is learning a weave. Magic, later, pulls a cord you are not touching, and the pull is never a lie about where an army will be.

## 3. The six factions

### The Aurel Crown

Ordinary people, extraordinary kit, because the king spent the cord. You play them as ranks: spears in front, crossbows behind, knights for the first round, and a choice about whether the ruler is a man or a paladin.

**Charter** (Claude's rule, taken as written): the Crown's splices cost no +1. The sunstone stitch was tied in public at Crownkeep, so later stitches do not fray. The turn is a waiting game. Hold Corvane's power until you see the enemy build, then buy Royal Armories or King's Roads at the printed price. A ruler who spent early is easier to kill. A ruler who waits can still die before he spends. That is the argument.

Rulers, unchanged. **King Corvane**, mortal line, front. **Royal Court**: +2/3/4/5 gold in his province. Heir in two turns at half power. **The Ever-Regent**, undying, second rank, knotted to the sunstone. **Mending Light**: heal 3/6/9/12 hp a turn on your wounded in his province. Re-forms in four turns at full power. One plan parks him as the hospital. The other spends him empty and fights on roads.

Roster, shipped. Spearmen **Shield Wall**. Crossbowmen **Volley**. Knights **Charge**. Knight Captain **Inspire**.

Recuts. **Bright Lances** (the bible's celestial look; the name Seraph is dropped): knights stay mounted, lance upright, blue plume kept, armour white-gold with a ring of cord-light. Provinces you own earn +2 gold. Loss: Charter ends. Further splices cost +1 like everyone else's, and King's Roads closes. **Living Iron**: same soldiers, same weapons, blue plume kept; plate becomes dark riveted iron with orange seams. Regulars have +3 hp and +1 defense and cost +3 gold. Hills you own earn +2 gold. Loss: King's Roads closes, and Mending Light heals half as much. They are slower, harder, and worse at recovering a bad fight.

From above: a blue-and-white rectangle of vertical spears, knights a wider slab of white horses, the captain's standard over the helmets. Crownkeep is a high pale keep, blue roofs, a gilt sun-disc over the gate, the sunstone lantern reading as a second sun. Muster is the inner yard, blocks on painted lines, one gate. Banner: royal blue, white border, three-point hem, gold crown over a rising sun. Names stay stone-and-sun: Corvane, Harl, Voss; Crownkeep, Stonegate, Rivermarch.

### The Thornmane Clans

Beastfolk who let cord grow fur, tusk, and thorn. You play them hurt, in terrain that likes them, and you are punished for standing in the open in front of crossbows.

**Wild Ground** (Claude's rule, narrowed so it survives §7.2): if the battle is in forest or hills and the Clans are the attacker, they still receive that terrain's defender bonus. Forest is +2 defense. Hills are +1 attack and +1 defense. In a border battle or a meeting there is no defender, and Wild Ground adds nothing. The turn is whether you can attack into wood or onto a hill, or whether you should refuse the open plain. Feast of Victory still cashes a win into full hp and turns Berserk off. The Fang is a second barracks. The god is a front-rank body that buys routs with Dread and comes back shorter.

Rulers, unchanged. **The Great Thornmane**, god, front. **Dread**: −1 morale on every enemy side in that battle. Returns next turn at −3 power for good. **The Ninth Fang**, mortal line, front. **Warband Muster**: recruit 1/2/3/4 units where the Fang stands. Heir in two turns at half power, and recruiting pauses until the heir sits.

Roster, shipped. Tuskers **Berserk**. Fangthrowers **Skirmish**. War Bear **Fury**. Warchief **Pack Leader**.

Recuts. **Thorn Wild**: the tusker, the thrower, and the bear keep their silhouettes; briar grows over the same bodies. They do not become trees. Forests you own earn +2 gold and defend at an extra +1 defense. Loss: Blood Frenzy closes. **Blood Pact**: crimson paint stays, bone darkens. Recruits cost 2 less gold and arrive 2 hp down. Loss: Feast of Victory closes. A cheap, wounded muster is the plan, and a lost fight does not refill you.

From above: an uneven tawny mass, clubs at odd angles, one bear wider than the line. Fangridge is a thorn-ring on a ridge around a horned skull lodge. Muster is the open slope, a wedge that spills downhill when the briars part. Banner: ochre hide, crimson border, bone-hung torn edge, horned skull in green thorns. Names are short compounds: Ruk, Salla, the numbered Fangs; Fangridge, Thornvale, Bloodfen.

### The Verdigris Pans

Salt-rakers of ordinary height. White crust armour, charcoal straps, a verdigris yoke on the shield. No glass, no bells, no wells. You play them by arriving a turn early.

**Already There**: in a battle where you are the defender, your side's morale is figured 2 higher. Meetings and border battles give nothing, because you are not the defender. The turn is a race. If you can be on the province when they arrive, the crust holds. If you both march in this turn, you are a low-attack wall with no wall.

**Warden Brack**, mortal line, front, crust shield and a short mace. **Dry Tithe**: the province earns +2/3/4/5 gold. Heir in two turns at half power. Keep power and Brack is the wall. Spend it and the pans are rich and the warden dies in front. **The Kept Crust**, undying, second rank, tied to the salt-seal stone at the capital. **Seal**: heal 2/4/7/10 hp a turn on your wounded in that province. Re-forms in four turns at full power. Skirmish can reach a second-rank ruler. Parking the Crust on a sag-yard is the hospital plan, and the Clans are the reason it is dangerous.

Roster sketches. Crustguard 11 hp, attack 8, defense 10, morale 9, cost 10. **Set**: +2 defense while defending. Brine Sling 8, 11, 5, 7, cost 9, second rank. **Weight**: +2 damage on hits against a target with higher defense than this unit's attack. Pan Ram 22, 10, 9, 8, cost 22, a low horned beast in crust plates, wider than tall. **Brace**: while it stands, your front rank has +1 defense. Pan Marshal 24, 9, 12, 12, cost 34. **Hold Fast**: while the marshal stands, your fighters have +1 defense.

Recuts. **Bright Crust**: white stays, verdigris stays. Plains you own earn +2 gold. Set becomes +2 defense and +1 morale while defending. Loss: Brine Sling's Weight works only while defending, so a meeting is worse than before. **Broken Crust**: charcoal spreads over the plates; verdigris stays the accent. Set becomes **Chip**: +2 attack while defending. Recruits cost 2 less and have −2 hp. Loss: Already There no longer raises morale. You still want to be the defender, and you break like anyone else.

From above: low white discs in a tight ring, the ram a wider wedge in the middle, verdigris yokes flashing as the dark mark. Nothing translucent, so they do not read as glass fan-shields. The capital **Dryhold** is a low circular crust-wall on plains, one notch for a gate, the seal-stone a dark block in the centre. Muster is the flat pan inside the ring; companies form circles and leave by the notch. Banner: white cloth, charcoal border, a verdigris yoke (a wide U under a bar). Names are dry and two-beat: Brack, Holm Vett, Sura Kem; Dryhold, Whitecrack, Yokelevee.

### The Kite Houses

Rival families on black reed and indigo silk, with lacquer-red tails. The wing is a mount. It is wider than tall, and the legs of the frame show. They do not cross extra provinces. They do not ignore forts on the campaign map. A fort still adds +2 defense to its defender, and a meeting is the only place their rule pays.

**Named Wind** (the decision in Codex's Appointment, retied to kites): once a turn, name one of your army's moves. If that move becomes a border battle or a meeting, that army's flyers have +2 attack for the battle. If you are the defender, or the province is empty, the naming does nothing. The forecast shows both lines before you commit. The turn is a hunt for the army that just left a wall. Holding a sag-yard means sitting still, and sitting still wastes the rule. That tension is Claude's herd problem, solved without grazing income.

**Ribbon-Captain Oku**, mortal line, front, on a kite, reed lance. **Signal Mast**: recruit 1/2/3/4 Reed Lancers where Oku stands. Heir in two turns at half power. A front-rank reed frame dies fast, so kept power is how the mobile barracks survives. **The High Cord**, god, front, a huge silk-and-reed body that fills the monster frame and does land. **Vertigo**: enemies in that battle have −1 morale, or −2 at the top two ranks of kept power. Returns next turn at −3. A second-rank god would be food for Skirmish, so the god is in front on purpose.

Roster sketches. Reed Lancer 9 hp, attack 12, defense 5, morale 7, cost 15, flyer. **Rake**: on round one, may strike the second rank. Threadcutter 8, 11, 4, 6, cost 11, flyer. **Stoop**: +2 damage on round-one hits. Stake Guard 10, 7, 9, 9, cost 8. **Stake Line**: +2 defense on rounds 4 through 8. House Pilot 18, 12, 8, 11, cost 33, flyer. **Wind Mark**: while the pilot stands, your flyers have +1 attack.

Recuts. **High Band**: a smaller second sail, the shape still wide, red tails kept. Rake works every round. Border provinces you own earn +1 gold. Loss: Stake Line closes. You cannot become the late-round wall. **Staked Houses**: frames tethered, wings still wider than tall, red tails kept. Named Wind flips: it pays the +2 attack only when you are the defender, and never on a meeting. Stake Line becomes +2 defense while defending, all rounds. Forts you own earn +2 gold. Loss: Rake and Stoop close. The fork is the whole faction: keep hunting collisions, or become the wall the other branch cannot be.

From above: flat red-tailed diamonds over a few narrow stake-guards. Not clouds, not oilskins, not lightning. **Redmast** is reed masts and silk roofs on hills. Muster is the roof-deck; the kites walk off the edge into the province. Banner: near-black indigo, lacquer-red border, a straw-gold kite and tail. Names are a house plus a person: Oku of House Sann, Miri, Tass; Redmast, Tailwind, Sparward.

### The Long File

Porters in cream cloth and soot straps, copper buckles, apricot lining. They march in a column, not a block. They keep their dead as a wage, and they do not edit the rout formula. A monster is never most of the army's hp, so one fall does not end the battle by arithmetic.

**Aftercount** (Codex's Aftercrop, paid where recruitment actually happens): after you win a battle, each of your regular units that fell gives 3 gold off the next recruit of that same unit at your capital. One coupon per body, shown in the battle report, spent or lost when you next recruit that unit. You do not recruit on the battlefield. The turn is whether to win ugly. Wounds already carry, and almost nobody heals them. A won fight with four dead guards is a cheaper muster next turn and a thinner army tonight.

**First Counter Ivo**, mortal line, front, copper tally-rod. **Quiet Road**: if Ivo did not fight this turn, the province earns +3/4/5/6 gold by rank. Heir in two turns at half power. A fighting Ivo is a body. A parked Ivo is the treasury, and he cannot do both. **The Closed Tally**, undying, second rank, tied to the tally-board at the capital. **Spare the Count**: in battles in that province, your morale is figured 1/2/3/4 higher by rank. Re-forms in four turns at full power. This is not the Regent's hospital. Wounds stay. The Tally only moves the break line, and only where the board is carried.

Roster sketches. File Guard 11 hp, attack 8, defense 8, morale 9, cost 10. **Close File**: +2 defense while another File Guard stands. Marker 8, 11, 5, 7, cost 9, second rank. **Mark**: for the rest of the battle, a target this unit has hit has −2 defense against your other fighters. Yoke Beast 20, 11, 7, 8, cost 20, a harnessed draft beast, cream housing, apricot fringe. **Drag**: +2 attack while a File Guard stands. File Speaker 22, 8, 11, 12, cost 33. **Keep the Count**: while the speaker stands, your regulars have +2 hp in this battle.

Recuts. **Open File**: apricot lining and cream stay. Aftercount pays 5 gold. Recruits cost 2 less and arrive 2 hp down. Loss: Close File closes. The column is a wage, not a wall. **Shut File**: soot covers the cloth; apricot stays the accent. Regulars have +3 hp and cost +2 gold. Close File becomes +2 defense while defending. Loss: Aftercount pays nothing. The dead are no longer a purse. You hold.

From above: a cream-and-soot line, longer than it is wide, the yoke beast the one tall shape on the spine, apricot showing when the line turns. **Longshed** is a roofless copper-framed shed on plains, one lane, no bowl and no flame. Muster is that lane, single file, out the mouth. Banner: cream, soot border, an apricot yoke. Names take one stress and a work-word: Ivo, Nessa Kett, Speaker Dain; Longshed, Copperspan, Halffile.

### The Factor Houses

Neighbours tied in pairs by white rope, marigold coats, ink-blue cuffs. They are not hosts and not a theatre. Two figures read as one soldier from above, so a block is a domino of pairs with white loops in the gaps. You play them by naming a hold the turn you mean to take it.

**Named Hold**: at the start of the turn, name one neighbouring Free Hold. Its garrison fights at −2 morale. If you take that province this turn, you gain 2 gold per garrison fighter that was there. If you do not take it, you get nothing. The forecast shows the morale and the gold. Two attackers into one hold make a three-way: the garrison is a third side, and the battle between the armies that both just arrived has no defender. The gold goes to whoever owns the province after the battle. Naming the wrong hold, or naming the one a rival also enters, is the whole drama.

Their exclusive mandate sketch, **Ink Fee** (cost 2): when a hold joins you by Sworn Vassals, gain 6 gold. It does not stack with Named Hold on the same province in the same turn, because a join resolves in the events step, after battles.

**Factor Ness**, mortal line, second rank, rope standard. **The Margin**: neutral garrisons in this province and next door fight at −0/1/1/2 morale by rank, and this does not stack with Named Hold; the worse single penalty applies. Heir in two turns at half power. **The Walking Seal**, god, front, a bulky knot of rope and marigold cloth, monster-sized, not a beast. **Loose Terms**: every 4/3/2/2 turns by rank, one neighbouring Free Hold joins you, garrison dismissed, no Named Hold gold. Returns next turn at −3 power. A spent god expands slowly. A kept god expands without battles and fields a thin nation. That is the budget.

Roster sketches. Pairguard 10 hp, attack 9, defense 8, morale 8, cost 10. **Twin**: +2 defense while another Pairguard stands. Bondsling 7, 11, 4, 7, cost 8, second rank. **Spoils**: +2 attack if your army entered a province you did not own at the start of the turn. Knotmule 16, 12, 6, 7, cost 16, a stocky draft mule in ink-blue tack, not a bear. **Due**: +3 damage on hits in a battle where a Free Hold garrison is one of the sides. Knot Herald 18, 9, 8, 12, cost 32. **Witness**: while the herald stands, your fighters have +1 attack against a Free Hold garrison.

Recuts. **Settled Ink**: marigold deepens, white rope stays the accent, coats lengthen. Provinces you own earn +2 gold. New unit, Doorward, 12 hp, attack 9, defense 9, morale 11, cost 14. **Bar**: +2 defense while a Pairguard stands. Loss: Named Hold no longer pays gold, and Due closes. You tax land instead of stripping garrisons. **Pulled Thread**: ink-blue goes near-black, marigold stays, white rope frays and stays the accent. New unit, Unpicker, 8 hp, attack 12, defense 5, morale 7, cost 12, second rank. **Unpick**: +1 damage for each mandate the enemy has, up to +3. The forecast adds it up, because mandates are public. A win against a mandated enemy pays 2 gold per mandate they have, once. Loss: Twin closes. Pairguards are no longer a wall. You punish a stitch-heavy Crown and you die if the pilot gets a clean meeting.

From above: pairs, white loops, marigold blocks with ink cuffs, the herald's standard the tall piece, the Seal a fat knot at the front if the god came. **Thirdseat** is a roofless double hall on plains, two doors, a knot-stone between them. Muster is the aisle; pairs form, tables go to the walls, both doors open. Banner: marigold, ink-blue fringe, a white overhand knot. Names in two beats: Ness, Sela Varn, Factor Pellan; Thirdseat, Warmdoor, Lowtable. Varn here is a family name inside the Factors, not a clan.

## 4. Rivalries, and why simultaneous turns make them worse

Crown ranks want a set battle on ground they already hold. Clans want to attack into forest or hills, where Wild Ground pays, and they want to refuse a meeting in the open, where it does not. Both can order the same wood. If the Crown is already there, both of them receive the forest bonus: the Crown as defender, the Clans as the attacking exception. If they cross into the wood on the same turn, nobody gets it. The report says which.

Pans against Kites is the clean bet. The Pans want to have arrived last turn. The Kites want both armies to arrive this turn, and they have named the move. If the crust is already set, Named Wind pays nothing and Already There does. If both arrive, the wind pays and the crust does not. The report line is the scene: "Both armies arrived this turn. The wind was named. The crust was not set."

The Long File wants a win with bodies on the ground. The Factors want a named hold. If both enter that hold, the garrison is a third side, the two armies have no defender against each other, and the gold goes to the owner at the end. The Factors can hand the File a softer garrison and then lose the purse. The File still banks Aftercount for its own dead at its own capital.

Crown stitches are the Factors' favourite enemy after Pulled Thread: every mandate is +1 damage, shown before the battle. Living Iron closes King's Roads and makes that Crown slower to reinforce the province the Kites are trying to catch empty. None of this is hidden. Each order's forecast shows the branch that depends on the other player.

## 5. Ascension sites

A **sag-yard** is a province where canopy has pooled into a climbable coil. The terrain under it stays plains, forest, or hills, with the usual income and the usual defender bonus. Holding enough of them ties the next band of the canopy. That is the win. While you hold one, it also yields +2 research a turn. It does not add ruler power. The pool stays 10.

They share that rule so a new player has one sentence to learn. They do not share a silhouette. **Sun-snag** is a gold-white coil caught on a pale tower, on plains. **Briar coil** is a green tangle in a tree, in forest. **Salt hitch** is a low white loop crusted around a dark stone, on plains. **Mast loop** is a red-tailed coil hooked on a reed mast, on hills. **Copper hitch** is a long coil lying down the length of a shed. **Knot well** is a marigold coil standing in an open double court. From the strategy camera each is sky lying on the ground, in the cord's own hue, never the player's colour, with no text. A hold on a yard is often rich enough to have a captain, so the first grab is a garrison fight. Sketch: two yards on a small map, four for six players, split across the mirror.

## 6. Magic school seeds

Six schools. A working says its numbers before you cast it. None of them invents a false destination. Fog, when it exists, hides armies; it does not hide the rules.

**Sun** (Crown, Bright Lances): mend a shown amount, and light one neighbouring province so its army count is reported as a range. **Law** (Crown, Living Iron): +2 defense on one of your provinces for a battle, and a fort's bonus stays visible in the forecast. **Wild** (Clans, Thorn Wild): briar on one forest battle, +1 defense for your side if the terrain already helps you. **Blood** (Clans, Blood Pact): spend a shown amount of hp for a shown attack bonus. Tithe of Blood can live here when rituals exist. **Seal** (Pans): the defender's Already There is shown as a reminder in the forecast, and one ritual dries 2 hp of wounds off each of your units in a province you own. **Lift** (Kites): +2 attack for your flyers for one round in a battle you already named with Named Wind, and a true rough count of one neighbouring army. **Tally** is not an eighth school; the File's gold is Aftercount, and Sun is how they buy mending if they want it. **Fray** (Factors, Pulled Thread): for one battle, one named enemy mandate gives half its usual number, rounded down, and the mandate is still there afterward. The preview shows the halved line. That is Codex's Severance, shrunk to a single battle and a single stitch.

Seal and Lift are the two schools the new map actually needs. Fray is the counter to a Crown who stitched everything on turn one under Charter. If the list has to stay at six, drop nothing of Sun, Law, Wild, and Blood, and let Seal, Lift, and Fray be the three seeds still to cut to two at M5.

## 7. Tone, and the lookalikes to watch

Bright cloth, sharp deals, no sermon from the sky. The Pans crack a crust at a fair and hold the wall with the slabs. The File sings the names it is about to cash. The Factors argue the price in the aisle, then walk out in pairs. Banners stay royal blue, moss and crimson, white and verdigris, indigo and lacquer, cream and apricot, marigold and ink. Darkness is a spent god and a stitch you cannot unpick, not a brown map.

Risks, watched on purpose. The canopy can slide into fate-weaving or into Stormlight if anyone calls a stitch an oath. It snags. It is cloth. It is not a promise with a personality. Bright Lances can slide back into angels the moment anyone adds wings; they stay knights, plume kept, ring of light behind the head, lance in the hand. Blood Pact can slide into a cult; it is a cheaper muster that arrives already hurt. Thorn Wild can slide into ents; the bear stays a bear. Living Iron can slide into forge-dwarves; it is still Crownkeep's roster in new metal. Kites can slide into air monks or into Claude's storm and Codex's thunder-parliament; they are reed, silk, and a named collision, with no cloud and no extra movement. The Long File can slide into a body-count army; apricot lining, sung names, no masks, no trenches. The Factors can slide into Dogs of War; paired coats and a rope, no pikes and no landsknecht stripes. Dryhold can slide into dwarven miners; ordinary height, open pans, no halls in the mountain. If one image has to go, the ring of light on the Bright Lances goes before the canopy does.
