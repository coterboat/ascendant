# The Lacquer Reed: the body is the instrument

A Reed did not learn an instrument. The Hollowing makes the body one: glossy deep-teal lacquer, gold joints, a gilded foot, one living face in a bone-white bell, violet resin at the rim. The shape is the gift. A stopped bore swallows a blow. A bellows does not breathe again. A coil spends its first breath at once. Open tubes stay a whisper until a second voice opens. The army is a wind section. The turn is whether the chord is still standing when the round begins.

Low Note stays the faction rule, and it is separate from the chords. In a forest the Reed already holds, second-rank fighters deal +2 damage on a hit. Swamp counts as forest. A meeting, a border and a three-sided hold turn Low Note off. Chords play either way. The forecast prints them on two lines, so a singer in a held wood shows the forest and the chord as two causes.

From the strategy camera the block is still a comb of vertical pipes. Up close each silhouette is a different wind. Sketches are hp, attack, defense, morale and cost. Design owns the numbers. One trait each.

## The section

**Reedward**, front, recorder. 9/8/8/8/9. **Dampen:** 1 less damage from each hit, and the hit still deals at least 1. The body is a straight bore whose holes can all be closed, so a blow lands softer. On the sheet this is the shielded recorder, the wall a chord leans on after round 1.

**Shawm**, front, shawm. 7/11/5/7/8. **Overblow:** +2 damage on a hit against a fighter already hit this battle. A flared bell and a double reed: loud, thin, and it finds the crack a heavier voice opened.

**Skirl**, front, bagpipe. 7/10/5/7/8. **Flat Note:** a fighter it hits has −2 attack on its next attack. The torso is the bellows, with three drones behind the bell, so the note does not wait for a new breath. Alone it flattens one swing. Inside Drone Fifth the bellows holds the whole front rank.

**Hole-singer**, second rank, panpipes. 7/10/6/8/10. **Harmony:** +1 attack to the side while two Hole-singers stand. The chest is a fan of open tubes, and a lone singer is marked "Harmony is quiet." Placement is a new value, `singer`: behind the front, with no Volley shield, so any enemy in the front rank can reach them.

**Crook**, front, the folded bass. 8/10/7/8/12. **Crook:** +2 damage on a hit against defense 10 or higher. The bore doubles back, as a bassoon does, and the note comes out around a shield. On the sheet that fold is the wide doubled tube.

**Horn**, front, the coil. 8/11/6/8/11. **Fanfare:** +3 damage on a hit in round 1. The coil stores one breath and spends it at the top of the battle. From round 2 it is simply a horn that still belongs to the chord.

## Rulers and the general

**Yssa of the Three**, mortal line, second rank, a bass recorder. She finished the Hollowing; her siblings stopped Half-poured, and the Solid sit beside her because she went through. She holds the three-hole reed upright. **Quiet Audience:** her province earns +2/3/4/5 gold while no enemy is in it or next to it. The recorder pays when the hall is empty, so the section is finished at Chimewater and then marched. In battle, the first time a chord would go quiet because a required voice fell, it sounds until the end of the next round, once. She holds the phrase. She does not invent a chord by standing there.

**The Pale Note**, god, second rank. The marsh was already an instrument, and this is the missing voice: a bell flooded with violet, a tuning fork in hand. It bristles at the name Wright. **Fearful Pitch:** enemies here start at −1 morale, or −2 if it has kept 6 or more power. While it stands and any chord is sounding, that penalty is 1 worse, on its own forecast line. A finished chord in the god's wood is what the other army hears before the first blow. It returns next turn, 3 power smaller.

**The Chormeister**, commander, a cornett. Same pipe and bell, a thin gold baton raised over the cone. 16/9/8/11/35. **True Pitch:** hit chance on his side cannot fall below 50%. While he stands the side cannot play flatter than half, and he is the fifth body of Resolved Chord. One per army, plus the usual +2 morale. The sheet ends on the Pale Note, violet, fork in hand. The general is a shorter Horn with the baton as the tall read. Where the sheet's first recorder also carries a spear, that spear is the soldier's. Yssa's held item is the three-hole reed.

## The four chords

A chord is not a spell and not a button. At the start of each round the battle looks at which voices are still standing, and in which rank, and sounds the single highest chord those bodies still make. Smaller chords inside it stay silent. The log names the interval that went quiet.

**Resolved Chord.** Horn, Shawm and Reedward in the front, a Hole-singer in the second rank, and the Chormeister still up. While it sounds, every enemy fighter's attack is reduced by 2, and stays at least 1. This is the phrase that resolves, one round at a time, for as long as those five live. Fanfare, the rank-wide drone and the singer's extra damage sit inside it and do not apply again. The log says, "Bright Third and Stopped Second sit inside Resolved Chord."

**Bright Third.** Horn, Shawm and Reedward, all in the front. In round 1, Fanfare's +3 damage on a hit is shared with the Shawm and the Reedward, and the Horn does not take it twice. From round 2: "Bright Third sounded." Leave the singer at home, hit once, very hard, then be a teal line with a wall. If Resolved also qualifies, Resolved plays.

**Drone Fifth.** Skirl and Crook, both in the front. While both stand, Flat Note hits every enemy front-rank fighter at the start of the round, and the Skirl's own hits do not apply it again. The Crook's trait still prints on the blows that meet a high defense. Kill either voice and the rank stands up straight.

**Stopped Second.** A Reedward in front and a Hole-singer behind. The singer deals +2 damage on a hit. In a forest you hold, Low Note adds its own +2 on a second line. The singer is unshielded, so one spearman can end the cheap chord, which is why it stays cheap.

A full army plays Resolved until it loses one of the five, then drops to the next chord it still has. A Bright Third army that never bought a singer never climbs. A Skirl beside a Crook plays Drone Fifth and nothing above it. The card says which, before the march.

## Across turns, and in the log

You build a chord by recruiting the missing voice. At the Sounding Bowl a Solid drinks, a new bore stands up, and the comb files out through the gate-pipes. The army card shows four rows: voices you have, the voice you lack, and the highest row you satisfy marked "will sound." The bodies are the cost. Finish the section where Quiet Audience is paying, then walk it into a wood you hold.

A split warns before you commit. "This split breaks Bright Third. The Horn leaves. The Shawm stays." A merge names the chord it would complete. Under fog, your chord uses only your own fighters. The enemy's chord stays hidden with their roster and is learned when the unit types are seen. Hotseat shows each seat its own card. Your chord never needs a fact from the other side of the handoff.

Under the odds: "Deepwood. Forest you hold. Low Note: Hole-singers deal +2 damage. Chord: Resolved Chord. Horn, Shawm, Reedward, Hole-singer, Chormeister. Enemy attack −2 while it sounds. If the Hole-singer falls, Resolved goes quiet, and Drone Fifth remains if the Skirl and the Crook live."

In the log: "Round 1. Resolved Chord sounds. Enemy attack −2. A Spearman hits the Reedward for 3." Then, "Round 4. The Shawm fell. Resolved Chord goes quiet. Drone Fifth sounds. Enemy front rank, attack −2." If she spends it: "Yssa holds the phrase. Resolved Chord sounds for one round more."

The 3D caption is the instruments, one line. "The horn, the shawm and the recorder sound together." "The drones hold." "The pipes answer from behind the recorder." The rule name stays in the log. Hover a hit and the bonus names its source.

## Cost, and the answer

New machinery is the placement `singer` and a table of four chords, checked at the start of each round from who still stands and in which rank. Effects are damage, attack and a one-round delay. Overblow needs one flag, "already hit this battle." No new phase and no new resource.

The computer scores a recruit by whether it completes a higher chord. If Bright Third is ready and a fight is in reach this turn, it marches.

You answer a chord by killing one voice: the Hole-singer, the Horn, or either half of the drone. That focus fire is the blows that might have cracked the Reedward, so the counter costs something. Lose the specialist and the card shows an empty row. The pleasure is the chord. The explanation is one marked row before the battle, and one line in the log when it goes quiet.
