# The Ember Orchard

## Pitch

The Ember Orchard is a society of walking funeral growths, each body carrying several consenting dead as warm seeds. It is slow, durable and willing to lose bodies because citizenship continues through remembered voices rather than solitary resurrection.

## Origin, choice, and the Wright

When the first people of Cindergrove burned their dead, seeds sprouted in the cooling ash and spoke with the dead person’s cadence. The discovery split the valley. One side feared imitation; the other asked the seedlings questions only the deceased could answer. Those first witnesses chose a third way: no ancestor would be planted without consent, and no seed would house only one voice. Several dead share each body, checking and comforting one another while a new personality grows among them. The living choose the Orchard because they dread neither death nor change, but oblivion and ownership. A body is a season; a household of voices is the citizen.

The Orchard believes the Wright did not leave: it died in making Halfmade, and its scattered recollections germinate in every living thing. This is faith, not proof. Their fiercest internal dispute asks whether planting all ancestral seeds would reconstitute the Wright and erase the smaller persons they became.

They seek **the Ripening**. The Held Sun descends into the soil and rises as warmth within every living body. Day and night begin, death becomes real, and every consenting mind leaves a seed that may join a future household. Their beautiful promise risks turning casualties into raw material and dissenters into “unripe” citizens.

## Play

**Faction rule — Returned Seed:** after a victorious battle, the first destroyed Orchard regular unit reduces the next recruit’s cost in that province by 3 gold; only one discount waits per province. The report names the stored discount. A turn asks whether a costly Hulk survives, whether one calculated casualty makes a frontier replacement affordable, and whether to attack for post-battle continuity. A loss gives nothing, so sacrifice is never free fuel.

Rivals: the Veiled Table says membership must be renewed by living households; the Orchard replies that death should not cancel a voice. Candlewake’s harvesting of living essence is an obscenity to them: continuity must be gifted. The Crown fears a citizenry whose witnesses can persist for centuries.

Rulers: **Grandmother Cinderpod**, undying, second rank, anchored to the ancestor furnace; *Warm Soil* heals wounded units in her province. **Rill of the First Garden**, god, front rank; *Ripening* reduces regular recruit costs in its province by 1/2/3/4 gold.

Roster: **Husk Bearer** — *Packed Pith:* +4 maximum hp while another Husk Bearer stands. **Sparkspitter** — *Cinder Seed:* +2 attack against targets below full hp. **Orchard Hulk** — *Last Fruit:* when it falls, one wounded allied regular heals 4 hp. **Ash Gardener** (commander) — *Tending:* after its side wins, every surviving allied regular heals 1 hp.

## Art direction and risks

An army reads as an orchard walking in rows: round orange crowns, forked charcoal limbs, cream seed-pods and huge fruit monsters. Hot-magenta interiors remain visible in hard shadow. The cream banner shows a charcoal seed split around magenta flame beneath three orange leaves. Cindergrove is a kiln-wall ring around ancestor growths and a burial furnace; troops muster as buds in ash furrows, then pull themselves free. Ceramic shells and multi-voice kernels prevent ordinary treants; consent, warmth and new composite selves prevent undead or plant-zombie shorthand.
