# The Underweather Assembly

## Pitch

The Underweather Assembly is a people of hollow civic bodies who keep weather inside themselves and govern through loud public argument. Fragile delegates screen dangerous ranged vessels, and the army is strongest when it predicts an enemy march and collides before anyone owns the ground.

## Origin, choice, and the Wright

The low country once suffered weather that arrived without skies: rain rose from wells, thunder rolled underground, and pressure crushed houses on clear days. Potters built open ceramic frames around dangerous gusts to measure them. During the Seven-Day Debate, a trapped storm answered every proposal with a different rhythm of rain. The people concluded it was not a beast but a multitude. Volunteers opened their bodies, replaced ribs and lungs with civic frames, and invited portions of the weather inside. They chose transformation because walls only stored the crisis; bodies could argue with it. Each citizen is now a negotiated quorum of clay craft, human habit and captive-yet-enfranchised storm.

The Assembly denies a solitary Wright. Creation’s seams, they argue, show several incompatible makers whose dispute was never adjourned. Thunder is surviving speech. A minority believes the absent chair at every session belongs to one Wright; leaving it empty prevents doctrine from claiming certainty.

Their ending is **the Great Session**. The Held Sun breaks into a moving parliament of weather and light: dawn, rain, heat and night take turns according to a cosmic procedure that can always be amended. Nothing is finally finished. The hope is corrigibility; the horror is a world where every harvest awaits another debate.

## Play

**Faction rule — Breaking Weather:** Assembly regular units get +1 attack in battles where no side is defender. It applies to meetings and border battles, not attacks on settled provinces, and the report names it. A turn asks where enemy movement will create a collision, whether to split forces to cover two routes, and whether a tempting fort is worth losing the bonus. The faction plays the simultaneous-turn layer rather than adding a resource.

Rivals: Glasswake wants to arrive first and settle; Underweather wants both forces to arrive now. The Aurel Crown calls endless revision weakness; the Assembly calls a permanent charter embalmed error. Stormherd dislike anyone claiming weather can be represented rather than herded.

Rulers: **Chair Sola Bront**, mortal-line, second rank; *Open Session* adds gold in her province if she did not fight that turn. **The Low Thunder**, god, front rank; *Pressure Front* gives second-rank units +1/1/2/2 attack in its province’s battles.

Roster: **Clay Delegate** — *Quorum:* +1 defense while at least three Clay Delegates stand. **Rainneedler** — *Volley:* attacks from protected second rank. **Thunder Vat** — *Discharge:* +4 damage with hits in round one. **Storm Chair** (commander) — *Call to Order:* after the first allied loss, allied fighters get +2 morale for that battle.

## Art direction and risks

From above, small terracotta hexagonal clusters screen tall copper vats and diagonal rain rods. Turquoise cords bind the palette; storm-gray glass provides darkness, lightning and white ceramic provide light. The turquoise banner traps a white spiral in an open red hexagon. Roaring Forum is an amphitheater around a cloud well with copper lightning towers; debating circles are the muster ground and snap into columns at decision. Open architectural frames, visible gaps and civic implements avoid warrior jars; contained clouds inside built bodies avoid generic storm elementals.
