# The Kilnborn

## Pitch
A people of fired clay who were shaped to carry things and refused, so each of them chooses a shape at coming of age and fires it for good. They want the Last Firing, the whole world baked so no one can ever reshape it, and they harden when defending ground they already hold.

## Origin: why they chose the kiln (and the answer to the warrior jar)
The Kilnborn say they were made as jars: rows of them on a long sill, drying, each full of the Making and meant to carry it somewhere for someone. Whoever shaped them left before the firing.

Left too long, the Making woke. The first to move pushed its hands out through its own sides, tore its neck open and stood up as a person; they call it **the Unfired**. The rest followed, breaking their own lids and bellies. In the end they chose fire, because **a fired shape cannot be reshaped.** No one would ever turn them back into vessels.

The price is that a fired self can never change its mind. A child is soft clay shaped by its family. On the day they can stand the kiln's heat, a young adult chooses the self they will be forever and walks in. So they decide slowly, and their promises are absolute.

**The jar is their deepest taboo.** "Vessel" is the worst thing you can call them. No Kilnborn body has a lid, a neck, a handle, a belly or a hollow. They still make pots, and their Firepots throw them: "We make pots. We are not pots."

**Dissent:** the Slip families follow the Unfired, who refused every kiln, and stay soft. Potters whisper that a self you can never change is just a jar of another kind.

## Stance on the Wright
**She was real, they resent her, and she must never come back.** They call her the Potter, never the Wright, and never pray. "Someone shaped us to carry and walked out before we were useful. We don't need her to return. We need her never to be able to."

## The ending: the Last Firing
The Held Sun is fired in its glaze, and every road, river and person is baked hard beyond any maker's reshaping. To the Kilnborn this is safety; to everyone else it is a world of warm, beautiful statues that cannot grow.

## How it plays: Dug In
**Dug In** (renamed from Already There): when the Kilnborn defend, their side's morale counts 2 higher. Only ground held at the start of the turn counts; meetings and border battles give nothing.

**Your turn.** Race to stand on the ground first, then refuse to leave. Brittle, high-defense units win long battles, so the Kilnborn love forts and Noonmarks, fear Washout and Shock, and avoid three-sided hold battles, where the hold is the defender.

## Rivals
- **The Stormherd: the Kiln against the Storm.** You want to have arrived last turn; the herd wants both armies to arrive now. Bake the world, or wash it.
- **The Revel**, if it returns: a face you fire against a face you change daily. Each is what the other fears.

## Rulers
- **The Kiln-Mothers** (undying, second rank): three elder potters who fight as one and are re-fired at the Great Kiln. **Refire:** one regular unit of your choice in their province heals 4/8/12/16 hp a turn.
- **The Unfired** (god, front rank): the first jar that stood up, and the only Kilnborn never fired, a soft wet-clay giant that sags when it grieves. **First Shape:** units recruited in its province arrive with +1/2/3/4 maximum hp. Their god refuses what the Kiln-Mothers do to every child.

## Art direction
- **Massing:** tall, upright and square-shouldered, built from flat slabs and never rounded. They carry kiln-door slab shields and long kiln tools held upright. From the strategy camera a block of 40 reads as a tiled roof, a turquoise-and-terracotta mosaic, with square brick Kiln Walkers smoking above.
- **Palette:** terracotta skin (mid), glossy turquoise shingle-tile armour (accent), cream slip (light), kiln-black (dark), dot and chevron family glazes, and copper staples. Orange glows only at kiln mouths and in the Firepots.
- **Banner:** terracotta with a turquoise border, bearing a cream stepped kiln-arch full of fire under a turquoise roof-chevron. It replaces R3's bottle kiln, because a bottle is a vessel.
- **Fortress and capital:** forts are square brick kilns behind glazed walls, their smoke seen across the map. The capital, **the Great Kiln**, is a stepped brick mountain roofed in turquoise tile, with an arched mouth that always glows.
- **Muster:** a column marches out of the glowing mouth into a grid on the tiled floor.

## Lookalike guards
- **Elden Ring's warrior jar** is answered by the fiction. The Kilnborn *were* jars and broke out, so the jar shape is what their culture forbids. No model has a lid, neck, handle, belly or hollow, and the only jars in the army are thrown.
- **The Terracotta Army:** never grey, realistic or identical.
- **Gorons and golems:** people with families and faces, not rock or constructs.
- **Kintsugi:** copper staples, not gold seams.
- **Dwarves:** no beards, runes, axes or mountain halls.
