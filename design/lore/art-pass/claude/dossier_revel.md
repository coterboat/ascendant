# The Revel

## Pitch
A travelling festival nation of people born without faces, who become whoever their mask says, and who want the Curtain: a sun that sets every night on a finished day and rises every morning on a new play. Each turn they choose in secret one mask for all their armies, so every battle against them is a read.

## Origin: why they refused to be finished
Halfmade finished some things in detail and only roughed in others. The Revel's ancestors were roughed in: crowd figures at the back of the world, with smooth blank faces. Nobody could tell what they felt, and they say they couldn't tell either.

Then one of them carved a face into a slab of bark, held it up, and for the first time somebody laughed at her joke. **A face is a part you play.** In a baker's mask you can bake; in a mourner's you can finally cry. Theatre became their law: your mask is your job and your name for the day.

**Why one face is forbidden.** The first Host carved a perfect face and kept it. It grew into her, and she became one thing forever: magnificent at first, then cruel, because she could no longer be anything else. Since **the Refusal**, no one wears one face for more than a day, and the festival never stops, because a festival is where everyone changes roles.

**The dark under the bunting.** Guests who stay three days are given a role, and they do not always get to leave the play. Free Holds along the Revel's roads slowly join the cast.

**Dissent:** the **True Face** party is tired of pretending and wants one face, kept and grown.

## Stance on the Wright
Something else entirely: **the world was not made but staged,** performed into being by everyone at once, and it stalled because nobody wrote the last act. "The Wright" is a role, a mask anyone could wear. The Revel keep one, **the Unworn Face**, a half-carved mask nobody has dared put on, which they say is the Wright's own. Whether a maker carved it or a Revel grandmother did is the one question they won't play with.

## The ending: the Curtain
The sun sets every evening on a finished day and rises every morning on a new one, so no ending is permanent and anyone can be recast at dawn. It is the exact opposite of the Kilnborn's Last Firing.

## How it plays: Masks
**Masks:** each turn, choose in secret one mask for all your armies:
- **Brass** gives Shield Wall.
- **Grin** gives Berserk.
- **Hare** gives Skirmish.

Your units with the **Masked** trait have that trait until the turn ends, and the turn report reveals the choice.

R2's per-army masks were a subsystem. This is one faction-wide order a turn, hidden only the way every simultaneous order is. The rules never are hidden: both forecasts show three odds lines, one per mask.

**Your turn: a pure read.** The Crown's Spearmen want Shield Wall to blunt you, so wear the Grin. Crossbows sheltering behind them call for the Hare, to reach the second rank. Expecting a charge? Brass. The opponent knows what you might do and must guess what you will: "You marched on Warmdoor. The Revel wore Hare."

## Rivals
- **The Kilnborn:** a face you fire against a face you change daily. Each is what the other fears.
- **The Candlewake:** an ending every night against one ending forever. The Revel's audiences are the Candlewake's food.

## Rulers
- **The Host** (mortal line, front rank): the mask is the ruler, and whoever picks it up after a death is the next Host. **Spotlight:** in battles in her province, enemy fighters have −1/1/2/2 defense.
- **The Unworn Face** (god, second rank): the great half-carved mask floating on ribbons over the Grand Stage. **Borrowed Glory:** your Masked units have +1/1/2/2 attack in battles in its province.

**Transformations** (the cherry-pink survives both):
- **True Face:** choose one mask for good. Masked units keep that trait and have +3 hp, and the secret choice ends. The dissenters win. Closes Drilled Ranks.
- **The Endless Revel:** masks are chosen per army, the deeper bluff, and Revellers cost 2 less. Closes Sworn Vassals: nobody swears fealty to a play that never ends.

## Art direction
- **Massing:** tall and spindly, with stilts, ribbon poles and the Puppet's head bobbing over the crowd. From the strategy camera it is a comb of poles and stripes, a parade and never a block.
- **Palette:** cherry-pink and leaf-green tent stripes (mid-tones), white lacquer masks (light), black gloves and eyeholes (dark), gilt trim, papier-mâché and silk.
- **Banner:** broad pink-and-green stripes with a black border and a scalloped valance hem, bearing a blank white mask framed by a gilt stage curtain.
- **Fortress and capital:** a fort is a **pavilion**, a painted wooden castle front propped on scaffolding among striped tents, a fortress from the front and a stage set from behind. The capital, **the Grand Stage**, is an amphitheatre under the hanging Unworn Face.
- **Muster:** the army costumes in public at racks of masks, then parades out behind the Puppet with the ribbons going up.

## Lookalike guards (why it was parked)
- **Majora's Mask:** no sentient evil mask and no moon. The Unworn Face is never worn and never speaks.
- **40k Harlequins:** R2's diamonds are gone; broad tent stripes replace them.
- **Slaanesh:** no excess, no pleasure cult and no purple. The festival is work and rehearsal.
- **The Faceless Men:** masks are carved, never taken. R2's masks pressed from wax faces are cut.
- **No-Face:** no white masks on black shadow bodies.
- **Codex's espionage faction:** the Revel bluff in battle only, never in treaties or intelligence.
