# The Stormherd

## Pitch
Gruff, funny nomads in yellow oilskins who herd living weather across the high fells, and who want the Washing: one rain to break every settled thing back into weather and start the morning over. They hit hardest when they come to you: their regular units strike harder in any battle where they are not the defender.

## Origin: why they refused to settle
On Halfmade's high fells, weather is still being made, and new clouds bud off the tops like lambs. The Stormherd's grandmothers farmed the valleys and lost every harvest to storms nobody tended. The first **Bellmother** climbed up with her father's sheep-bell and found that a newborn storm follows a bell the way a lamb does. Her people learned to drive the young storms where rain was wanted and away from where it wasn't.

The valley lords, the Aurel Crown's ancestors, offered them land and walls to keep the storms penned. They refused, and the refusal became their creed: **everything settled is weather that gave up.** A hill is a cloud that sat down, a crown is a man who stopped moving, and the Held Sun is the same sickness grown to the size of the sky. So they build nothing but folds, stay nowhere more than a season, and count their heirs in the old sheep-count: Yan, Tan, Tethera.

**Dissent:** the river herds believe weather wants to come down and rest, which is the Floodborn fork.

## Stance on the Wright
**There wasn't one.** "If somebody made the world they'd have fixed the fences." The world is weather that settled, and the Making is the warmth weather leaves in things. Old Bellmothers add that a maker would be a waste of a good rain.

## The ending: the Washing
One great rain breaks every settled thing back into weather, roads, kilns, laws and crowns included, and the sun moves on to a new morning. It is a joke about a bath until you live in a town.

## How it plays: Breaking Weather
**Breaking Weather:** Stormherd regular units have +2 attack in any battle where they are not the defender. This is my seat's R3 version, built on Codex's trigger and Grok's +2. It works against Free Holds, which always defend, so the early game is live, and no enemy can switch it off by staying home. Floodborn flips it.

**Your turn.** You want to be the one arriving: attack holds, catch armies on the road (meetings and border battles have no defender), and never sit still. The Crown's two-province road marches are your favourite prey, and the Rainwife takes forts off the table. In a three-sided hold battle you are never the defender, so it is the herd's favourite fight.

## Rivals
- **The Kilnborn: the Kiln against the Storm.** They want to have arrived last turn; you want both armies to arrive now. They would bake the world, and you would wash it. "Both armies arrived this turn: the weather broke, and the kiln was not set."
- **The Aurel Crown:** roads, walls and noon nailed up, the most settled thing in the world.

## Rulers
- **Grandsire Thunder** (god, front rank): a thunderhead the size of a hill, with old curled horns and a beard of rain. **Cloudburst:** in battles in its province, enemy fighters have −1/1/2/2 attack.
- **The Bellmother** (mortal line, front rank): the chief shepherd with the great brass bell. **Shearing:** whenever your side wins a battle in her province, you gain 3/4/5/6 gold. It shears the beaten, not the neighbours.

R3's flags are answered by renames. Thundersheep became Stormwethers (a wether is a herd ram, and the pun is the point). The Thunder Ram became a chained storm, away from Thor's goats. The Weatherwife became the Rainwife, away from Weatherwax. Washout now touches forts only.

**Transformations** (the oilskin yellow survives both):
- **Thunderborn:** the herds go up. Stormwethers become **Skywethers** with Skirmish, armies move two provinces a turn anywhere, and regulars have −2 hp. Closes Veteran Levies.
- **Floodborn:** the river herds win and go down into the rivers. Breaking Weather pays when you defend instead, and armies that did not move heal 3 hp a turn. Closes Sworn Vassals.

## Art direction
- **Massing:** a loose drifting cloud-shape dotted with yellow oilskins, never a line. Cyan lightning reads at any distance, and the Thunderhead floats above on chains like a captive balloon. From the strategy camera a herd is weather crossing the map.
- **Palette:** oilskin yellow on the people (light), slate-grey cloud on the beasts (mid), black faces and iron chain (dark), cyan lightning (accent), plus brass bells and grey drystone.
- **Banner:** yellow with a slate border and a lightning-cut hem. The emblem is a storm cloud whose curls are a ram's horns, with a cyan fork beneath: a cloud first and a ram second, which keeps it off goat and Chaos heraldry.
- **Fortress and capital:** forts are drystone folds, concentric rings on a fell. The capital, **the High Fold**, sits under one enormous storm cloud chained down, a flickering beacon.
- **Muster:** the herd circles inside the rings. The bell rings, the gates open, and it floods downhill like weather breaking.

## Lookalike guards
- **Mareep and Wooloo:** the beasts are never fluffy, round or yellow. They are pony-sized thunderclouds that walk on legs of falling rain, and the yellow is on the people.
- **Thor's goats:** no goats, chariots or hammers.
- **Miyazaki weather spirits:** no cute spirits. These are hill farmers with dirty jokes and a flood for a creed.
- **FF9's Black Mages:** faces are always visible under the hat brims. One lineup with shadowed faces was rejected for this.
