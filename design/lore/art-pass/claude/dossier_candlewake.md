# The Candlewake

## Pitch
Death was never finished in Halfmade, so the dead stay on as a shambling host of candle wax, kept lit only by the Making they draw out of the living. A Candlewake army that keeps fighting burns hotter and hits harder; one that stops starts to gutter.

## Origin: why they chose the candle
When a body fails in Halfmade, its Making has nowhere to go: there is a door, and behind it a room nobody built. For an age the dead lay in the fields, awake.

Then a living candlemaker, the first **Chandler**, poured her stock into the shape of her drowned son and set a wick in his crown, and he stood. But a flame burns, and the only loose Making in the world is in the living. A touch, a wound or a battle sends warmth up the wick.

The dead chose hunger over lying awake in the dark. They march because a candle can do what a corpse can't: finish the room, so that everyone, themselves included, can stop. "We are sorry. We are cold."

They disagree among themselves:
- **The Kept** have burned so long they fear the end.
- **The Snuffed** refused the candle and lie in the fields as a reproach.

## Stance on the Wright
They claim proof, since every one of them has stood at the unbuilt door. **The Wright did not walk out. She died at noon, mid-stroke,** the first death in a world with no death built, so she never finished dying either. Half-Death, they say, is what is left of her. Others call it grief dressed as theology.

## The ending: the Going-Out
The Held Sun sets once, death works at last, and every wick in the world goes out like candles at bedtime.

## How it plays: Wick (Wick and Afterglow as one rule)
**Wick:** each Candlewake army burns at a flame of 0 to 3, shown on its banner.
- Winning a battle raises it by 1.
- Ending a turn without a battle, outside a fort you own, lowers it by 1.
- Regular units get +1 attack per flame.
- At 0 the army gutters: its regular units lose 2 hp at the end of each turn until it fights again.

What it keeps and adds:
- **From Wick:** the visible clock.
- **From Afterglow:** the fought-or-home test, the fort as harbour, and no whole-army deletion.
- **New:** momentum, Alex's Khorne and Lu Bu feel.

The red team's open cases, closed:
- The flame changes in the events step, after battles.
- Recruits start at 1.
- A split keeps the flame; a merge takes the lower ("an army burns as hot as its coldest candle").
- Rulers and commanders never gutter.

**Your turn.** Every field army is a fuse and a furnace: "Second Procession: flame 3, +3 attack. If it doesn't fight this turn, flame 2." You chain battles, against Free Holds early (a hold always fights) and rival armies late. Enemies win by refusing battle, so the turns become a chase. The choice is always the same: go home and hold the flame, or push on and grow it.

## Rivals
- **The hospitality faction** (Codex's Concord): its Long Supper keeps the golden hour forever, and death unfinished. Both hunt the same holds.
- **The Aurel Crown:** noon nailed up never ends, and Sun magic heals the warmth the Candlewake drink.
- **The Thornmane Clans** are uneasy kin. Both want the sun down, so they are allies until sunset.

## Rulers
- **Half-Death** (god, front rank): half its face is alabaster, half a glowing lilac outline. **Harvest:** after it wins a battle in its province, 1/2/3/4 Wickwardens join your army. It never raises enemy units.
- **The Chandler** (mortal line, second rank): the one living woman at the head of the dead. **Vigil:** at the end of each turn, your armies in her province rise to at least flame 1/1/2/2. She feeds them her own warmth, and no Chandler grows old.

**Transformations** (the lilac survives both):
- **Golden Wake:** the Kept win. They feed on hives and meadows instead of people, and the wax turns honey-gold. Flame never falls in land you own. Closes Muster Grounds.
- **Red Wake:** they stop apologising, and the wax runs cherry-red. The flame runs to 5, but an idle army loses 2 a turn. It replaces Black Wick, which deleted the rule. Closes Iron Discipline.

## Art direction
- **Massing:** a slumped, uneven crowd with heads sunk into shoulders, never a rectangle. From the strategy camera a block of 40 is an ivory blot glittering with lilac points. **Flame height on the models is the momentum,** from smoke at 0 to a tall flame at 3.
- **Palette:** ivory tallow (light), soot-black wicks and armour scraps (dark), lilac flame (accent), and brass snuffers. The living's warmth burns gold; in a Candlewake wick it burns lilac, the colour of something being used up.
- **Banner:** lilac with a soot border and a hem cut into wax drips, bearing an ivory candle ringed by flames.
- **Fortress and capital:** forts are stepped towers of melted candles. The capital, **the Long Vigil**, is a wax citadel around one candle as tall as a keep.
- **Muster:** the dead shuffle out of the Vigil's mouth in no order and thicken into a tide as they move.

## Lookalike guards
- **Dark Souls:** lilac flame, not orange, and a finished death rather than a preserved fire.
- **Vampires:** they take warmth, not blood, and want to end.
- **Litwick:** never candlesticks with faces. A candle-headed lineup was rejected.
- **Ghost Rider:** wax faces, never flaming skulls. The lineup was re-rolled twice for this.
- **Dunharrow's oathbound dead:** they owe no oath.
- **Hela:** no antlers, and no green and black.
