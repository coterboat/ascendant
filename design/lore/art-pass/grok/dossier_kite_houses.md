# The Kite Houses

## Pitch

The Kite Houses are rival roof-families who fight from inside reed-and-silk kite frames, vermilion ribbons streaming, and who want the Held Sun cut loose so the day can finish its flight. In hills their mounted fighters hit harder, so every order asks whether the battle will be high enough for the wings.

## Origin

Halfmade's edge-hills run out into glowing outline. Their wind is a line you can hold, not a herd. A house shares a mast and a silk, rivals in peace and one army in war. The Step-Off is the choice: walk off the roof-deck inside a frame, or stay and be Staked, mending silk and fighting on foot. A house needs both. They took the air because the Making left the hill unfinished.

## The Wright

The Wright, they say, is still above, holding the sun like a kite and forgetting the string. House Sann says the holder is tired and kind. House Mire says there is only a snag, and "Wright" is a face on a knot. Both climb to cut the string, not to meet the hand. The Crown's noon would tie it harder.

## The ending

The Cut String lets the sun finish its arc because nobody holds the line. Wet silk is why they dread the Stormherd's rain.

## How it plays

**Higher Air.** In hills, mounted fighters have +2 attack, attacking, defending, or meeting. Forest and plains pay nothing. A hill fort still gives its defender +2 defense. Kites do not cross extra provinces and do not ignore forts. The frame is a mount: wider than tall, reed legs on the ground. You hunt hills, and park the Staked on a flat purse. Attack a Clan-held hill and both rules can pay. Arrive together and only Higher Air pays. A three-sided hill hold still pays it. One fork lets Rake work every round. The other flips the bonus into defense.

## Rivals

The Stormherd want any meeting. A hill meeting pays you both. A plains meeting pays only them. The Thornmane share the high ground. The Pans will not climb, and the war between you is the slope. Crown roads can slide knights along their own hill roads. You want them there on a turn the fort is absent.

## Rulers and roster

**Ribbon-Captain Oku** of House Sann, mortal line, front, inside a kite. **Signal Mast:** recruit 1/2/3/4 Reed Lancers where Oku stands. Heir in two turns at half power. Kept power is how that moving barracks lives.

**The High Sail**, god, front, a riderless siege-tower kite with a calm face in the silk. **Vertigo:** enemies have −1 morale, or −2 at kept power of 6 or more. Returns next turn at −3. The god stands in front, where Skirmish is less of a meal.

Sketches are hp/attack/defense/morale/cost.

- **Reed Lancer**, mounted, 9/12/5/7/15. **Rake:** on round one, it may strike the enemy second rank.
- **Threadcutter**, mounted, 8/11/4/6/11. **Stoop:** +2 damage on round-one hits.
- **Stake Guard**, infantry, 10/7/9/9/8. **Stake Line:** +2 defense on rounds 4 through 8.
- **House Pilot**, commander, mounted, 18/12/8/11/33. **Wind Mark:** mounted fighters have +1 attack while it stands.

## Art direction

From above, vermilion-tailed diamonds over a few narrow stake-guards. The wing is wider than the rider is tall, and reed poles are the legs. Light is straw-gold frame and an open face. Dark is near-black indigo silk. **Redmast** is reed masts and indigo roofs on a hill, ribbons at the mastheads. Muster is the roof-deck: kites step off, and the Staked follow down the path. The banner is indigo, a vermilion border, and a straw-gold diamond with long tails.

## Lookalikes

Silk and reed, with no sheep and no lightning, keep them off the Stormherd. Open faces and weapons keep them off air-monks. Reed poles and a cloth wing keep them off feathered beasts. Crown blue is royal cloth on steel. This indigo is near-black silk.

Keep the faces, the indigo silk, the vermilion ribbons, the stake soldier, and the plain background.

The reed frame's lower poles become two legs standing on the ground, and the rider's own legs are hidden. Leave the third figure as a wingless soldier on foot holding a planted stake. Turn the giant kite into a standing creature on reed feet, still with the calm face in the silk and the vermilion ribbons. Keep the indigo, straw-gold, and vermilion colours and the plain background.
