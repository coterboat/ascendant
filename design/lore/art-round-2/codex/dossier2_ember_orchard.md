# Ember Orchard: Second Dossier

## What changed

The Orchard’s body-horror has softened into warm civic continuity: ceramic seed chambers and consenting ancestor households remain, but the rulers now argue over stewardship versus cosmic regrowth. Its land mark is ash made fertile, not conquest made barren.

## Rulers and commander

**Grandmother Cinderpod** is the undying keeper of the first consent-ledger, anchored to Cindergrove’s ancestor furnace. She rules because only she remembers the promises made to the first planted dead; she wants every new household to remain voluntary and plural. Her game is defensive continuity: spend power on mandates, keep her behind the front, heal veterans with **Warm Soil**, and accept her four-turn re-forming risk. A late sacrifice buys national strength but leaves a fragile custodian.

**Rill of the First Garden** is a god grown from the oldest ash furrow. It believes the Wright died into creation and wants to germinate those scattered recollections. Its game is an aggressive ruler build: kept power makes a dangerous front-rank fighter and strengthens **Ripening**, reducing regular recruit costs in its province. Sacrifice power for mandates and Rill becomes an efficient mobile nursery but risks permanent loss of power whenever slain.

The **Ash Gardener** commands from behind the ranks, pruning damaged shells, separating quarrelling ancestor voices, and raising a hooked staff above the orange crowns. Its **Tending** seed remains: after a victory, surviving regulars heal slightly. It is compassionate logistics given a battlefield silhouette.

## Cindergrove

Cindergrove began as a circular cremation wall around the first speaking seedlings. Living citizens and ancestor households built it together, firing cream seed-pods in funeral kilns and setting charcoal stone while it was warm. The central muster ground is a grid of ash furrows: recruits awaken there, pull free, and form rows.

Tier one is an **Ash Ring**, low wall, split-pod gate, furnace and furrows. Tier two adds repeating **Pod Towers** and more beds. Tier three is **Cindergrove Crowned**, with a monumental open seed-furnace visible across the province. Each upgrade thickens the same five modular parts rather than changing its language.

## Mark on the land

The Orchard turns burial into cultivation. Newly held ground gains thin charcoal furrows and a few pale seed husks. After a while, orange-crowned hedges and magenta warmth appear in soil cracks. Heartland becomes dark fertile loam patterned in broad curves, with ceramic seed markers at field edges. When lost, the glow dies first; orange growth sheds, while dark soil and empty pods linger for years before another mark overtakes them.

Cheap board treatment: a charcoal ground tint/mask, sparse cream pod props, orange foliage swaps, and a few magenta emissive cracks at borders. Low quality removes particles and props, then reduces the tint. Rule seed: the first Orchard regular recruited in a heartland province may receive its stored Returned Seed discount; design owns all values.

Sharpest rivalry: **Candlewake**, because it harvests living essence where the Orchard permits only gifted continuity. Unlikeliest ally: **Glasswake Compact**, whose voluntary memory reservoirs understand that identity can be shared without being owned.
