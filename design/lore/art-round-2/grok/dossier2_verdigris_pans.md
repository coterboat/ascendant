# The Verdigris Pans, round 2

## What changed

Open Pan, the First Rake, the Wetfeet, and the Wright-stance are the same. This round names why the two rulers rule, gives Dryhold three build tiers, and says what a rake does to land they hold.

## The two rulers

**Warden Brack**, mortal line, front rank. He stood the First Rake until the salt became plates. He rules because a crust that can be raked again needs a person who still sweats. **Dry Tithe:** his province earns +2/3/4/5 gold. Heir in two turns at half power.

Pick Brack to ride the frontier. Keep some power so the front-rank warden survives, collect gold where he stands, spend the rest on mandates. Empty the pool and he dies in the first even fight. A mid-game sacrifice still costs +1.

**The Kept Crust**, undying, second rank. Bound to Dryhold's seal-stone: the crossing gone dry, raked until the mineral learned a face. It rules because someone has to stay with the stone. **Seal:** wounded units there heal 2/4/7/10 hit points a turn. Re-forms in four turns at full power.

Pick the Kept Crust to hold Dryhold. Park it on the seal-stone, heal the garrison, spend power on mandates: death is slow, not half-power. Keep power if you want Seal to save veterans. For four turns after it breaks, the capital has no healer.

At 96×128: Brack is a sunburnt man in cracked white crust with a verdigris yoke and a short mace. The Kept Crust is a white mineral face with a plain dark cube in both hands.

## The commander

The **Pan Marshal** raises a charcoal pole topped with a verdigris yoke. **Hold Fast:** fighters have +1 defense while it stands. Stacks with Open Pan and a Crustguard's Set. One per army, plus the usual +2 morale.

## The fortress and capital

**Dryhold** is a low circular crust-wall, one notch of a gate, and a dark seal-stone on a short plinth as the keep. There is no tower. The First Rake drew the circle; later seasons piled cracked plates into a wall a person can see across. Muster, **the Pan**, is the flat inside the ring: companies form circles and leave by the notch.

Tiers: (1) a raked circle and a seal-stone; (2) a low crust-wall of salt-block segments and one gate-notch, fort +2 defense; (3) concentric rake-lines on the Pan, a second low ring, the seal-stone raised on a plinth. Repeatable kit: crust-wall segment, gate-notch, seal-stone keep, raked disc floor. Height never goes above two soldiers.

## The mark on the land

They rake land they hold because brine ate their tracks, and a raked crust can be raked again. Newly taken: salt dust on the road, rake-lines in the nearest field. Held a while: fields become pans, the stream goes briny green, forest trunks go white. Heartland: a white disc scored with lines; the same cottages stand as crust-roofed discs; verdigris yokes as waymarkers.

When they lose it, the mark can be raked back. If the new owner works the ground, the crust fades and the brine stays in the stream. If the land is abandoned, the crust cracks and the pan floods. A Kilnborn firing on a former pan would bake the salt into a plate that cannot be raked; that is the quarrel.

Cheap 3D: a white ground tint, a cracked-salt overlay, rake-line decals, a crust-roof swap on cottages, scattered yoke waymarkers. Low quality keeps the tint only. Brine shimmer on water on high.

Rule seed, design owns the number: a plains province they hold earns +1 gold from the rake. Nothing on forest or hills. The forecast shows it.

## Rivalry and ally

**Sharpest rivalry:** the Kilnborn. Already There is morale on any defense; Open Pan is +2 defense on a plains they hold. On a pan the two can both be true. On a hill the hill is the kiln's. A Crown knight wants a meeting, where the crust is off.

**Unlikeliest ally:** the Lacquer Reed. The Reed sits in the wet wood; the Pans sit on the open crust. They do not bid for the same last click, and both refuse a world baked so it cannot be raked or retuned.
