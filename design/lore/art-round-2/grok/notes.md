# Notes for Alex

## Tool

Every image was made with Grok Imagine in Grok Build: `image_gen` for new pictures and `image_edit` to keep a round 4 scene or to correct a face. The rate-limit notice in round 4 named the model `grok-imagine-image-quality`. The tool returned JPEG. I converted each shipped file to PNG with `sips`, and cropped the portraits from 2:3 (832×1248) to 3:4 (832×1109) by taking the top of the frame so the face stays in the upper half. Wide images are 1280×720. No prompt asked for letters. The Kept Crust's first seal-stone came back with rune-like marks; I edited them off. An early Dryhold grew a tower; I flattened it. An early Chimewater sprawled into a city; I pulled it back onto one plot.

## What to know

The Reed people are still the pipe itself, face inside the bone-white cone. Early fortress troops drifted into cone-hats; the shipped fortress keeps them small, as a comb of bells in the Sounding Bowl.

Dryhold has no tower on purpose. The kit is wall-segment, gate-notch, seal-stone, raked floor. Height never goes above two soldiers.

The two ruler pairs are built to read apart at 96×128: teal face and three-hole reed against a violet-lit god with a fork; sunburnt warden with a mace against a white mineral keeper with a dark cube.

## Strongest faction this round

The Verdigris Pans. The dominion picture does the new job in one glance: the same road, stream, cottages and trees, with the rake crossing the hedge. Dryhold is a fortress kit you could model from four parts. Brack and the Kept Crust are different people with different games, and they look it. The Reed still owns the stranger body, and Yssa is the best single portrait of the set, but the Pans are the ones whose land, keep and faces all landed together.
